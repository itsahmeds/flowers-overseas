## 1. Problem

After a buyer pays, the product is a promise: a stranger in another country will make and deliver flowers. `plan/04` §9 and §13 say the confirmation and the tracking page are where that anxiety is replaced by a timeline, a florist's first name and, at the end, the photograph. Nothing after the pay button exists yet. Spec 010 will place an order, the order-state spec will move it through `order_event`, and the notifications spec will send emails that link to pages that are not built.

Two legal duties also land here. We are the seller of record (ADR-0004, `plan/06` §4 Model A), so every paid order needs our own invoice (`plan/06` §2.2, `plan/07` §4). Its content depends on the accountant's `supply_model` answer, which is still open. `plan/09` therefore says the templates are "data-driven from day one". GDPR rights also need a self-service path (`plan/07` §1.5): export, erasure, and a lawful address book. The schema already carries the flags (`customer.redacted`, `recipient.contact_redacted`, `retention_policy` — spec 002 §8), but no route uses them. This spec builds the post-purchase surface and the minimal account. It covers roadmap items `018` and `019` in one spec and has a two-week build budget.

## 2. Scope

**Confirmation and tracking**
- A **signed tracking link**: `/{locale}/track/{token}`, where the token is an HMAC-signed, unguessable credential. It carries no personal data, no order number and no date. An admin can rotate it (version bump), and it expires 90 days after delivery (§13 Q7).
- The **confirmation page** `/{locale}/checkout/confirmation/{token}`, as drawn in `docs/design/wireframes/confirmation-*.dc.html`. It shows the order number, a masked buyer email and a four-step "what happens next" list. Its summary total equals the charged amount. It states that cancellation is free until acceptance, gives a contact path, and has tiles for the tracking link, the calendar (`.ics`), the invoice state and sharing. It ends with a one-tap account nudge.
- The **tracking page** `/{locale}/track/{token}`, as drawn in `track-order-*.dc.html`. Its timeline is a projection of `order_event`. It shows the florist's first name and city only after acceptance, and a re-route line when a florist declines. It shows the delivery photo through a token-checked proxy, the substitution note, the cancelled or refunded state, and the contact path.
- The **lookup page** `/{locale}/track`. A buyer enters an order number and email, and we email the link. The response is the same whether or not the pair matched.
- No PII on any token page. The view models are strict allowlists (AC-4).

**Invoices**
- **Invoice issuing** when money is taken: one invoice per order, gap-free numbering per series, and an immutable snapshot.
- A **credit note** for each refund.
- **Data-driven profiles** keyed on `(supply_model, destination country)`. A profile sets the title, the issuer statement, the languages, which lines carry VAT, the series and an optional second VAT currency. Switching profile is a data change, never a code change (AC-18).
- **Issuer identity**: the Estonian OÜ from `src/config/company.ts`, plus per-country VAT registrations held as data.
- **The PDF**: VAT lines by rate. It is tagged for accessibility, uses embedded fonts, is stored privately and is hash-checked.
- **Download** is available to the signed-in owner, or through a purpose-scoped signed link in emails. It is never available through a tracking token.

**Customer account (minimal)**
- **Magic-link sign-in.** Auth.js v5 database sessions on spec 002's tables. The link and the verify step are our own, because the stock email provider puts the address in the URL and consumes the token on GET (§5.2).
- **Order history**: every order of the signed-in customer, including guest orders placed with the same verified email. Each order has a detail page with the recipient recap until the recipient sweep runs, and its invoice downloads.
- **Saved recipients** only after an explicit, unticked "save" action by a signed-in buyer, recorded in `consent_log`. Saved recipients are exempt from the 90-day sweep and get their own retention row.
- **Privacy requests.** Self-service export (JSON) and erasure for signed-in buyers. Guest buyers use a verified email link. Requests have a 30-day due date with an SLA alert. Erasure follows spec 002's pseudonymise-and-keep-the-invoice rule.

**Cross-cutting**
- `noindex` on every route here, with the `X-Robots-Tag` header, `no-store` and no sitemap entry. `robots.txt` gains the locale-prefixed disallows.
- Locales en, en-gb, de and pl. WCAG 2.1 AA. Rollout is behind the flags `account` and `invoice.issue`.

## 3. Non-goals

- **Self-service cancel, address or date edit, redelivery and complaints.** The pages print the free-cancellation sentence and route the buyer to the contact path. The actions belong to spec 020 (refunds and the admin order queue) and the order-state spec.
- **Sending T1–T13 and attaching the invoice to them.** That is the notifications spec. This spec exposes `invoices.getIssuedPdf(orderId)` and `tracking.linkFor(orderId, locale)` for it to call. This spec ships only the four account and privacy emails (§5.2).
- **Live polling** (`plan/05` row 66, `/api/track/{token}.json`). The page is `no-store`, and a reload is current.
- **The recipient loop** `/r/{code}` (spec 029), recipient-side DSAR self-service (spec 029, or the manual runbook until then), and the consented "deliveries like these" photo band on the confirmation page. That band is omitted, not shown as placeholders, until spec 027/029 supplies consented photos.
- **Marketing preferences and passkeys** (Phase 3, `plan/05` row 56). The admin UI for privacy requests and invoice re-issue is spec 020; Phase 1 has an alert, a runbook and SQL.
- **Accounting export** (`plan/11` §8, CSV or UBL to the accountant), spec 039. Wise payouts and self-billing are spec 030.
- **Reviews**: invited from the photo email (notifications spec, spec 022), never from these pages.

## 4. User stories

- As a **buyer** who has just paid, I see what happens next, when and in which town, with one link I can keep, so that I stop worrying whether a stranger got my order.
- As a **buyer**, I open the tracking link from any device, without an account or password, and see every real step with its time, my florist's first name once they accept, and the photo, so that I know the flowers arrived.
- As a **buyer who lost the link**, I type my order number and email and get the link by email. Someone guessing at that form learns nothing.
- As a **buyer**, I receive a proper invoice from a registered EU company with VAT shown by rate, in a language I read, and a credit note when I am refunded.
- As the **founder**, when the accountant answers the Model A or B question, I change data, not code. No invoice number is ever skipped or reused.
- As a **returning buyer**, I sign in with a link and see every order I placed with that email. I can save a recipient's address only when I choose to.
- As a **buyer**, I download everything you hold about me, or erase my account, without writing to anyone. You keep only what the law makes you keep, and you tell me so.
- As a **recipient**, my name, address and phone never appear on a page that the sender may forward. My details leave an address book only when I object, and leave anything else 90 days after delivery.
- As **Googlebot**, none of these URLs is in a sitemap. Every one I fetch says `noindex`, and `robots.txt` asks me not to fetch them.

## 5. Design

### 5.1 Data model changes (tables, columns, migrations + rollback)

There is one migration, `NNNN_post_purchase.sql`, with `NNNN_post_purchase.down.sql`. It takes the next free number when the task lands and depends on spec 002's `0005`–`0011` (TASK-018…023).

- **`order`** gains:
  - `number text UNIQUE NOT NULL DEFAULT ('FO-' || lpad(nextval('order_number_seq')::text, 6, '0'))`. The sequence starts at `100001`, so no buyer sees "order 1". Spec 010 writes nothing for this column.
  - `tracking_token_version smallint NOT NULL DEFAULT 1`.
  - `public_token` is restated as **16 random bytes from a CSPRNG**, base64url-encoded. It stays `UNIQUE`. Spec 010 generates it with `crypto.randomBytes(16)`, and a check constraint enforces a length of 22.
- **`fulfillment_partner`** gains `display_first_name text NULL`. This is the name the buyer sees, as `plan/04` §13 has it ("Anna"). When it is null the page says "your florist in {city}" and never invents a name.
- **`recipient`** gains:
  - `saved_at timestamptz NULL`;
  - `saved_consent_log_id uuid NULL FK consent_log`;
  - `last_used_at timestamptz NULL`;
  - `CHECK ((saved_at IS NULL) = (saved_consent_log_id IS NULL))`.
- **`tax_registration`** has the columns `(id, country_iso2, vat_id text NOT NULL, valid_from date NOT NULL, valid_to date NULL, UNIQUE (country_iso2, valid_from))`. Each row is one VAT registration of the OÜ: EE now, PL if Model A. It is data, so registering in a new country is a row and not a deploy.
- **`invoice_profile`** has the columns `(key PK, supply_model CHECK IN ('principal','agent'), destination_iso2, document_kind CHECK IN ('vat_invoice','receipt_on_behalf'), title_key, issuer_statement_key NULL, label_locales text[] NOT NULL, vat_bearing_line_kinds text[] NOT NULL, series_key FK invoice_series, credit_series_key FK invoice_series, vat_currency_code NULL, vat_rate_source CHECK IN ('nbp_previous_business_day') NULL, active bool, UNIQUE (supply_model, destination_iso2) WHERE active)`.
  - The seed has one active row, `principal/PL`: `vat_invoice`, labels `['pl','{buyer}']`, VAT-bearing `product`, `addon` and `surcharge`, VAT currency `PLN` from NBP (§13 Q5).
  - It also has an **inactive** `agent/PL` fixture row, used by AC-18.
- **`invoice_series`** has the columns `(key PK, prefix text NOT NULL, year smallint NOT NULL, next_number integer NOT NULL DEFAULT 1, UNIQUE (prefix, year))`. The default format is `FO-PL/2026/000001` for invoices and `FOK-PL/2026/000001` for credit notes (§13 Q4).
- **`invoice`** has the columns `(id, kind CHECK IN ('invoice','credit_note'), order_id FK, refund_id NULL FK refund, corrects_invoice_id NULL FK invoice, series_key, number text UNIQUE NOT NULL, issued_at timestamptz, supply_date date, currency_code, total_minor bigint, profile_key FK, snapshot jsonb NOT NULL, snapshot_version integer NOT NULL, pdf_object_key NULL, pdf_sha256 NULL, rendered_at NULL)`.
  - It also has `UNIQUE (order_id) WHERE kind = 'invoice'` and `UNIQUE (refund_id) WHERE kind = 'credit_note'`.
  - A `BEFORE UPDATE OR DELETE` trigger raises `INVOICE_IMMUTABLE`. The only exception is the one-time `NULL → value` write of the three render columns. The trigger follows the pattern of `order_event`'s trigger in spec 002 AC-12.
  - The money pair is `total_minor` plus `currency_code`, which spec 002's `db:check` already enforces.
- **`privacy_request`** has the columns `(id, kind CHECK IN ('export','erase'), customer_id NULL FK, email_hash bytea NOT NULL, channel CHECK IN ('account','email_link','admin'), status CHECK IN ('received','verified','in_progress','waiting_open_orders','completed','rejected'), received_at, verified_at NULL, due_at timestamptz NOT NULL, completed_at NULL, export_object_key NULL, export_expires_at NULL, note NULL)`.
  - `email_hash` is HMAC-SHA-256 under `SIGNED_LINK_SECRET`, so the table holds no address.
  - `due_at = received_at + 30 days` (GDPR Art. 12(3)).
- **`retention_policy`** gains three seed rows:
  - `saved_recipient`: delete 730 days after `last_used_at` (§13 Q6);
  - `privacy_export`: delete the object at `export_expires_at`;
  - `privacy_request`: keep for 3 years, as accountability evidence (Art. 5(2)).
- **`retention.sweep`** (spec 002 TASK-025) changes one predicate: the 90-day recipient rule skips rows with `saved_at IS NOT NULL`. `order.recipient_snapshot` is still pseudonymised at 90 days. History never needs the street, and the address book does not live in the snapshot.
- **Row-level security**:
  - `invoice`, `privacy_request` and saved `recipient` are customer-scoped through `customer.user_id = app.user_id` (spec 002 §5.5).
  - `tax_registration`, `invoice_profile` and `invoice_series` are admin and system only. Public reads return zero rows (AC-17 of spec 002 is extended).
- **Rollback**: the down file drops the five tables, the four columns and the sequence. It deletes the three retention rows and restores the old sweep predicate. Spec 002's round-trip test executes it.

### 5.2 API / server actions / jobs / events (zod schemas named)

The modules follow `plan/01` §5. Every module exports its public API from `index.ts` only.

- **`src/lib/signed-link.ts`**: `sign(purpose, payload)` and `verify(purpose, token)`.
  - `Purpose = z.enum(["track","invoice","privacy","export"])`.
  - The MAC is HMAC-SHA-256 under `SIGNED_LINK_SECRET`. The MAC input is prefixed with `purpose + ":v1:"` (domain separation), truncated to 16 bytes, and compared with `timingSafeEqual`.
  - An optional `expiresAt` goes inside the MAC.
  - A token is `base64url(payload) + "." + base64url(mac)`. There is one secret and a purpose per use, so a privacy token can never open a tracking page.
- **`modules/orders`** gains read-only projections. No transitions are added here.
  - `TrackingToken = z.object({ publicToken: z.string().length(22), version: z.number().int() })`.
  - `tracking.linkFor(orderId, locale)` and `tracking.resolve(token) → { ok, orderId } | { ok: false }`. The function has one failure shape for every cause.
  - `getTrackingView(orderId)` returns `TrackingView`, and `getConfirmationView(orderId)` returns `ConfirmationView`.
  - Both are `.strict()` zod schemas whose key sets are the allowlists of AC-4.
  - The timeline maps each `order_event.to_status` through `order_status.buyer_projection`. It takes the first occurrence per projection in `seq` order. A `routed` event after an `assignment.declined` or `assignment.timed_out` adds a `rerouted` line with the new partner's city.
  - `rotateTrackingToken(orderId, actor)` is admin-only. It bumps the version and writes `audit_log`.
- **`modules/invoices`** (new; `plan/01` §5 gains the row):
  - `issueInvoice(orderId)`: one transaction that takes `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on the series row, allocates the number, inserts `invoice` with its `snapshot`, and enqueues `invoice.render`.
  - `issueCreditNote(refundId)` follows the same pattern.
  - `renderPdf(invoiceId)` builds the PDF with PDFKit (`tagged: true`, `lang`, embedded Noto Sans subset, A4) from the snapshot and the profile. It never reads live order rows. It puts the object in `R2_PRIVATE_BUCKET` under `invoices/{yyyy}/{id}.pdf` and writes the key and the sha-256.
  - `getIssuedPdf(orderId)` is for the notifications spec.
  - `InvoiceSnapshotV1 = z.object({ issuer, buyer, supplyDate, issueDate, lines[], byRate[], totals, currency, vatCurrency?, vatRate?: { value, source, date }, profileKey, orderNumber })`.
  - Refusals are typed errors:
    - `INVOICE_ISSUER_INCOMPLETE`: `company.registered` is false, or there is no `tax_registration` row for the profile's country;
    - `INVOICE_SUPPLY_MODEL_UNSET`;
    - `INVOICE_NO_PROFILE`;
    - `INVOICE_FLAG_OFF`.
  - A refusal raises inside the transaction, so no number is consumed.
  - **VAT arithmetic.** The order's `vat_breakdown` is the source of truth. The invoice groups lines by `vat_rate_bp` and takes gross per rate from the order. VAT per rate is `round_half_even(gross × rate / (10 000 + rate))` on the group total, and net is gross minus VAT. The invoice asserts that the sum of gross per rate equals `order.buyer_total_minor` and that each per-rate amount equals `vat_breakdown`. On a mismatch it raises `INVOICE_TOTAL_MISMATCH` and alerts. It never prints a number the buyer was not charged.
  - The NBP adapter sits behind `VatRateProvider`. It reads table A (`api.nbp.pl/api/exchangerates/rates/a/{ccy}/{date}`) for the last business day before the tax point and stores the value, the source and the date on the snapshot. It is called only when the profile sets `vat_currency_code`.
- **Jobs** (pg-boss, one zod payload each, idempotent on their key):
  - `invoice.issue {orderId}` runs on the money-taken event. That event is `order.captured` in `plan/11` §2's catalogue; immediate-charge methods emit the same event or the order-state spec's named equivalent, and both are subscribed.
  - `invoice.credit {refundId}` runs on `order.refunded`.
  - `invoice.render {invoiceId}` retries 5 times, then alerts.
  - `privacy.export {requestId}`, `privacy.erase {requestId}`, and `privacy.sla_check {}` (daily).
- **Authentication (`modules/customers/auth`)**: Auth.js v5 with the Drizzle adapter and the `database` session strategy. Session reads go through Auth.js. The cookie is `fo_session`: HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax, 30-day rolling.
  - **Sign-in and verify are ours**, because the stock email provider builds `…/callback/email?token=…&email=…` (an address in a URL breaks `CLAUDE.md`'s "no PII in URLs") and consumes the token on GET (mail scanners pre-fetch links).
  - `requestMagicLink({ email, locale, next })` stores `verification_tokens(identifier = email_normalised, token = sha256(raw))` with a 15-minute expiry. It emails `/{locale}/account/verify/{raw}` and returns the same result in every case.
  - `GET verify` renders a "Sign in as a•••@domain" button and consumes nothing. `POST verify` consumes the token (single use), upserts `users`, and links or creates `customer` by `email_normalised` where `redacted = false`. It inserts `sessions` and sets the cookie. It redirects to `next` only when `next` is a same-origin path.
  - `signOut()` deletes the `sessions` row.
  - `SignInRequest = z.object({ email: z.string().email().max(254), next: SafePath.optional() })`.
- **Server actions** (Zod at the boundary; each one re-checks the session server-side):
  - `saveRecipient({ recipientId })` and `removeRecipient({ recipientId })`;
  - `requestExport()` and `requestErasure({ confirm: z.literal(true) })`. Erasure requires a session verified less than 15 minutes ago; otherwise it re-sends a magic link with `next` set to the erase page;
  - `lookupTracking({ orderNumber: z.string().regex(/^FO-\d{6,}$/), email })`;
  - `requestGuestPrivacy({ email })`, `sendAccountLink({ token })` for the confirmation nudge, which sends to the order's email so nothing is typed.
- **Rate limits.** `RateLimiter` is an interface with an in-memory adapter, which is enough for the single Railway replica (ADR-0018). A Postgres adapter can be swapped in when there is a second replica. The limits are:
  - sign-in: 5 per hour per email hash and 20 per hour per IP;
  - lookup: 3 per hour per email hash and 5 per hour per IP;
  - guest privacy request: 3 per day per email hash;
  - tracking GET: 60 per minute per IP.
  Over the limit, the response is HTTP 429 with the same localised copy.
- **Emails** (this spec's four, as React Email components on the notifications sender, every locale, plain-text part):
  - T14, the magic link;
  - T15, privacy verification;
  - T16, the tracking link for the lookup;
  - T17, the export is ready.
  None of them contains PII beyond the recipient's own address in the To: field.
- **Erasure semantics** (spec 002 §8 "DSAR"):
  - The `customer` row is pseudonymised (`name`, `phone_e164` and the `address` rows are cleared, `email_normalised` is replaced by `redacted+{id}@invalid`, `redacted = true`), and `marketing_consent` is set to false.
  - The `users`, `accounts` and `sessions` rows are deleted, and saved recipients are deleted.
  - Recipient contact and `recipient_snapshot` are pseudonymised for every order that is not open.
  - Delivery photos are deleted except where a dispute or chargeback is open (Art. 17(3)(e)).
  - Orders, payments, invoices and their PDFs are kept, flagged by `customer.redacted`, because accounting law requires it (`plan/07` §1.2).
  - For orders that are still open, the request moves to `waiting_open_orders` and finishes on their closing event.
  - `consent_log` is kept for 5 years.
- **Export**: `PrivacyExportV1 = z.object({ schema: z.literal("privacy-export/v1"), generatedAt, customer, orders[], savedRecipients[], consents[], invoices[] (number, date, total, currency), privacyRequests[] })`.
  - It omits `payment.risk_score` and fraud signals (§8, lawyer flag).
  - It is stored at `exports/{requestId}.json` in the private bucket. The link is signed with the `export` purpose and lasts 7 days, and the sweep deletes the object at expiry.

### 5.4 Rendering & caching (SSG/ISR/SSR, tags invalidated, per plan/01 §3)

Every route is SSR with `Cache-Control: private, no-store`. This is `plan/01` §3's rows for confirmation, tracking and account. The lookup page is also SSR, because it carries a form with CSRF. Cloudflare is told to bypass the cache for `/*/track/*`, `/*/checkout/*` and `/*/account/*`. These routes are `no-store`, so the CSP uses a nonce (ADR-0016). No tag is invalidated, since nothing here is cached. Photo and PDF responses are `private, max-age=300` and `private, no-store` respectively.

## 6. SEO considerations (mandatory)

- **Indexability.** Every route in §5.3 is `noindex,nofollow`, through both the `X-Robots-Tag` header and `<meta name="robots">`. None appears in any sitemap. None emits a canonical or `hreflang` alternates; a token page with alternates would advertise credentials in four locales. The lookup page is `indexable (utility)` in `plan/05` row 22 and on the tracking artboard, but noindex in this brief. The default is **noindex** (§13 Q2).
- **robots.txt.** `DISALLOWED_PATHS` (`src/modules/seo/robots.ts`) gains `/*/track/`, `/*/account/` and `/*/checkout/` (spec 010 may already have added the last). This closes the "known gap" its header records. The unprefixed forms stay. `plan/02` §7 requires a noindex header plus a disallow for these page types.
- **URL patterns.** The `track`, `account` and `checkout` segments are **not localised**: they have no search value, and keeping them unlocalised keeps the disallow rule to one line per segment. `plan/02` §4.1 lists `/account/*` with no locale prefix. This spec uses `/{locale}/account/*` (§13 Q3), because the route group already lives under `[locale]` and the account renders in four languages.
- **Internal links.** The two new footer links point from every indexable page to disallowed pages. This is harmless for link flow (two of roughly 30 footer links), and the links carry no `nofollow`.
- **Schema.** None.
- **Core Web Vitals.** Indexable budgets are unaffected. The header is unchanged, the footer gains two text links, and the island ships only on the routes here.
- **Thin content.** Not applicable, because nothing is indexable.

## 7. i18n considerations (mandatory)

- **Namespaces**: `confirmation`, `tracking`, `trackLookup`, `account`, `auth`, `recipients`, `privacy`, `invoice`, and `emails.account` (T14–T17). Every key exists in en, en-gb, de and pl (`i18n:check`). There are no literal strings. ICU plurals are used for Polish ("1 zamówienie / 2 zamówienia / 5 zamówień").
- **Formatting.** Dates, times and money go through spec 003's formatters. Tracking times render in the **destination's** IANA zone with `timeZoneName: "short"`; the cutoff copy has the same "recipient's time" rule. Amounts are formatted with the order's currency, never the display cookie. Order numbers are formatted only as `FO-…`.
- **Invoice language.** The profile's `label_locales` sets bilingual labels. `principal/PL` uses Polish plus the buyer's order locale (identical when the buyer is in `pl`). Descriptions use the product name (untranslated by design, `plan/10` §2.2) plus the localised tier descriptor (§13 Q5).
- **Addresses.** The recipient recap in the account uses `formatAddressBlock` (spec 003) in the destination's format, preserving diacritics.
- **Right-to-left.** Logical properties only. There is no RTL locale in scope.
- **Review.** de and pl strings go through the native-reviewer queue (`docs/runbooks/i18n-translations.md`) before the `account` flag is turned on for those locales. Pages are noindex, so there is no hreflang gating. A locale whose keys are unreviewed does not get the flag.

## 8. Compliance considerations (mandatory)

- **New data flows**, recorded in RoPA (`docs/compliance/ropa.md`) by the closing task:
  - invoices: buyer identity, address and amounts kept for 10 years, under Art. 6(1)(c) legal obligation (🧾 confirm the period);
  - account and session: Art. 6(1)(b), held until erasure;
  - saved recipients: Art. 6(1)(f) for the buyer's address book with the buyer's explicit save action, recipient notice through the Art. 14 card and the privacy policy, and 730 days unused → delete (⚖️, §13 Q6);
  - privacy requests: Art. 6(1)(c), kept for 3 years;
  - the NBP rate fetch: no personal data.
- **New processor**: none. Neon, R2 and Resend are already listed. The new private R2 bucket is recorded.
- **Recipient minimisation** (`plan/07` §1.3). Token pages show no recipient field at all: no name, street, phone or card message. AC-4 proves it by allowlist and by a fixture string scan. The account detail shows them to the authenticated buyer until the sweep runs.
- **Consumer information** (`plan/07` §2.1, §2.2). The confirmation repeats the perishable-goods and free-cancellation-until-acceptance sentence and the contact path. The "durable medium" duty is the notifications spec's T1. The invoice carries trader identity (`plan/07` §6): legal name, registry code, VAT IDs and address. This comes from `company.ts`, and issuing refuses until `registered = true` (AC-20), so no invoice is ever issued without the identity.
- **Price display.** The confirmation total and the invoice gross both equal `order.buyer_total_minor` (AC-10, AC-18). There is never a recomputed price.
- **Invoices** 🧾: the content follows `plan/07` §4's list. The open points are the accountant's (`plan/06` §4 items 1, 3, 4 and 7): issue timing, series, language and the PLN VAT equivalent (§13 Q4, Q5). The profile and snapshot design makes each answer a data change.
- **GDPR rights** (`plan/07` §1.5): export and erasure are self-service, the guest route verifies by email, and the 30-day SLA is alerted at 25 days. The export omits fraud scores. Whether Art. 15 requires them is a lawyer flag (⚖️, `plan/07` §11 item 2), and the default relies on the fraud-prevention restriction.
- **Cookies.** `fo_session` moves from "declared, not set" to set, as essential (authentication for a service the user explicitly requested). Its lifetime becomes 30 days rolling, and the register row in `docs/compliance/cookie-register.md` is updated. No new non-essential storage is added. GA4 still loads only after consent.
- **Analytics and logs.** GA4 `page_location` on token routes is rewritten to `/{locale}/track/:token` before send. Sentry `beforeSend` scrubs path tokens. The logger never receives a token, an email, a name, a phone or a street (AC-31). `view_tracking` carries only `order_age_hours` (`plan/04` §14).
- **Accessibility** (`plan/07` §8):
  - WCAG 2.1 AA;
  - the timeline is an `<ol>` with `aria-current="step"`;
  - the status headline is in an `aria-live="polite"` region on reload;
  - the photo alt text reads "Photo of your delivered flowers, taken by your florist on {date}";
  - forms link their errors through `aria-describedby`;
  - the erase confirmation is a native `<dialog>` with focus return;
  - the PDF is tagged (StructTreeRoot, `/Lang`, reading order), with an HTML equivalent of the invoice in the account order detail;
  - the accessibility statement gains the invoice line.
- **Security.** Tokens are 128-bit, purpose-separated and MAC-checked in constant time. Unknown, forged and expired tokens share one response, so there is no oracle. The lookup and sign-in responses are also identical. `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` is set on token routes. Every endpoint is rate-limited. Redirect targets are same-origin only. Erasure requires a recent session.

## 9. Acceptance criteria

**Tokens and token pages**
- **AC-1** `signed-link.verify(purpose, token)` accepts a token produced by `sign` with the same purpose, and returns one failure value for each of the following: any single-byte change in payload or MAC; a token signed for another purpose; a payload past its `expiresAt`. The MAC comparison calls `timingSafeEqual`.
- **AC-2** A tracking token decodes to exactly `{ publicToken, version }`. `publicToken` is 22 base64url characters (16 CSPRNG bytes). After `rotateTrackingToken`, the previous token fails and the new one resolves, and an `audit_log` row records the rotation.
- **AC-3** `/{locale}/track/{token}` for a valid token returns 200 with server-rendered HTML. It shows the order number, a headline for the current buyer projection, and one timeline line per buyer projection in `seq` order, dated in the destination's zone with the short zone name. Projections not yet reached render without a date. A `routed` event after a decline or timeout renders the re-route line with the new city.
- **AC-4** `TrackingView` and `ConfirmationView` are `.strict()` schemas.
  - Their key sets equal the allowlists:
    - Tracking: number, projection, timeline, deliveryDate, destinationCity, floristFirstName, floristCity, substitution, photo state, cancellation/refund state.
    - Confirmation: tracking's set plus lines, total, currency, maskedEmail, invoiceState and demo.
  - For a fixture order whose recipient name, phone, street, postcode, card message and buyer email are unique marker strings, the HTML of both pages, the `.ics` body and the not-working page contain none of the markers.
- **AC-5** No florist name or city renders before an `assignment.accepted` event. After it, `display_first_name` and the partner city render. When `display_first_name` is null, the page reads "your florist in {city}".
- **AC-6** When a `delivery_proof` exists, the page renders an `<img>` whose `src` is `/{locale}/track/{token}/photo`, with the localised alt text. That route returns the object bytes with the asset's MIME type and `Cache-Control: private, max-age=300`, whatever the value of `public_use_consent`. It returns 404 for any token failure. When retention has deleted the photo, the page shows the dated deletion sentence.
- **AC-7** An invalid token, a forged token, a rotated token and an expired token (`delivery_date` + 90 days, or cancellation + 90 days) each return the same HTTP status (404) and the same body for a given locale. The body offers the lookup form and contains no order data.
- **AC-8** `POST /{locale}/track` returns byte-identical HTML for a matching pair, a wrong email and an unknown number. Only the matching pair sends T16, to the order's email, with a valid tracking link. Past the limits of §5.2, the response is 429 with the same localised copy.
- **AC-9** The confirmation page shows the masked email (first character, `•••`, `@domain`), the four-step list with the current step marked, the free-cancellation sentence and the contact path.
  - The WhatsApp link is `https://wa.me/{digits}` with no `text` parameter.
  - The tiles show the invoice state that matches whether an `invoice` row exists.
  - For `order.source = 'demo'`, the demo banner renders and no tile is a link.
- **AC-10** The summary lines and the total on the confirmation page are formatted from `order_line` and `order.buyer_total_minor` in `order.buyer_currency_code`, and the "VAT and delivery included" line is present. A fixture whose display-currency cookie differs from the order currency still renders the order currency.
- **AC-11** `calendar.ics` returns `text/calendar` that parses as RFC 5545, with one all-day `VEVENT` dated `delivery_date`. Its summary contains the city and not the recipient's name.

**SEO and privacy headers**
- **AC-12** Every route in §5.3 responds with `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow` and `Cache-Control` containing `no-store` (photo: `private`). HTML routes also carry `<meta name="robots" content="noindex,nofollow">` and no `rel="canonical"` or `rel="alternate"` link. Token routes send `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`.
- **AC-13** `robots.txt` in the indexing environment contains `Disallow: /*/track/`, `Disallow: /*/account/` and `Disallow: /*/checkout/`. No URL from §5.3 appears in any sitemap child.
- **AC-14** A GA4 `page_view` fired on a token route carries `page_location` with the token replaced by `:token`. A Sentry event raised on a token route has the token removed from `request.url` and the breadcrumbs.

**Invoices**
- **AC-15** Delivering the money-taken event twice to `invoice.issue` creates exactly one `kind = 'invoice'` row. Twenty concurrent issuances in one series produce the numbers 1–20 with no gap and no duplicate.
- **AC-16** `UPDATE` of any `invoice` column raises `INVOICE_IMMUTABLE`, except the first `NULL → value` write of `pdf_object_key`, `pdf_sha256` and `rendered_at`. A second write of those columns raises, and `DELETE` raises.
- **AC-17** The extracted text of a `principal/PL` invoice PDF for a fixture order (one 8% bouquet and one 23% add-on, paid in GBP) contains all of the following:
  - the issuer legal name, registry code, EE and PL VAT IDs, and address;
  - the buyer name and country, the invoice number, the issue date and the supply date;
  - each line description;
  - per rate, the net, VAT and gross amounts;
  - the totals and currency.
  The gross total equals `order.buyer_total_minor`, and the per-rate amounts equal `order.vat_breakdown`.
- **AC-18** With `principal/PL` deactivated and the `agent/PL` fixture activated (a data change only), the same renderer prints the agent title and issuer statement and treats only the profile's `vat_bearing_line_kinds` as carrying VAT. No source file differs between the two runs.
- **AC-19** When the profile sets `vat_currency_code = 'PLN'` on a GBP invoice, the PDF prints the VAT per rate in PLN as well, at the rate stored on the snapshot with its source and date. For a tax point on a Monday, the NBP adapter fixture selects the previous Friday's table A rate.
- **AC-20** `issueInvoice` refuses with `INVOICE_ISSUER_INCOMPLETE`, `INVOICE_SUPPLY_MODEL_UNSET`, `INVOICE_NO_PROFILE`, `INVOICE_FLAG_OFF` or `INVOICE_TOTAL_MISMATCH` in the matching fixture case. In every refusal, `invoice_series.next_number` is unchanged and an alert is raised.
- **AC-21** `order.refunded` with a partial refund creates one credit note in the credit series. It references the original number and carries negative per-rate amounts summing to the refund amount. A full refund's credit note and the invoice net to zero per rate.
- **AC-22** The stored PDF has a `StructTreeRoot`, `/MarkInfo << /Marked true >>`, a `/Lang` and embedded fonts. Text extraction round-trips "Zażółć gęślą jaźń" and "Größe". The sha-256 of the served bytes equals `invoice.pdf_sha256`.
- **AC-23** `account/invoices/{number}.pdf` returns the PDF to the owning session and to a valid signed `invoice` link. It returns 404 to another customer's session, to no session, to a tracking token and to an expired invoice link.

**Account**
- **AC-24** `POST account/sign-in` returns identical HTML for a known and an unknown email. T14's link is `/{locale}/account/verify/{token}` and contains no `@` or email text. `GET` on that link does not consume the token. `POST` consumes it once and sets `fo_session` (HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Lax). A second `POST`, or one after 15 minutes, shows the "link used or expired" page. `next` values that are not same-origin paths (`//evil.example`, `https://…`, `/\evil`) are ignored. Signing out deletes the `sessions` row, so a replayed cookie no longer authenticates.
- **AC-25** On a first verified sign-in, a non-redacted guest `customer` with the same normalised email gets `user_id` set, and the account lists that customer's orders newest first with the fields of §5.3. Requesting another customer's `account/orders/{number}` returns 404 under the `customer` role (RLS, not only an application check). Unauthenticated access to any account route except sign-in, verify and privacy-request returns 303 to sign-in.
- **AC-26** The account order detail shows the recipient's name, address and card message while `contact_redacted = false`. After the sweep it shows the city and the dated removal sentence, with no other recipient field.
- **AC-27** Saving recipients:
  - A recipient appears in `account/recipients` only after `saveRecipient`. `saveRecipient` writes `saved_at` and one `consent_log` row (`subject_kind = 'customer'`, `choices.purpose = 'recipient_address_book'`, `policy_version`).
  - The save checkbox renders unticked.
  - `retention.sweep` at 91 days skips a saved recipient. It deletes a saved recipient whose `last_used_at` is 731 days old.
  - `removeRecipient` clears the recipient's contact fields immediately.

**Privacy**
- **AC-28** `requestExport` creates a `privacy_request(kind = 'export')`. The job writes a JSON object that validates against `PrivacyExportV1` and contains the customer, all of their orders, saved recipients, consents and invoice metadata, and no `risk_score`. The download link works for 7 days and returns 404 afterwards. The sweep deletes the object at expiry, and T17 is sent.
- **AC-29** `requestErasure` with a session older than 15 minutes sends a re-verify link and changes nothing. With a fresh session it applies §5.2's erasure semantics: afterwards no row outside `order`, `payment`, `invoice`, `consent_log` and `privacy_request` contains the customer's email, name or phone. With an open order, the status becomes `waiting_open_orders` and completes when that order closes. A new sign-in with the same email then shows an empty account.
- **AC-30** The guest form returns identical HTML for known and unknown emails. A known email receives T15 with a signed `privacy` link valid for 24 hours, which leads to the choice of export or erase. Each `privacy_request` has `due_at = received_at + 30 days`. `privacy.sla_check` alerts for every request that is not completed and is due within 5 days (fixture clock).

**Cross-cutting**
- **AC-31** Across the e2e flows of T-25, a logger spy records no token, email, name, phone, street or postcode.
- **AC-32** `i18n:check` passes with every new key present in en, en-gb, de and pl. axe reports zero violations on confirmation, tracking (accepted, photo and not-working states), lookup, sign-in, account, order detail, recipients and privacy in `en-gb` and `pl`. Sign-in, export and erase complete by keyboard alone.
- **AC-33** With the `account` flag off, account routes return 404, the footer omits the two links and the confirmation omits the nudge. With `invoice.issue` off for PL, AC-20's flag refusal applies, and the confirmation shows the "we will email it" state.

## 13. Open questions

Each question carries the default this spec is written against. The spec stays `draft` until the founder rules.

- **Q1 — How many hours after the delivery photo may a buyer say "not what I ordered"?** Both artboards print "[slot] N hours". **Default: 48 hours from our delivery photo.** The number is held as data (`company.ts` `complaintWindowHours`), so the pages and the guarantee page print the same value. The field runs from 24 hours (FloraQueen) to 48 hours (Aquarelle).
- **Q2 — Is the lookup page `/{locale}/track` indexable?** `plan/05` row 22 and the artboard say yes ("track my flower order"). The brief says noindex everywhere. **Default: noindex.** The page is a thin form, the query has little volume for a new brand, and keeping it noindex lets all of `/*/track/` stay disallowed with a single rule.
- **Q3 — Should the account be at `/{locale}/account` or at `/account` (`plan/02` §4.1)?** **Default: `/{locale}/account/*`.** The scaffolded route group is under `[locale]`, the pages render in four languages, and `CLAUDE.md` says "locale in URL". The closing task records a `plan/02` §4.1 correction.
- **Q4 — When is the invoice issued, and how are series numbered?** 🧾 **Default: at the money-taken event.** For cards that is capture on florist acceptance, which is also when the contract forms (`plan/07` §2.3). For immediate-charge methods it is payment. The default is one series per VAT-registration country per calendar year (`FO-PL/2026/000001`), with credit notes in their own series (`FOK-PL/…`). Polish advance-payment (zaliczka) rules may move the tax point to payment for every method. The accountant's answer changes data, not code.
- **Q5 — What language is the invoice in, and does it show the PLN VAT equivalent?** 🧾 **Default: Polish labels plus the buyer's language on one document, the charged currency as the invoice currency, and VAT per rate also shown in PLN at the NBP table A rate of the business day before the tax point.** The alternative, buyer language only and no PLN line, is a single profile edit.
- **Q6 — On what lawful basis do we keep saved recipients, and for how long?** ⚖️ **Default: the buyer's explicit, unticked save action, recorded in `consent_log`, under legitimate interest (the buyer's address book).** The recipient is informed by the Art. 14 card notice and the privacy policy, and can object, which deletes the entry. The entry is deleted after 24 months without an order to that recipient. The alternative is no address book in Phase 1, which is simpler and loses the reorder convenience the roadmap expects in Phase 3.
- **Q7 — How long does a tracking link work?** **Default: until `delivery_date` + 90 days, or cancellation + 90 days.** That lines up with the recipient sweep, after which the page has little to show. A signed-in buyer keeps the history forever.
- **Q8 — On erasure, do we keep delivery photos and payment records as evidence?** ⚖️ **Default: only while a dispute or chargeback on that order is open (Art. 17(3)(e)).** Everything else is deleted or pseudonymised at once. Orders, payments and invoices are kept because accounting law requires it. The alternative is to keep photos for the full 2-year chargeback reasoning, which is stronger evidence and a weaker erasure.
- **Q9 — The share tile: OS share sheet or a WhatsApp link?** The artboard says "Share on WhatsApp". A `wa.me/?text=` link sends the tracking credential through Meta's redirect URL. **Default: `navigator.share` (WhatsApp is one tap in the sheet) with a copy-link fallback.** The tile reads "Share the tracking link".
- **Q10 — Which spec numbers are the boundaries?** The brief names 013 (order states) and 016 (notifications). `plan/09` numbers them `015` (state machine, paired with `013` payments) and `017` (notifications), with `016` as routing. **Default: `plan/09`'s numbering.** This spec names the specs by role, so only the citations change.
