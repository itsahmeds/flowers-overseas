## 1. Problem

After a buyer pays, the product is a promise: a stranger in another country will make and deliver flowers. `plan/04` §9 and §13 say the tracking page is where that anxiety is replaced by a timeline, our florist's first name once the order is being made and, at the end, the photograph. Spec 010 places the order and shows a confirmation for as long as the checkout cookie lives (24 hours). Spec 002 §14 A6 now fixes every status the order can be in and what the buyer is told about each one (A6.4). Spec 016 will send emails that need a tracking link, and nothing yet answers that link.

Two legal duties also land here. We are the seller of record (ADR-0004, `plan/06` §4 Model A), so every paid order needs our own invoice, and every refund a credit note (`plan/06` §2.2, `plan/07` §4). The invoice's content depends on the accountant's `supply_model` answer, which is still open, so `plan/09` says the templates are "data-driven from day one". GDPR rights also need a self-service path (`plan/07` §1.5): export and erasure (the address book the first draft also proposed is out of Phase 1, §13 Q6). The schema already carries the flags (`customer.redacted`, `recipient.contact_redacted`, `retention_policy`, spec 002 §8), but no route uses them. The founder's ruling F4 on spec 002 A6 (2026-10-12) adds one more duty to this spec: once a refund has succeeded, the buyer sees "Refunded {amount} on {date}" next to the status, because A6.4 shows a refunded order as "closed", never "cancelled". This spec builds the post-purchase surface and the minimal account, covering roadmap items `018` and `019`.

**What ships when (founder, 2026-10-14, §13 Q14).** The **launch slice** is tasks 1 to 3 (data and seams, tracking and the confirmation block, invoices): it ships before the first paid order. The account and self-service privacy (tasks 4 and 5) follow launch. Until task 5 ships, a buyer's access or erasure request is handled by hand through `docs/runbooks/dsar.md`, which GDPR allows (Art. 12 sets the deadline, not the channel). A saved-recipient address book is not part of Phase 1 at all (§13 Q6).

**The buyer's voice (founder ruling, 2026-10-14, §14; binding, overrides the earlier approvals in this file).** Every order is charged at placement, so the buyer is never told a card is "held" and never waits for anything. And the buyer never learns that we relay an order to a partner florist: **we** send the flowers, **our florist** makes them (decisions-log 2026-10-04; spec 004 A22). Every page, email, PDF and copy key in this spec's scope is written to that rule and scanned for it (AC-38 to AC-43). Acceptance, offers, declines, timeouts, re-routing and "no florist available" are internal facts that the founder, admin and florists see; they never reach a buyer-facing view model.

## 2. Scope

**Tracking**
- A **signed tracking link** `/{locale}/track/{token}`. The token is an HMAC-signed credential over `{ publicToken, version }`. It carries no personal data, no order reference and no date. It can be rotated (version bump, audited) and stops working 90 days after the delivery date, or 90 days after the order is cancelled (§13 Q7).
- The **tracking page**, as drawn in `docs/design/wireframes/track-order-*.dc.html` (artboards amended, §5.3). Its headline is the current status's `order_status.buyer_projection` (spec 002 A6.4), folded to the buyer states **We have your order, Being made, On its way, Delivered**, plus Closed and Cancelled (§5.2 "The buyer projection"). Its timeline is a projection of `order_event` and contains only those steps. **No assignment, offer, acceptance, decline, timeout or re-route line exists** on it. It shows "our florist {first name}" and the city only once the order is being made (after `order.in_production`), the delivery photo through a token-checked proxy, the substitution note, the cancelled state, and the contact path.
- **Refund lines (A6.4, advisor F4, D5).** For every refund on the order whose `refund.status = 'succeeded'`, one line "Refunded {amount} on {date}" beside the status, in every state, partial and full alike. The same lines appear in the account. A partially refunded order keeps its fulfilment headline (D1); a fully refunded one reads "closed" (D5).
- A **calendar file** `/{locale}/track/{token}/calendar.ics`.
- The **lookup page** `/{locale}/track` (founder Q2: kept out of Google). A buyer enters the `FO-XXXX-XXXX` reference (spec 010) and the email, and we email the link. The response is the same whether or not the pair matched.
- No recipient field on any token page. The view models are strict allowlists (AC-4).

**The post-purchase block (rendered inside spec 013's `test` and `live` confirmation states on spec 010's page)**
- Spec 010 owns `/{locale}/checkout/done`, its cookie, its recap and its `demo` and "no longer available" states (010 AC-29). **Spec 013 alone owns the page's `test` and `live` states** and registers them through spec 010's `registerConfirmationState` seam (spec 010 §14 A2.4). **This spec owns the post-purchase block** that spec 013's states mount through `getConfirmationBlock`. So one spec owns each part: 010 the page and the demo state, 013 the states, 018 the block. The block holds: the masked buyer email, the four-step "what happens next" list (we have your order and your payment, being made, on its way, delivered), the contact path, tiles for the tracking link, the calendar file, the invoice state and sharing, and, last, **one quiet line**: "Plans changed? You can cancel for free until we start preparing your order.", whose words "You can cancel for free" are the cancel action (§5.2 "Buyer cancellation"; §14 A-founder-2026-10-14b). There is no cancel panel, no highlighted box and no separate button, and nothing in the block speaks of a refund. In the launch slice this line is where a buyer cancels for free. The one-tap account nudge joins it with task 4. The `demo` state is unchanged.

**Invoices**
- One **invoice** per live order, issued when money is taken, which is at placement (the first `order.captured`, A6.3 row 2; §5.2), with gap-free numbering per series and an immutable snapshot.
- One **credit note** per succeeded refund.
- **Data-driven profiles** keyed on `(supply_model, destination country)`: title, issuer statement, label languages, which line kinds carry VAT, series, and an optional second VAT currency. Switching profile is a data change (AC-18).
- **Issuer identity** from `src/config/company.ts`, plus the OÜ's VAT registrations held as data.
- **The PDF**: VAT by rate, tagged for accessibility, embedded fonts, stored privately, hash-checked.
- **Download** through a purpose-scoped signed link in emails (launch slice), and by the signed-in owner (task 4). Never through a tracking token.

**Customer account (minimal; after launch, tasks 4 and 5)**
- **Sign-in** as a third surface on spec 012's sign-in core (`createAuthSurface`), with spec 011's link design. No sign-in code of our own.
- **Order history**: every live order of the signed-in customer. The guest orders placed with the same email are the same `customer` row (spec 010's `upsertGuestCustomer` matches on the normalised email). Each order has a detail page with the recipient recap until the recipient sweep runs, the refund lines, and its invoice and credit-note downloads.
- **Free cancellation before the order is prepared** (A6 D4, row 13, and spec 002 §14 A10's rows 39 and 40; internally: while the status is in set P, which ends at `in_production`, 06:00 destination-local on the delivery date; the refund is full and goes through spec 013's refund path): the quiet cancel line on the live confirmation (launch slice) and the "Cancel for free" control on the account order detail (task 4), offered only while the status is in set P. Never on the tracking page, which is shared (§13 Q10).
- **Privacy requests** (task 5): self-service export (JSON) and erasure for signed-in buyers, a verified email link for guests, a due date one calendar month after receipt (GDPR Art. 12(3)) with an SLA alert, and spec 002's pseudonymise-and-keep-the-invoice rule. Before task 5, by hand (`docs/runbooks/dsar.md`).

**Cross-cutting**
- `noindex` on every route here, by header and meta; `no-store`; no sitemap entry; `robots.txt` gains `/*/track/` and `/*/account/` (spec 010 already adds `/*/checkout`).
- Locales en, en-gb, de and pl. WCAG 2.1 AA. Rollout behind the flags `account` and `invoice.issue`.

## 4. User stories

- As a **buyer** who has just paid, I see what happens next and get one link I can keep, so that I stop worrying whether a stranger got my order.
- As a **buyer**, I open the tracking link from any device, without an account, and see every real step (we have your order, being made, on its way, delivered) with its time, our florist's first name once the order is being made, and the photo, so that I know the flowers arrived.
- As a **buyer whose order we could not deliver**, I see a plain cancellation and my full refund, with no reason that names anyone, so that I know I lost nothing.
- As a **buyer who was refunded**, I see "Refunded €12.00 on 14 November" beside the status, so that "closed" does not read like "you lost your money".
- As a **buyer who lost the link**, I type my order reference and email and get the link by email. Someone guessing at that form learns nothing.
- As a **buyer who changed my mind before my order was being prepared**, I cancel for free from my account or the confirmation and get my full refund, without writing to anyone.
- As a **buyer**, I receive a proper invoice from a registered EU company with VAT shown by rate, in a language I read, and a credit note when I am refunded.
- As the **founder**, when the accountant answers the Model A or B question, I change data, not code. No invoice number is ever skipped or reused, and no demo or test order ever takes one.
- As a **returning buyer** (after launch), I sign in with a link and see every order I placed with that email.
- As a **buyer** (after launch), I download everything you hold about me, or erase my account, without writing to anyone. You keep only what the law makes you keep, and you tell me so. Until then I write to you and the founder does it by hand within the legal month.
- As a **recipient**, my name, address and phone never appear on a page that the sender may forward.
- As **Googlebot**, none of these URLs is in a sitemap, every one says `noindex`, and `robots.txt` asks me not to fetch them.

## 5. Design

### 5.1 Data model changes (tables, columns, migrations + rollback)

Two migrations, each with its own `.down.sql`, numbered when the tasks land. Both run after spec 002's chain through TASK-025 (including A6's seed in `0007` and the triggers in `0008`, PR 210, whose tables stay unchanged) and after spec 010's `checkout_draft`.
- **`NNNN_post_purchase.sql`** (task 1, launch slice): the `order` and `fulfillment_partner` columns, `tax_registration`, `invoice_series`, `invoice_profile`, `invoice`, the `invoice.issue` and `account` flag rows, the RLS for the invoice tables, and `sessions.created_at` unless spec 016's migration has already shipped it (spec 002 §14 A8, below).
- **`NNNN_privacy_requests.sql`** (task 5, after launch): `privacy_request`, its RLS, and the two retention rows.

- **`order`** (spec 002 §5.1, spec 010 §5.1 A) gains:
  - `tracking_token_version smallint NOT NULL DEFAULT 1`;
  - on `public_token`: `DEFAULT translate(rtrim(encode(gen_random_bytes(16), 'base64'), '='), '+/', '-_')` (16 CSPRNG bytes, base64url, pgcrypto), `NOT NULL`, and `CHECK (public_token ~ '^[A-Za-z0-9_-]{22}$')`. Existing rows are backfilled first. The default means spec 010's `place` writes nothing for it (a note for TASK-202's brief, §12);
  - **No reference function or index is created here.** Spec 010 §14 A2 (founder-approved, §13 Q13) ships `orderReference(orderId)` (exported from the orders public API), the `IMMUTABLE` SQL function `order_reference(uuid)` and `UNIQUE INDEX order_reference_idx ON "order" (order_reference(id))` in its `NNNN_order_engine_columns` migration, and `place` retries with a new id on a clash. This spec's lookup (AC-8) and the account route resolve a reference through that function and index, and the migration orders after spec 010's (§12). No column is added.
- **`fulfillment_partner`** gains `display_first_name text NULL`: the name the buyer sees ("Anna", `plan/04` §13), set with the florist's agreement. When null the page says "Made by our florist in {city}" and never invents a name. The column is read only once the order is being made (§5.2 "Florist card").
- **`recipient`** is not changed: there is no address book in Phase 1 (§13 Q6).
- **`tax_registration`** `(id, country_iso2, vat_id text NOT NULL, valid_from date NOT NULL, valid_to date NULL, UNIQUE (country_iso2, valid_from))`: one VAT registration of the OÜ per row (EE now; PL under Model A).
- **`invoice_series`** `(key PK, prefix text NOT NULL, year smallint NOT NULL, next_number integer NOT NULL DEFAULT 1, UNIQUE (prefix, year))`. Default format `FO-PL/2026/000001`, credit notes `FOK-PL/2026/000001` (§13 Q4).
- **`invoice_profile`** `(key PK, supply_model CHECK IN ('principal','agent'), destination_iso2, document_kind CHECK IN ('vat_invoice','receipt_on_behalf'), title_key, issuer_statement_key NULL, label_locales text[] NOT NULL, vat_bearing_line_kinds text[] NOT NULL, series_key FK invoice_series, credit_series_key FK invoice_series, vat_currency_code NULL FK currency, vat_rate_source CHECK IN ('nbp_previous_business_day') NULL, active bool NOT NULL, UNIQUE (supply_model, destination_iso2) WHERE active)`. Seed: one active `principal/PL` row (`vat_invoice`, labels `['pl','{buyer}']`, VAT-bearing `product`, `addon`, `surcharge`, VAT currency `PLN` from NBP, §13 Q5) and an **inactive** `agent/PL` fixture row (AC-18) with `issuer_statement_key = NULL` (§7 "Invoice wording").
- **`invoice`** `(id, kind CHECK IN ('invoice','credit_note'), order_id FK, refund_id NULL FK refund, corrects_invoice_id NULL FK invoice, series_key, number text UNIQUE NOT NULL, issued_at timestamptz NOT NULL, supply_date date NOT NULL, currency_code FK currency, total_minor bigint NOT NULL, profile_key FK, snapshot jsonb NOT NULL, snapshot_version integer NOT NULL, pdf_object_key NULL, pdf_sha256 NULL, rendered_at NULL)`, with `UNIQUE (order_id) WHERE kind = 'invoice'` and `UNIQUE (refund_id) WHERE kind = 'credit_note'`. A `BEFORE UPDATE OR DELETE` trigger raises `INVOICE_IMMUTABLE`, except the one-time `NULL → value` write of the three render columns (the pattern of spec 002's `order_event` trigger). The money pair passes spec 002's `db:check`.
- **`privacy_request`** (second migration, task 5) `(id, kind CHECK IN ('export','erase'), customer_id NULL FK, email_hash bytea NOT NULL, channel CHECK IN ('account','email_link','admin'), status CHECK IN ('received','verified','in_progress','waiting_open_orders','completed','rejected'), received_at, verified_at NULL, due_at timestamptz NOT NULL, completed_at NULL, export_object_key NULL, export_expires_at NULL, note NULL)`. `email_hash` is HMAC-SHA-256 under `SIGNED_LINK_SECRET`, so the table holds no address. `due_at = received_at + interval '1 month'` (GDPR Art. 12(3) "within one month"). Postgres month arithmetic clamps to the last day of the month, so a request received on 31 January is due on 28 February (29 in a leap year), never later; `+ 30 days` would land on 2 March.
- **`sessions.created_at`** `timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now()`, on spec 002's Auth.js `sessions` table. The standard adapter shape holds only `session_token`, `user_id` and a rolling `expires`, so nothing records when a session was signed in. It is **spec 002 §14 A8** (approved 2026-10-14, together with spec 016's two `no_partner_available` transition rows). A8 ships in the **earliest** of spec 016's and this spec's first migration; when both are in flight the orchestrator folds them into one migration, and the later spec's migration does not add the column again. This spec's task 1 migration adds it only if A8 has not yet shipped, with the one-line rollback `ALTER TABLE sessions DROP COLUMN created_at`. Our adapter never updates it; a rolling refresh moves only `expires`. It is read only by `requestErasure` (task 5); the column ships early because it has a default and costs nothing.
- **`retention_policy`** (second migration) gains `privacy_export` (delete the object at `export_expires_at`) and `privacy_request` (keep 3 years, Art. 5(2) accountability). `retention.sweep` is **not** changed by this spec: `order.recipient_snapshot` and the recipient contact are pseudonymised at 90 days as spec 002 already says.
- **`feature_flag`** rows `account` (global, default off) and `invoice.issue` (default off, `country` scope rows).
- **Row-level security**: `invoice` and (second migration) `privacy_request` rows are customer-scoped through `customer.user_id = app.user_id` (spec 002 §5.5). `tax_registration`, `invoice_profile` and `invoice_series` are admin and system only; `app.role = 'public'` reads zero rows (spec 002 AC-17 is extended to them).

### 5.2 API / server actions / jobs / events (zod schemas named)

Modules follow `plan/01` §5; each exports from `index.ts` only.

**The order machine, read only (spec 002 §14 A6).** This spec adds no status, no `order_transition` row, no catalogue event and no outbox target outside A6.6's names as extended by spec 002 §14 A7 (37). It reads `order_status.buyer_projection` and `is_terminal` from the database, never from a copy in code, and its tests take the machine from `tests/fixtures/order-machine.ts` (A6.8). Three sets are used below, all from A6:
- **E** (before acceptance; to the buyer "before we start preparing your order") = `authorised`, `paid_unrouted`, `routed`, `no_partner_available`, `manual_reroute` (A6.3). At launch every card and wallet payment is captured at placement (spec 013, A-founder-2026-10-14), so an order enters E as `paid_unrouted`; `authorised` is not reached by any launch path and stays in the set because A6's rows are unchanged.
- **P** (the buyer's free-cancellation window, spec 002 §14 A10.2; to the buyer "until we start preparing your order") = E plus `accepted` and `paid` (7 states), exactly the statuses whose folded projection is `confirmed`. Under the founder's "2 B" (§14 A-founder-2026-10-14b) an accepted order stays `paid` until 06:00 destination-local on its delivery date.
- **Settled** = `closed` plus the terminal statuses of A6.4 (`chargeback_won`, `chargeback_lost`, `payment_failed`, `abandoned`, `cancelled`, `cancelled_refunded`).

**`src/lib/signed-link.ts`**: `sign(purpose, payload, expiresAt?)` and `verify(purpose, token)`. `Purpose = z.enum(["track","invoice","privacy","export"])`. HMAC-SHA-256 under `SIGNED_LINK_SECRET`; the MAC input starts with `purpose + ":v1:"` (domain separation), is truncated to 16 bytes and compared with `timingSafeEqual`. `expiresAt` sits inside the MAC. A token is `base64url(payload) + "." + base64url(mac)`; one failure value for every cause. Distinct from spec 016's florist links and spec 011's sign-in tokens, which have their own secrets.

**`modules/orders`, read projections** (no new command):

- `getTrackingView(orderId) → TrackingView` (`.strict()`, the AC-4 allowlist).
  - **The buyer projection (founder ruling 2026-10-14; amended).** A function `buyerStep(projection)` folds the stored `order_status.buyer_projection` into the buyer's seven keys: `placed` (a headline only, "We are confirming your payment", shown while the order is `placed`), `confirmed` ("We have your order"), `being_made`, `on_its_way`, `delivered`, `closed`, `cancelled`. It folds `with_florist` into `confirmed`, so an `accepted` or `paid` order reads "We have your order" until production starts. Internal states (`routed`, `accepted`, `no_partner_available`, `manual_reroute`, and every offer, decline and timeout, which change no projection) therefore read "We have your order"; `in_production` and `awaiting_buyer` read "Being made". The fold is a safeguard: spec 002 §14 A9.1 (approved) moves `accepted` and `paid` to `confirmed` at the source, after which the fold is a no-op (`with_florist` stays an allowed `CHECK` value that no row uses). The status is still read from the database, never copied into code; only the fold is code. No copy key is named for `with_florist`.
  - **Headline**: `buyerStep` of the current status's `buyer_projection`.
  - **Timeline steps**: the five non-cancelled steps in order: `confirmed`, `being_made`, `on_its_way`, `delivered`, `closed` (`placed` is a headline, not a step). Each `order_event` with a `to_status` maps to that status's folded projection; events that keep the status (`assignment.offered`, `assignment.declined`, `assignment.timed_out`, `order.routed`, `order.manual_reroute`) map to nothing and appear nowhere. A step up to and including the current projection is *done* or *current*, dated at the first event that reached it (for "We have your order" that is the capture at placement, row 2). A step after the current projection is *future* and undated, even if an earlier event reached it (a `closed` order that is then disputed, row 27, shows `delivered` as current and `closed` as future).
  - **Cancelled**: when the current projection is `cancelled`, the timeline ends with a dated cancelled line after the last step reached. The view carries `cancelledBy: 'buyer' | 'us'`, from the actor of the `order.cancelled` event (`buyer` only for the buyer; admin, system and any other actor give `us`) and nothing else about the reason. `cancelledBy = 'buyer'` reads "You cancelled this order."; `'us'` reads "We could not deliver this order." and names no reason, no florist and no routing outcome, whether the cause was `no_partner_available`, an admin cancel or anything else. While a full refund of that order is `requested` or `pending`, the view carries `refundPending` (the amount and currency) and the page reads "We are refunding {amount} to your original payment method." That sentence is never shown as done. Once the refund succeeds, the dated refund line takes its place (below). A cancelled-projection order with no captured payment (`payment_failed`, `abandoned`) has neither.
  - **No re-route, offer or acceptance line, ever.** The first draft showed one for each `assignment.declined`, `assignment.timed_out` and `order.manual_reroute`. They are removed. No view-model field is derived from an `assignment.*` event or from `order.no_partner_available` or `order.manual_reroute`.

Never before production, and never worded "accepted", "taken" or "partner".

**Buyer cancellation (A6 row 13, D4; spec 002 §14 A10; amended by A-founder-2026-10-14b).** On the confirmation the promise is one quiet line at the foot of the block: "Plans changed? Both steps work without JavaScript. Nothing says "until a florist accepts", and the line has no panel, highlight or separate button. ## 6. SEO considerations (mandatory)

- **Indexability.** Every route in §5.3 is `noindex,nofollow`, by `X-Robots-Tag` and `<meta name="robots">`, registered as `SeoPageType`s with a constant `noindex` rule in `modules/seo/indexability.ts` (spec 010's pattern). That is the intended result. The footer links to the lookup from every page at launch and to the account once task 4 ships, and Google obeys a `noindex` only on a page it may crawl ([Google, block indexing](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/block-indexing)). So those two pages are crawlable, and each serves the `noindex` header and meta itself (AC-12). The account page answers a crawler with a 303 to `/{locale}/account/sign-in`, which is disallowed. The sign-in page then serves `noindex` too, should Google reach it through a link.
- **URL patterns.** `track` and `account` are **not localised**: no search value, one disallow line each, the same reasoning as spec 010 §13 Q10. `plan/02` §4.1 lists `/account/*` with no locale prefix; this spec uses `/{locale}/account/*` (§13 Q3), as the route diagram at the top of `plan/01` already draws it. No personal value in any URL: references and invoice ids only, tokens only on token routes.
- **Internal links.** The footer links (one at launch, two with task 4) point from every indexable page to noindex pages; harmless for link flow (two of about 30 footer links), no `nofollow`.
- **Schema.** None.
- **Core Web Vitals.** Indexable budgets are unaffected: header unchanged, two text links in the footer, the island only on these routes, and no public route reads the account cookie (AC-36).
- **Thin content.** Not applicable; nothing here is indexable.

**S.1 What the buyer sees.** The signed-out `/{locale}/account` (C.5, C.13) shows, in this order: "Continue with Google", "Continue with Apple", a divider "or", then C.13's email field and "Email me a link". **S.2 The providers.** Auth.js's Google and Apple OpenID Connect providers, configured only in `src/lib/auth/buyer.ts` (and `src/lib/auth/providers/`) through the core extension below.
- **Scopes, exactly:** `openid email profile` (Google) and `name email` (Apple). Authorization code flow with PKCE (`S256`), `state` and `nonce`. Apple uses `response_mode=form_post`, which Apple requires when name or email is requested.
- **What we read**, from the verified ID token only: `sub`, `email`, `email_verified`, and `hd` (Google) or `is_private_email` (Apple); the name from Google's `name` claim, or from Apple's `user` form field, which Apple sends on the first authorisation only. The picture is never read; `users.image` stays null.
- **What we store:** the `accounts` row holds `userId`, `type`, `provider`, `providerAccountId` (`sub`) and `linked_at`; every token column (`access_token`, `refresh_token`, `id_token`, `expires_at`, `token_type`, `scope`, `session_state`) is null. The core's adapter `linkAccount` writes only those columns. We call no Google or Apple API after sign-in, so we keep no token to call one.
- **No provider script, ever.** No Google Identity Services, One Tap or Apple JS SDK on any page. Before the click nothing on our pages contacts Google or Apple; after it, one top-level redirect. CSP: `form-action` on the `no-store` responses that render a provider button (`/{locale}/account` and the checkout's step 3, spec 010 §14 A-founder-2026-10-25a) becomes `'self' https://accounts.google.com https://appleid.apple.com`, because Chrome applies `form-action` to the redirect that follows a form submission; no other route's policy changes.
- **Callbacks:** `GET /api/auth/buyer/callback/google`, `POST /api/auth/buyer/callback/apple`. Both answer with `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer` and a redirect to a URL without the provider's query, so the `code` never reaches a page, a referrer or analytics. No Origin check of ours applies to these two routes (Apple's POST comes from `appleid.apple.com`); their protection is `state`, PKCE and `nonce`.
- **Apple's client secret is generated at runtime.** `appleClientSecret()` (`src/lib/auth/providers/apple-secret.ts`) signs an ES256 JWT with the `.p8` key: header `kid` = `AUTH_APPLE_KEY_ID`; claims `iss` = `AUTH_APPLE_TEAM_ID`, `sub` = `AUTH_APPLE_ID`, `aud` = `https://appleid.apple.com`, `iat` now, `exp` one hour later; cached in memory and regenerated five minutes before `exp`. No secret is stored, so nothing expires every six months; the key itself does not expire and is replaced only if it leaks (runbook).
- **Environment** (`src/lib/env.auth.ts`, the existing "optional at boot, required at use" pattern; `.env.example` lists each, empty, with a one-line comment): `AUTH_GOOGLE_ID`, `AUTH_GOOGLE_SECRET`; `AUTH_APPLE_ID` (the Services ID), `AUTH_APPLE_TEAM_ID`, `AUTH_APPLE_KEY_ID`, `AUTH_APPLE_PRIVATE_KEY` (the `.p8` PEM, newlines as `\n`). Resolvers `googleProviderConfig()` and `appleProviderConfig()` return a config or null. Each group is all or none: a partial group fails `validateEnv` in `staging` and `production`, naming the missing keys and printing no value; an Apple key that does not parse as a PKCS#8 P-256 private key fails the same way. PR environments carry none of the six (their hosts are not registered redirect URIs), so no button renders there whatever the shared flags say.
- **Core extension (additive; a requirement on spec 012's core, built in the provider task; admin and florist unchanged).** `AuthSurfaceSchema` gains `oauthProviders?: ('google'|'apple')[]`, admitted only when `name` is `buyer`. The surface's OAuth transient cookies are `__Secure-fo-buyer.state`, `__Secure-fo-buyer.pkce.code_verifier` and `__Secure-fo-buyer.nonce`, each `Secure`, `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=None`, `Path=/api/auth/buyer`, `Max-Age=900`; and `__Secure-fo-buyer.callback-url` becomes `SameSite=None` (it holds only a same-origin path validated again at use). `SameSite=None` is needed because Apple's callback is a cross-site `POST`, which carries no `Lax` cookie. The session and CSRF cookies keep C.3's attributes. The `afterSignIn` hook runs for provider sign-ins as for the email link. Spec 012 AC-2, AC-3, AC-8, AC-9 and spec 011 AC-18 to AC-22 pass unmodified (AC-47).

**S.3 Linking without takeover (binding).** On a provider callback, in one transaction:
1. An `accounts` row for (provider, `sub`) exists: sign that user in. A changed email at the provider changes nothing here.
2. Otherwise decide whether the provider's email is **trusted**. Google: `email_verified === true` and either the domain is `gmail.com` or `googlemail.com`, or `hd` is present and equals the email's domain (Google is authoritative only for Gmail and Workspace addresses; for any other address the flag records a check made when the Google account was opened). Apple: `email_verified` is `true` or `"true"`. Anything else is untrusted.
3. Trusted, and a `users` row has that normalised email: insert the `accounts` row and sign in. Trusted, and none has: create the `users` row (the email, `emailVerified` now), insert the `accounts` row, sign in. Then `afterSignIn` (C.4) links the non-redacted guest `customer` of that email, so its orders appear, or creates one.
4. **Untrusted: nothing is linked or created and no session starts.** We store a pending link (`auth_pending_link`, S.7: provider, `sub`, the SHA-256 of the normalised email, one hour), set `__Secure-fo-buyer.pending-link` (32 random bytes; only their SHA-256 is stored; `Secure`, `HttpOnly`, `SameSite=Lax`, `Path=/`, one hour), send T14 to that email through the core (its limits and global cap apply; the response is the same whether or not a link was sent) and land on `/{locale}/account?state=confirm-email`. When that email link is confirmed in the same browser and the confirmed user's email hash equals the pending one, the `accounts` row is inserted in the `afterSignIn` transaction and the pending row and cookie are deleted. Confirmed in another browser, the email sign-in works and nothing is linked.
5. No email in the token: refused, `auth.buyer.provider_error {code: 'no_email'}`, `?state=provider-error`.
6. C.4's rules stand: a redacted customer is never linked; an address under `invalid` is refused.
7. A provider link authenticates only the buyer surface. Admin and florist stay email-link only; a `users` row they share with a buyer gains no admin or florist session from it.
8. The provider's name is written to the primary `customer.name` only while that is null; a name the buyer saved is never overwritten. Every provider sign-in creates a fresh session (C.10) and sets `last_signed_in_at`.
9. **Disconnect.** `account/details` lists "How you sign in" (email, plus each linked provider with the date) and a "Disconnect" submit per provider that deletes that `accounts` row. The email link to the account's address always remains.
