## 1. Problem

Specs 013 and 016 raise alerts at the point of failure (`captureAlert` in `src/lib/sentry.ts`: a Sentry event tagged `alert=<name>`), and say "Sentry's alert rule forwards it to email and WhatsApp (`plan/08`)". That forward does not exist. Today an alert is a Sentry event nobody is woken by. Nothing pages the founder when the site is down, the worker stops, a florist has not taken an order, or a delivery day is slipping. Several conditions `plan/08` §7 and `plan/09` Phase 1 acceptance 7 name are not watched at all: the webhook error rate, the job queue, delivery-day risk, email bounce and complaint rates, FX staleness as an alert rather than a log line, and the Neon cap as a page rather than a warning.

The founder asked (2026-10-22, 2026-10-24) for alerts on the phone **before the first paid order**. The founder is alone, so the system must decide what is worth waking up for, say once and not fifty times, stay quiet at night unless an order will be lost, and point at a written procedure for each alert. Operational simplicity is priority 4: the channel must cost almost nothing, need no app of ours, and need no secret handled by an agent beyond environment variables the founder sets.

## 2. Scope

**Alert core (`src/modules/ops`)**
- `ALERT_REGISTRY`: one closed list of every alert name, with its severity, scope (order or component), how it resolves, its act-by rule, and its runbook path and anchor. It absorbs spec 013's 12 names, spec 016's 3 names, spec 002's and spec 005's signals that become alerts, and the new names of §5.2.
- `raiseAlert(name, input)`: the one entry point. It validates with zod, keeps the Sentry event (calls `captureAlert`), and upserts one `ops_alert` row per open fingerprint (deduplication), then enqueues delivery. Existing `captureAlert` call sites in specs 013 and 016 code move to `raiseAlert`.
- Severity levels P1, P2, P3; quiet hours with the order-at-risk override; storm control; escalation; auto-resolve; reopen-on-flap.

**Channels**
- **Push: Pushover** (research in §5.6): P1 as emergency priority (repeats until acknowledged; iOS Critical Alerts bypass silent mode and Do Not Disturb once the founder enables them), P2 as high priority, resolve notices as low priority.
- **Email: Resend** (already in use, ADR-0013) to `OPS_ALERT_EMAIL` (spec 016's key): every P1 and P2 at once, and the daily summary.
- **Dead man's switch: healthchecks.io** heartbeat, pinged by the minute job tick; if the pings stop (worker down, database down, Railway down), healthchecks.io pages the founder through its own Pushover integration. This is the only path that works when our whole stack is down.

**Monitors (`alerts.monitor`, a minute pg-boss job on the worker, read-only except for `ops_alert`)**
- Webhook error rate above 2 % in 10 minutes (spec 013 §11 leaves it here).
- Order risk: not accepted in time, dispatch at risk on the delivery day, not delivered by evening, photo missing.
- Job queue backlog and failure spike.
- Site probes from the worker through the public URL: site down, a key page failing (this is the in-app "5xx spike" check; Sentry covers real-user error spikes).
- FX stale and FX aging.
- Email hard-bounce rate and complaints (from spec 016's `notification_delivery`).
- Neon usage (from spec 002's `neon.usage_probe`) and backup verification.

**Daily summary email** at 08:00 in the founder's zone, sent every day including quiet days.

**Admin alert log** `/admin/alerts` and `/admin/alerts/[id]`: list, filters, detail with the notification timeline, acknowledge, resolve with a reason, quiet-hours settings, "Send test alert".

**Go-live gate**: in production, the `payments.stripe` flag cannot be turned on until push, email and heartbeat are configured and a test P1 was acknowledged within the last 7 days.

**Runbooks** in `docs/runbooks/`: the top 10 (§5.7), each with Symptom, Check, Fix, Contact; `alerts.md` as the index from every alert name to its runbook; `alerts-setup.md` for the founder's one-time setup; `contacts.md` for vendor support routes.

**Data**: three tables (`ops_alert`, `ops_alert_notification`, `ops_alert_setting`), one migration with its rollback, RLS, retention.

**Env**: `PUSHOVER_APP_TOKEN`, `PUSHOVER_USER_KEY`, `PUSHOVER_BACKUP_USER_KEY` (optional), `ALERT_HEARTBEAT_URL`, `ALERT_SUMMARY_HEARTBEAT_URL` (optional); `OPS_ALERT_EMAIL` reused.

## 3. Non-goals

- **WhatsApp alerts to the founder.** The WhatsApp Cloud API needs Meta business verification, approved templates and per-message pricing; it is spec 028's (buyer and florist WhatsApp). `plan/08` §7's "WhatsApp webhook" line is superseded by Pushover (ADR-0021, §12).
- **SMS and voice calls.** Pushover emergency with Critical Alerts is the wake-up path. A paid voice-call service is a later decision (§13 Q3).
- **An on-call rota or a second person.** The optional backup key (§5.5) is the only provision; a rota is Phase 2+ (`plan/09`).
- **Automatic remediation.** Rerouting, cancelling and refunding stay in specs 013, 016 and 020. Alerts tell; they never act on an order.
- **Dashboards, the incident console, webhook replay UI** → `plan/11` §7 (Phase 4). The summary email and the alert log are the Phase 1 view.
- **A public status page** and any buyer-facing message. Buyer communication during an incident is a runbook step the founder takes by hand, inside the founder's rulings (no relay language, no negative framing post-purchase).
- **Changing what specs 013 and 016 detect.** Their conditions and names are unchanged; this spec gives them a severity, a channel and a runbook.
- **Cloudflare paid alerting, WAF alerts, R2 usage alerts.** Cloudflare's free plan has no origin error-rate alert; the worker's probes replace it. R2 usage is well under the free tier at launch; a later spec adds it if the summary shows growth.
- **Railway disk.** Neither `web` nor `worker` has a volume (spec 040 §5.3), so there is no disk to fill. Railway cost stays spec 040's usage alert.
- **Admin performance analytics, florist scorecards** → `plan/11` §7.

## 4. User stories

- As the **founder**, my phone wakes me only when money or a delivery is at risk and waiting would lose it, so I can sleep the rest of the time.
- As the **founder**, I get one notification per problem, not one per occurrence, and a calm "resolved" when it clears, so a storm does not bury the one alert that matters.
- As the **founder**, every alert names an order reference and links to the alert page, which links to the runbook, so I know what to do at 03:00 without thinking.
- As the **founder**, if my phone path itself is broken (Pushover down, worker down, Railway down) I still find out, by the heartbeat page or by the daily summary not arriving.
- As the **founder**, I read one email at 08:00 that tells me the system was healthy yesterday, or what was open, so silence is never ambiguous.
- As the **founder**, I cannot switch on real payments until I have proved that a test alert reaches my phone and I acknowledged it.
- As an **ops user** (later hire), I can acknowledge and resolve alerts in admin, and the audit log records who did it.
- As **Googlebot**, nothing changes: no new public URL, no client script, and the site probes catch an outage that would otherwise cost crawl budget.

## 5. Design

### 5.1 Data model changes (tables, columns, migrations + rollback)

Migration `NNNN_ops_alerts.sql` (next free number when the task starts), module `ops`, RLS on all three tables with policies for `system` and `admin` roles only (florists and buyers never read them).

1. **`ops_alert`**
   - `id uuid PK`, `environment text NOT NULL` (`APP_ENV`), `name text NOT NULL` (validated against `ALERT_REGISTRY` by zod at the boundary; no DB enum, so a new name is not a migration), `severity text NOT NULL CHECK IN ('P1','P2','P3')` (current severity, may rise, §5.5), `initial_severity` same check.
   - `fingerprint text NOT NULL` (§5.4), `order_id uuid NULL REFERENCES "order"(id)`, `component text NULL CHECK IN ('stripe','mollie','resend','worker','queue','site','fx','neon','backup','push','email','alerts')`, `CHECK ((order_id IS NULL) <> (component IS NULL))`.
   - `mode text NULL CHECK IN ('test','live')` (the order's mode; null for component alerts).
   - `status text NOT NULL DEFAULT 'open' CHECK IN ('open','acknowledged','resolved')`, `first_seen_at`, `last_seen_at`, `occurrences integer NOT NULL DEFAULT 1`, `reopen_count integer NOT NULL DEFAULT 0`, `act_by timestamptz NULL`.
   - `acknowledged_at`, `acknowledged_by text NULL` (an `ActorSchema` value or `pushover`), `resolved_at`, `resolved_by text NULL`, `resolution text NULL CHECK IN ('condition_cleared','fixed','false_alarm','accepted_risk')`, `CHECK ((status = 'resolved') = (resolved_at IS NOT NULL))`.
   - `context jsonb NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}'`: closed keys only (`AlertContextSchema`, §5.2): ids, counts, rates, provider names, ISO instants, the destination country code and city id. Never a name, address, phone, email, message or free text.
   - `pages_24h integer NOT NULL DEFAULT 0`, `last_page_at timestamptz NULL` (escalation cap, §5.5).
   - **Deduplication index:** `UNIQUE (environment, fingerprint) WHERE status <> 'resolved'`. Two concurrent raises meet in `INSERT … ON CONFLICT … DO UPDATE` and produce one row.
   - Indexes: `(status, severity, last_seen_at DESC)`, `(order_id)`.
2. **`ops_alert_notification`**: `id uuid PK`, `alert_id uuid NOT NULL REFERENCES ops_alert(id) ON DELETE CASCADE`, `channel text CHECK IN ('push','email')`, `kind text CHECK IN ('raise','reminder','escalation','bump','held_digest','resolve','test')`, `priority smallint NULL` (Pushover priority sent), `recipient text CHECK IN ('primary','backup','email')`, `status text CHECK IN ('sent','failed','held','skipped')`, `skip_reason text NULL CHECK IN ('quiet_hours','storm','not_configured','push_disabled','severity')`, `provider_ref text NULL` (Pushover receipt or request id, Resend message id), `error_class text NULL` (class and HTTP status only), `created_at`, `receipt_acknowledged_at NULL`. A digest or storm notice that covers several alerts gets one row per covered alert with the same `provider_ref`.
3. **`ops_alert_setting`**: one row (`id smallint PK CHECK (id = 1)`), `quiet_start time NOT NULL DEFAULT '23:00'`, `quiet_end time NOT NULL DEFAULT '07:00'`, `time_zone text NOT NULL DEFAULT 'Asia/Karachi'` (IANA, validated by zod against `Intl.supportedValuesOf('timeZone')`; §13 Q2), `updated_at`, `updated_by`. Seeded by the migration. Changes are written through an admin action with an `audit_log` row (spec 012).
4. **Retention:** a `retention.sweep` handler row (spec 002 mechanism): resolved alerts older than 12 months are deleted with their notifications. Open alerts are never swept.
5. **No change** to `order`, `order_event`, `webhook_inbox`, `notification_delivery` or pg-boss tables; the monitors read them.

**Rollback** (`NNNN_ops_alerts.down.sql`): drop `ops_alert_notification`, `ops_alert`, `ops_alert_setting`, the retention row. No accounting or order data lives here, so no guard is needed; the alert history is lost, which the down file states in its header. Code rollback first: with the tables gone, `raiseAlert` falls back to Sentry plus log (AC-4), so a down migration never silences the Sentry trail. Spec 002's migrate, rollback, migrate round trip runs over it.

### 5.2 API / server actions / jobs / events (zod schemas named)

**Module `src/modules/ops` (public `index.ts`)**
- `ALERT_REGISTRY: Record<AlertName, AlertDefinition>` with `AlertDefinitionSchema`: `{ severity (one level, or a map by component where the table below gives two, as for `webhook.dead`), scope: 'order' | 'component', component?, resolve: 'auto_monitor' | 'auto_order_state' | 'manual', actBy?: ActByRule, runbook: 'docs/runbooks/<file>.md#<anchor>', titleKey }`. `AlertName` is the union of the keys.
- `raiseAlert(name: AlertName, input: AlertInput): Promise<RaiseResult>`; `AlertInputSchema` = `{ orderId?: uuid, component?, mode?, actBy?: Date, context?: AlertContext }`; `AlertContextSchema` is `.strict()` over the closed key set.
- `clearCondition(name, subject)`: called by monitors when a check is clear (auto-resolve, §5.5).
- `acknowledgeAlert(id, actor)`, `resolveAlert(id, actor, resolution)` (`ResolveAlertSchema`).
- `quietHoursDecision(alert, setting, now): 'page' | 'hold' | 'email_only'`: pure; the whole rule of §5.5.
- `fingerprintOf(name, subject)`: pure (§5.4).
- `PushChannel` interface `{ send(PushMessage): Promise<PushResult>; receipt(ref): Promise<ReceiptStatus>; cancel(ref): Promise<void> }`, `pushoverChannel(env)`, `capturingPushChannel()` for tests. Schemas: `PushMessageSchema` (title at most 250 characters, message at most 1024, url at most 512, priority -1 to 2, retry at least 30, expire at most 10800, as Pushover's API limits), `PushoverSendResponseSchema`, `PushoverReceiptSchema`.
- `heartbeat(env)`: one `GET` to `ALERT_HEARTBEAT_URL` with a 5 s timeout; never throws.

**Changes to existing code**
- `src/lib/sentry.ts` `captureAlert` stays (the Sentry trail) but is called only from `src/modules/ops`. Every current caller (spec 013's payments code, spec 016's routing and Resend code) switches to `raiseAlert` with the same name. A source scan enforces it (AC-1).
- `neon.usage_probe` (spec 002) and `db.backup_verify` keep their `warn` and Sentry message and also call `raiseAlert` (`db.usage_high`, `db.backup_failed`).
- `src/modules/notifications/mailer.ts` tag registry gains `ops_alert` and `ops_daily_summary`.
- `ADMIN_PERMISSIONS` gains `alerts.view` (admin, ops), `alerts.act` (admin, ops), `alerts.settings` (admin).
- Spec 012's flag guard gains the go-live precondition for `payments.stripe` in production (AC-32).

The probes double as the "5xx spike" check: Cloudflare's free plan cannot alert on an origin error rate, and the app cannot see its own final status codes reliably. Real-user server errors are Sentry's job: an issue alert rule (production, "seen more than 20 times in 5 minutes") emails the founder's Pushover P2 alias (§5.6). That rule is founder dashboard setup, not code.

**Jobs (pg-boss, added to `REGISTERED_JOBS` in `src/jobs/index.ts` with versioned zod payloads)**
- `alerts.dispatch` `{ alertId, kind }`, `singletonKey = alertId + kind`: decides and sends per §5.5; retries per channel (Pushover 5xx or no answer: 3 tries at least 5 s apart; 4xx: no retry, because repeated 4xx can block the IP).
- `alerts.monitor` `{}`: the checks above; writes nothing but `ops_alert` (through `raiseAlert` and `clearCondition`).
- `alerts.escalate` `{}`, every minute: receipts, bumps, reminders, held digests at quiet end (§5.5).
- `alerts.daily_summary` `{ date }`, pg-boss cron at 08:00 in `ops_alert_setting.time_zone`, `singletonKey = date`.
- Heartbeat: the minute tick (`/api/internal/jobs/tick` or the worker loop, whichever runs the tick per spec 040) calls `heartbeat()` **after** a tick that reached the database and found `alerts.monitor` completed within the last 5 minutes. A failed tick sends nothing, so healthchecks.io sees the silence.

### 5.5 Delivery rules: severity, quiet hours, storms, escalation, resolve

**Severity meaning**
- **P1, page now:** money or a delivery will be lost if nobody acts within hours, or the site or the pipeline is down. Pushover priority 2 (emergency): `retry` 120 s, `expire` 3600 s, `tags` = alert id (so an admin acknowledgement can cancel it). Email at once.
- **P2, tell me today:** needs action the same day but not at night. Pushover priority 1. Email at once.
- **P3, tell me tomorrow:** no push, no immediate email; in the daily summary and the alert log.

**Quiet hours** (`quietHoursDecision`, pure; window from `ops_alert_setting`, may span midnight, evaluated in its IANA zone so DST is the zone's job):
- (a) **P1 pages**, with one exception: an **order-scoped** P1 whose `act_by` is later than quiet end plus 60 minutes is **held** until quiet end (nothing is lost by waiting; for example an order exhausted at 22:00 Warsaw for delivery in four days).
- (b) **P2 is held** until quiet end, **except an order-scoped P2 whose `act_by` falls before quiet end plus 60 minutes: it pages as P1** (the order-at-risk override).
- (c) **P3 never pushes.**
- Email is silent by nature and is never held: P1 and P2 emails go at once at any hour.
- At quiet end, `alerts.escalate` sends **one** grouped push (priority 1, or 2 if any held alert is P1) listing the count and the highest severity of held alerts still open; alerts resolved during the night are not pushed.

**Test-mode orders and staging**
- An alert on an order with `mode = 'test'` is capped at P2 and its title starts `[TEST]`. A test order never wakes the founder.
- When `APP_ENV` is not `production`, push is sent only while the database flag `alerts.push` (global, registered in spec 012's flags page) is on; it is off by default on staging. Email is always sent. The founder turns it on to rehearse, then off.

**Storm control**
- At most 10 pushes in any rolling 15 minutes per environment, and at most 20 alert emails. Beyond that, deliveries are recorded `held` with `skip_reason = 'storm'`, and one storm push ("N more alerts held; open the alert log", priority 2 if any is P1) and one storm email go out; the cap resets when the window clears.

**Escalation (`alerts.escalate`, every minute)**
- **Receipts:** for each P1 push with a receipt and no acknowledgement, poll Pushover's receipt endpoint (at most every 2 minutes per receipt). An acknowledged receipt sets `acknowledged_at`, `acknowledged_by = 'pushover'`, `status = 'acknowledged'`. No public callback endpoint is built, so there is nothing new to authenticate.
- **15 minutes unacknowledged:** an escalation email ("still unacknowledged"), and, if `PUSHOVER_BACKUP_USER_KEY` is set, the same emergency push to that key.
- **Expiry (60 minutes) unacknowledged and still open:** one new emergency push. At most **3** emergency pages per alert in 24 hours (`pages_24h`); after that, email only, and the summary flags it.
- **Admin acknowledgement** cancels outstanding emergency retries (Pushover cancel by tag) and stops escalation.
- **Bump:** an order-scoped alert still open at `act_by` minus 60 minutes rises one level (P3 to P2, P2 to P1) and notifies once with `kind = 'bump'`, subject to the quiet-hours rule at its new level. The monitor's own thresholds (`order.not_accepted`, `order.dispatch_at_risk`, `order.photo_missing`) raise severity the same way.
- **Reminder:** a P2 open and unacknowledged for 4 hours outside quiet hours gets one reminder push.

**Resolve**
- `auto_monitor` alerts resolve after the condition is clear on 2 consecutive runs (flap guard). `auto_order_state` alerts resolve when the order leaves the at-risk status set named in the monitor table (checked by `alerts.monitor`). `manual` alerts resolve only in admin.
- A resolve sends a priority -1 push ("Resolved: …") outside quiet hours only, and no email (the summary lists it). Resolution `condition_cleared`.
- **Reopen:** a raise of the same fingerprint within 30 minutes of its resolve reopens the same row (`reopen_count + 1`) instead of inserting; a reopened P1 pages again within the 24-hour cap; a reopened P2 or P3 does not push again until 4 hours after its last push.

**Channel failure**
- Pushover answering 4xx, or 5xx after retries, or `X-Limit-App-Remaining` at 0 raises `alerts.channel_failed` (component `push`), delivered **by email only** (no loop). Email failing after Resend retries raises `alerts.channel_failed` (component `email`) by push only. Both failing: Sentry and the log; the heartbeat keeps running, so the founder learns from the missing summary (`alerts-setup.md` says so).
- Push not configured (either Pushover key empty): pushes recorded `skipped` with `not_configured`, one `warn` per process, email still sent, banner and summary say so.

**Message content (push and email)**
- Push title: `[P1] ` + the registry title (message key), plus `[TEST]` where it applies. Push body: the order reference (`FO-XXXX-XXXX`) or the component name, the act-by time formatted with `Intl.DateTimeFormat` in the founder's zone (and destination zone for order alerts), occurrences if more than 1. Push `url`: `{APP_BASE_URL}/admin/alerts/{alertId}` (an alert UUID; no order id, reference or personal data in the URL). Nothing else.
- Email: the same, plus the context keys (destination country, city name, delivery date, attempt counts, rates), the runbook link and the admin link. Never a buyer or recipient name, email, phone, address or card message, and no free text from any input (spec 016's ops-alert rule, `plan/07`).

## 9. Acceptance criteria

**Registry and raising**
- **AC-1** Every alert name the application raises has exactly one entry in `ALERT_REGISTRY` with severity, scope, resolve mode, act-by rule and a runbook path with an anchor. A source scan of `src/` finds no `raiseAlert` call with a name outside the registry and no `captureAlert` call outside `src/modules/ops`.
- **AC-2** Two raises of the same name and subject, including two concurrent ones, leave one open `ops_alert` row with `occurrences = 2` and produce one push and one email; the same name for a different order produces a second row.
- **AC-3** A raise with a name not in the registry, or a context key outside `AlertContextSchema`, writes no row; in tests it throws, in production it logs `alerts.invalid` and still sends the Sentry event.
- **AC-4** When the database write fails, `raiseAlert` still sends the Sentry event with the `alert` and `order_id` tags and logs `alerts.record_failed`.

**Delivery**
- **AC-5** A P1 raised outside quiet hours produces, within 60 s, one Pushover request with priority 2, retry 120, expire 3600 and the alert id as tag, and one email to `OPS_ALERT_EMAIL`; both are recorded in `ops_alert_notification` with their provider refs.
- **AC-6** A P2 outside quiet hours produces a priority 1 push and an email; a P3 produces neither and appears in the next daily summary and the alert log.
- **AC-7** `quietHoursDecision` returns exactly the outcomes of §5.5 rules (a), (b) and (c) for the table of fixtures, including a window that spans midnight, a configured zone on its DST change night (`Europe/Warsaw`, last Sunday of October), an order P1 with `act_by` after quiet end plus 60 minutes (hold) and an order P2 with `act_by` before it (page as P1).
- **AC-8** At quiet end, alerts held overnight and still open produce one grouped push whose priority is 2 if any is P1 and 1 otherwise; alerts resolved overnight produce none.
- **AC-9** An alert on a `test`-mode order is capped at P2 and its push title starts `[TEST]`; with `APP_ENV` not `production` and `alerts.push` off, no push is sent and the email is.
- **AC-10** The eleventh push inside 15 minutes is recorded `held` with `storm` and exactly one storm push goes out for the window; emails continue until their own cap of 20, and the twenty-first is held with exactly one storm email.
- **AC-11** Every push has a title of at most 250 characters, a body of at most 1024 containing only the registry title, severity, order reference or component, act-by time and occurrences, and a URL of the form `{APP_BASE_URL}/admin/alerts/{uuid}`; the PII scan over pushes and emails built from fixtures with names, emails, phones, addresses and card messages finds none of them.
- **AC-12** With either Pushover key empty, pushes are recorded `skipped` with `not_configured`, email is still sent, and the admin banner names the missing variable (name only). A Pushover 4xx is not retried and raises `alerts.channel_failed` by email only; a 5xx is retried up to 3 times at least 5 s apart before doing the same.

**Escalation and resolve**
- **AC-13** A P1 push whose receipt is unacknowledged after 15 minutes produces one escalation email (and one push to `PUSHOVER_BACKUP_USER_KEY` when set); at receipt expiry with the alert still open and unacknowledged, one new emergency push; no alert receives more than 3 emergency pages in 24 hours.
- **AC-14** A receipt reported acknowledged sets `status = 'acknowledged'` and `acknowledged_by = 'pushover'`; an admin acknowledgement sets the admin actor and sends Pushover's cancel for the alert's tag; after either, no escalation or reminder is sent for that alert.
- **AC-15** An order-scoped alert still open at `act_by` minus 60 minutes rises one severity level and sends one `bump` notification, which obeys the quiet-hours rule at the new level.
- **AC-16** A monitor alert resolves after 2 consecutive clear runs, not after 1; an order alert resolves when its order leaves the at-risk set; a resolve sends one priority -1 push outside quiet hours and none inside; a re-raise within 30 minutes of a resolve reopens the same row (`reopen_count = 1`) rather than inserting.

**Monitors**
- **AC-17** `webhook.error_rate` for one provider over 10 minutes: 2 failed of 50 fires; 1 failed of 3 does not; 2 failed of 200 (1 %) does not; rows older than 10 minutes are not counted.
- **AC-18** `order.not_accepted`: a `routed` order with no acceptance raises P2 at first offer plus 120 minutes (plus 60 for same-day), not one minute earlier, and P1 from the routing deadline minus 3 hours; a night-deferred order is measured from its real `offered_at`; acceptance resolves it.
- **AC-19** `order.dispatch_at_risk`: an `accepted` or `in_production` order due today destination-local raises P2 at 13:00 and P1 at 16:00 local, and nothing for an order due tomorrow; `out_for_delivery` resolves it.
- **AC-20** `order.not_delivered` raises P1 at 20:00 destination-local on the delivery date for an order not delivered, closed, cancelled or refunded, and nothing at 19:59.
- **AC-21** `order.photo_missing` raises P3 for an order `delivered` more than 2 hours without proof and rises to P2 after 24 hours; `photo_uploaded` resolves it.
- **AC-22** `jobs.backlog` fires for a job in `created` whose `start_after` is 11 minutes in the past and not for one 9 minutes in the past or one scheduled in the future; `jobs.failed_spike` fires at 5 failed jobs in 60 minutes and not at 4.
- **AC-23** Site probes: health failing on 2 consecutive runs raises `site.down`; one probe URL failing on 2 consecutive runs raises `site.page_failing` with the probe key; a single failed run raises nothing; 2 clean runs resolve. Probes use `APP_BASE_URL`, no query string, a 10 s timeout and `User-Agent: fo-probe/1`.
- **AC-24** With `fxAsOf` past `fxRateStaleAfter()` the monitor raises `fx.stale` (P2); within 24 counted weekday hours of it, `fx.aging` (P3); a weekend does not count toward the 24 hours.
- **AC-25** `email.bounce_rate` fires at more than 4 % hard bounces over 24 hours with at least 25 sends and not with 24 sends; one complaint raises `email.complaint` at P3 and a second within 24 hours raises it to P2.
- **AC-26** `neon.usage_probe` at 70 % raises `db.usage_high` P3 and at 90 % P2; a failed `db.backup_verify`, or none successful in 36 hours, raises `db.backup_failed` P2.

## 11. Observability

- **This spec is the alerting layer**; its own health is watched three ways: the healthchecks.io heartbeat (pipeline and database alive), `alerts.channel_failed` (each channel watching the other), and the daily summary plus its optional heartbeat (silence is itself a signal).
- **Logs:** one `info` per raise (`alert`, `severity`, `fingerprint`, `outcome: inserted | deduplicated | reopened`), one per delivery (`channel`, `kind`, `priority`, `outcome`, `skip_reason`), one per monitor run with counts and `duration_ms`. Field allowlist in §8.
- **Sentry:** every raise still produces the Sentry event (`captureAlert`), so the stack context lives where it did. Sentry stops being the paging path for registry alerts (the alert-channel ADR): its only paging rule is the production error-spike rule to the P2 alias.
- **Metrics in the summary:** alerts by severity per day, median time to acknowledge P1, pages per alert, Pushover quota remaining.

## 13. Open questions

Each has a recommended default; the spec can be approved on the defaults.

- **Q1: Which push channel?** Options: Pushover, ntfy, Telegram bot, WhatsApp Cloud API (§5.6). **Default: Pushover** ($4.99 one-time; emergency priority with acknowledgement; iOS Critical Alerts), with Resend email beside it and healthchecks.io as the dead man's switch.
- **Q2: Quiet hours and time zone.** **Default: 23:00 to 07:00, `Asia/Karachi`** (`plan/06` describes a Pakistan-resident founder), editable on `/admin/alerts` at any time. Note: Warsaw evening (19:00 to 20:00 in winter) already falls in this window; the order-at-risk override covers what matters.
- **Q3: A backup person or a voice call for unacknowledged P1s?** **Default: none at launch.** The env key `PUSHOVER_BACKUP_USER_KEY` exists so a trusted helper can be added later without code; a paid voice-call service is not added.
- **Q4: The severity table in §5.2.** **Default: approve as written** (P1 only where money or a delivery is lost within hours, or the site or pipeline is down).
- **Q5: Order references to a US push service.** Pushover is a US company, and the reference is pseudonymous data. **Default: accept**, with the push carrying the reference only (no city, date or name), recorded in the RoPA; the founder checks Pushover's terms and DPA position. If rejected: ntfy self-hosted on Railway EU, same payload.
- **Q6: Delivery-day thresholds.** **Default: dispatch check P2 at 13:00 and P1 at 16:00 destination-local; not delivered P1 at 20:00.** Peak days (spec 024) may pull these earlier later.
- **Q7: "Not accepted" thresholds.** **Default: P2 at first offer plus 120 minutes (60 for same-day), P1 from the routing deadline minus 3 hours.** Individual offer timeouts are normal routing (spec 016) and do not alert.
- **Q8: The go-live gate (AC-32).** **Default: yes.** Real payments cannot be switched on until a test alert reached the phone and was acknowledged within 7 days. This makes "alerts before the first paid order" a check, not a promise.
- **Q9: Daily summary time and cadence.** **Default: 08:00 founder zone, every day, including quiet days** (a missing email is how a dead email path is noticed).
- **Q10: Uptime monitor product.** The worker's probes plus the heartbeat cover what spec 040's external monitor was for. **Default: keep the external monitor optional;** if the founder already has UptimeRobot or Better Stack from spec 040, point its alert email at the Pushover P1 alias. UptimeRobot's free-plan terms for commercial use were reported changing in 2024 to 2026; check before relying on it.
- **Q11: Pushover e-mail aliases at emergency priority.** Pushover documents per-alias priority; that an alias can be set to emergency (repeat until acknowledged) is assumed, not confirmed. **Default: use it for Sentry and Railway emails;** if an alias cannot be emergency, those sources page at high priority (P2 behaviour), which is acceptable because the worker's own probes and the heartbeat carry every P1 outage.
