## 1. Problem

Our buyers are people abroad who want to be present in a parent's or partner's life in Poland. One bouquet on a birthday does that once; flowers every month does it all year. Every large flower retailer sells this: Bloom & Wild sells prepaid gift subscriptions of 3, 6 or 12 months beside pay-as-you-go plans every 7, 14 or 28 days; Serenata sells weekly, fortnightly or monthly deliveries over 3, 6 or 12 months with skip, pause and message changes; Fleurop Switzerland requires at least 3 deliveries to the same address, bills the whole period in advance on one invoice, and asks for 2 working days' notice to pause or cancel; Interflora Belgium (Fleurop) sets every later delivery on the same date as the first (§14). The founder approved monthly flowers on 2026-10-24 (idea J) for recurring revenue.

Two things make it more than "an order with a repeat button". **First, money.** A recurring card charge needs a stored card and off-session charges, which the founder ruled out on 2026-10-14 ("don't hold money, charge instantly"), and in the UK it will fall under the subscription-contracts regime of the Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024 for contracts made from 1 January 2027 (reminder notices, one-step exit, renewal cooling-off). A **prepaid** plan is one charge at purchase for a fixed number of deliveries with no renewal: no stored card, no mandate, and on the face of DMCCA s.254 not a "subscription contract" at all, because the buyer never becomes automatically liable for a further payment (lawyer confirms, §13 Q7). **Second, orders.** Each delivery must be a real order that a florist is offered, makes, delivers and photographs, through `orderService.transition` (ADR-0009) and spec 016's routing, with its own date, its own cutoff and its own tracking, while the money was taken once, months earlier. This spec defines how one prepaid plan becomes N orders without a second charge, a second price or a second state machine for orders.

## 2. Scope

Each bullet is testable; §9 names the AC.

**What is sold**
- **One plan product per destination country**, flagged `planEligible` in the catalogue (through spec 005's provider; editable in spec 012b's CMS once its catalogue cutover lands). The default is a "seasonal bouquet": a different mix each month in the size the buyer picks, priced per tier like any product (§13 Q2). A plan is: that product, one tier, **3, 6 or 12 monthly deliveries**, one recipient, one printed card message, one buyer.
- **Prepaid only.** One charge for the whole plan at purchase, captured at once (spec 013's automatic capture), with no saved card, no `setup_future_usage`, no off-session charge and no renewal. Recurring monthly billing is a non-goal (§3, §13 Q1).

**Price shown = price charged**
- Per-delivery price = the plan product's tier price for the destination on a **standard date** (selectable and surcharge-free), projected into the display currency by `priceProjection()`, VAT and delivery included, less an optional plan discount in basis points per plan length (`src/config/plans.ts`, default 0 for every length, §13 Q3), rounded half-up to the minor unit. Plan total = per-delivery price × number of deliveries, exactly, so each delivery's share is a whole number of minor units with no remainder.
- `planPriceProjection(sku, countryIso, tierKey, locale, deliveries)` in `modules/catalog` (an additive request on spec 005, §12) returns `{ perDeliveryMinor, totalMinor, currency, vatPerDelivery }`. The plan page, its JSON-LD, the checkout quote and the charge all read it; nothing else computes a plan price.
- No add-ons in a plan. No date surcharges can apply (every scheduled date is a standard date). Nothing is added at checkout.

**Dates and cutoffs**
- **First delivery date:** chosen on the plan page from spec 009's grid, restricted to standard dates at least **2 days** after today in the destination's time zone (§13 Q9), so no same-day or next-day cutoff can race the payment. Surcharge dates and closed dates show as unselectable with their reason in words.
- **Later deliveries:** `planSchedule({ countryIso, firstDate, deliveries })` in `modules/geo` (pure): delivery *k* targets the first date's day of month in month *k − 1* after the first, clamped to the month's last day (31 January → 28 or 29 February), then moved **forward** to the first standard date on or after the target (skipping non-delivery weekdays, Sundays where the country has no Sunday delivery, public holidays and surcharge days). Dates are strictly increasing and at least 14 days apart, or the schedule is refused.
- **Holiday coverage:** a plan length is offered only when the destination's holiday data covers every target date of that length plus 14 days (`holidayCoverageUntil(countryIso)`); otherwise that length is absent from the page and refused by the server.
- **Cutoff per delivery:** each delivery becomes an order **7 days** before its date (the lead, `plans.ts`, §13 Q10), long before any cutoff, so routing (spec 016's SLAs) has a week. The first delivery is materialised at payment when its date is within the lead.
- **A date that stops being available** (a holiday added to the data, a country closure) is re-checked at materialisation: the delivery moves forward to the next standard date, its original date is kept, the buyer is emailed (S2), and nothing is charged or refunded. A move that would land within 14 days of the next delivery is not made; the founder is alerted instead.

**Buying a plan (spec 010's checkout, one kind more)**
- The plan page posts to spec 010's `POST /api/checkout/start` with `kind: 'subscription'`, the tier, the number of deliveries, the first date and the displayed total. The draft carries `kind` and the signed quote covers the plan.
- Step 1 (recipient) is unchanged, including spec 016's `canServe` coverage check for the postcode. Step 2 has the card message and buyer details and **no add-on row**. Step 3 shows the plan summary, **every delivery date**, the per-delivery price, the one total, the VAT by rate, the plan's pre-contract block (§8) and the button "Pay {amount} and order" (wording lawyer-gated with spec 010's button keys).
- **Mode:** a subscription draft is never `demo`: where `checkoutMode()` would return `demo`, it returns `closed` for a subscription draft. `live` additionally needs `planReadiness()`: the `terms` and `cancellation` documents for the buyer's regime list `subscription` in their frontmatter `covers[]` (they contain the plan sections and are lawyer-reviewed), and the country's `subscriptions.open` flag is on.
- **Placement:** `subscriptionService.create(draft)` writes the `subscription` row (`pending_payment`) and its N `subscription_delivery` rows with their dates, amounts and VAT, in one transaction, idempotent on the draft. Spec 013's pay step then charges the total against the subscription (§5.2). On `payment captured`, the subscription moves to `active`, S1 is sent, and the first delivery is materialised if due. An unpaid plan expires on spec 013's unpaid-order window.

**Each delivery becomes an order (ADR-0009)**
- The hourly job `subscription.materialise` takes every `scheduled` delivery whose date is within the lead and, per delivery, in one transaction: `orderService.place()` with `funding = 'subscription'`, the plan's product and tier at the delivery's locked amount, the plan's buyer and recipient snapshots, card, `terms_version`, `supply_model`, FX snapshot and the delivery's stored VAT; then `orderService.transition(orderId, 'order.captured', 'system:subscription.materialise', { funding: 'subscription', subscriptionId, deliveryId })` (spec 002 A6 row 2, `placed → paid_unrouted`, actor class `system`). No `payment` row is written for the order. From there the order is an ordinary order: spec 016 routes it, the florist accepts and delivers, spec 013 moves it to production on the delivery morning, spec 018 tracks it.
- Idempotent: one delivery yields at most one order (`subscription_delivery.order_id UNIQUE`), whatever the re-runs or concurrent workers (`FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`).

**Cancelling**
- **Buyer, whole plan, any time:** from the plan page (two steps: review, then confirm) or from the public cancel page (below). Every delivery **not yet being prepared** is cancelled and refunded in full: deliveries still `scheduled`, and materialised orders whose status is in spec 018's free-cancel set P (before 06:00 destination-local on the delivery date). Deliveries being prepared, on their way or delivered are not refunded. The subscription moves to `cancelled` and S3 states the refund amount.
- **Refund mechanics:** one refund through spec 013's refund service against the plan's payment for all `scheduled` deliveries cancelled (each delivery records the `refund_id`); each materialised order in P goes through spec 013's `cancelOrder`, whose refund draws on the plan's payment for a plan-funded order (§12 request on spec 013). The refunded total never exceeds the captured total; a delivery is refunded at most once.
- **One delivery** (admin only, for example the recipient is away, or spec 016's no-florist path cancels its order): the same rules for that delivery; the plan stays `active`.
- **The public cancel page** `/{locale}/plan/cancel` (footer link "Cancel monthly flowers"; in `de` "Verträge hier kündigen"): plan reference, email, optional reason, one button ("Cancel now"; `de` "jetzt kündigen"). It answers with the same receipt page (time stamped) whether or not the pair matches; on a match it cancels exactly as the plan page does and sends S3. Honeypot, limiter and bounded body as spec 048 §2. This one page serves the EU withdrawal function (CRD Art. 11a, from 19 June 2026), Germany's cancellation button (BGB §312k) and the UK's one-step exit (§8).

**What the buyer sees**
- **Plan page** `/{locale}/plan/{token}` (spec 018's signed-token scheme on the subscription's own `public_token` and `token_version`; `noindex`, `no-store`): the plan reference `FP-XXXX-XXXX`, product and size, destination city, status, amount paid and any refunds ("Refunded {amount} on {date}"), every delivery with its date (and "moved from {date}" where moved) and its state ("Scheduled", then the order's buyer states: "We have your order", "Being made", "On its way", "Delivered", "Cancelled"), a link to each materialised delivery's tracking page, and the cancel control. No recipient name, street, phone or card message (spec 018 AC-4's rule).
- **Lookup:** spec 018's tracking lookup accepts a plan reference with the buyer's email and emails the plan link; the answer is the same for unknown pairs.
- **Emails (spec 016's notifications target and Mailer):** S1 plan confirmed (thanks, city and first date, every date, the "Your plan terms" foot block, terms PDF and invoice PDF attached, the plan link); S2 delivery moved; S3 plan cancelled with the refunded amount. For plan-funded orders spec 016 **skips B1** (S1 already confirmed the contract) and adds "Delivery {k} of {n} of your monthly flowers" to B2, B3 and B4. No buyer message ever mentions an offer, an acceptance, a florist being found or another florist (`RELAY_PATTERNS`).

**What the recipient sees**
- Each month, the bouquet with the printed card carrying the buyer's message and the standard card notice (`plan/07` §1.3). Nothing else: no recipient email exists anywhere, and no "1 of 6" line is printed (§13 Q11).

**Admin (`/admin/subscriptions`)**
- List: newest first, 50 per page, filters by status, destination and next delivery date; a row shows the reference, created time, status, mode marker (`test`), destination city, product and tier, deliveries done and total, next date, total via `formatMoney`. No recipient or buyer contact data in the list.
- Detail: the schedule with each delivery's date, original date, state, linked order (to spec 012's order detail), amount and refund; the payment; the event history. Actions, each audited: **move a scheduled delivery** to another standard date strictly between its neighbours' dates and at least the lead from today (sends S2); **cancel one delivery**; **cancel the plan** (actor `admin:{uuid}`, same refund rules). Opening the detail writes `audit_log` `subscription.viewed`.

**Gates, docs, artboards**
- e2e for buying a 3-delivery plan in `en-gb` (Stripe test mode, card) and `pl`, JS-on and JS-off up to the pay step; materialisation, routing hand-off, cancellation and refund integration tests; axe and visual baselines for the plan page, checkout plan states, plan token page and cancel page; Lighthouse on the plan page; `docs/runbooks/monthly-flowers.md`; RoPA rows; artboards (`plan-page`, `checkout-plan-review`, `plan-status`, `plan-cancel`, `email-plan-confirmed`, `admin-subscriptions`) in `docs/design/` before `/plan-tasks`.

## 3. Non-goals

- **Recurring monthly billing, saved cards, off-session charges, automatic renewal** (pay-as-you-go plans). Ruled out by the founder's "charge at once, never hold cards"; it would also bring the UK DMCCA subscription regime (from 1 January 2027) and per-renewal reminders. Bringing it back is a new founder decision and an amendment (§13 Q1).
- **Weekly or fortnightly plans** (§13 Q5); **skip, pause, change of address, change of card message** by the buyer. In v1 the buyer cancels or asks us; the founder can move a date (§13 Q8).
- **Gifting a plan to start later as a voucher, gift cards, plan vouchers** → `plan/05` #24 (Phase 4).
- **Plans with add-ons, or a different product each month chosen by the buyer** (§13 Q2).
- **A plan hub without a country** (`/en-gb/flower-subscription`) and plans in non-live countries. One country-scoped page per live country.
- **Google Shopping feed entries for plans** (idea D's feed lists single products only).
- **Plans for companies** beyond buying one per recipient (spec 048 slice B).
- **The florist's view.** A plan delivery is an ordinary order to the florist; specs 011 and 016 are unchanged on their side.
- **Editing `CLAUDE.md`, `plan/*` or an accepted ADR.**

## 4. User stories

- As a **daughter in Manchester**, I pay once for six months of flowers for my mother in Gdańsk, see every delivery date before I pay, and never think about it again until the photos arrive.
- As that buyer, I can stop the plan any time and get back every delivery that is not yet being made, in one click from my email or from a page I can find without signing in.
- As a **buyer in Berlin**, I find "Verträge hier kündigen" in the footer, as German law requires, and it works.
- As the **mother in Gdańsk**, I get a fresh bouquet each month with my daughter's card, and nobody asks me for anything.
- As a **florist**, a plan delivery reaches me like any order, a week ahead, already paid.
- As the **founder**, I see every plan, its next date and its money in admin, I can move a date or cancel one delivery when a buyer asks, and the books show one payment and one invoice per plan.
- As **Googlebot**, I find one server-rendered plan page per live country and locale, whose `Offer` prices equal the visible plan totals.

For a subscription the amount is read from `subscription.total_minor` (no exported function takes an amount), capture is automatic, the PaymentIntent carries `metadata.payment_id` and `metadata.subject = 'subscription'`, and `setup_future_usage` is never set. `reconcile` on a captured subscription payment calls `subscriptionService.transition(id, 'subscription.paid', 'system:payments.reconcile', { paymentId })` instead of an order transition. **Order events (requested amendment to spec 010's registry and spec 013's schema).** `order.captured` gains payload version 2: `{ funding: 'payment'; paymentId } | { funding: 'subscription'; subscriptionId; deliveryId }`; v1 payloads still parse. `place()` accepts `funding` and, for `subscription`, refuses unless called from `materialiseOne` with a delivery in `scheduled` status (checked in the same transaction).

**Refunds (requested amendment to spec 013).** For an order with `funding = 'subscription'`, the refund that follows `order.cancelled` (row 32) draws on the subscription's payment, for exactly the delivery's `amount_minor`, and records the delivery as refunded. `succeededRefunds` serves the plan page and spec 018's tracking page alike.

**Notifications (requested amendment to spec 016).** `notificationsFor` skips B1 when `order.funding = 'subscription'`; B2, B3 and B4 render the "Delivery {k} of {n}" line from the delivery's `seq` and the plan's `deliveries_total`. New templates S1, S2, S3 on the `notifications` target for `subscription.paid`, `delivery.rescheduled` and `subscription.cancelled`, with spec 016's mode rule. S1 waits for the invoice PDF as B1 does (spec 016 A-founder-2026-10-14b item 3).

**Invoices (requested amendment to spec 018).** One invoice per plan, issued at `subscription.paid`, for the plan total, with every delivery's date and VAT by rate (VAT falls due on prepayment; accountant confirms, §13 Q14); one credit note per refund. Plan-funded orders get no invoice of their own.

**Analytics (requirement on spec 023 when written).** One server-side `purchase` per plan at `subscription.paid`, value = `total_minor`, `item_category = 'plan'`; no `purchase` for a plan-funded order.

**Jobs.** `subscription.materialise`: hourly; selects `scheduled` deliveries of `active` plans with `scheduled_date ≤ (today in the destination zone) + lead`, locks with `SKIP LOCKED`, re-checks `isStandardDate`, reschedules or materialises. `subscription.complete`: daily; an `active` plan with no `scheduled` delivery whose last delivery's order is delivered or later, or cancelled, moves to `completed`. `subscription.expire_unpaid`: expires `pending_payment` plans past spec 013's unpaid window and cancels their payment intent.

## 8. Compliance considerations (mandatory)

- **Data flows added → RoPA.** (1) **Plans.** Subjects: buyers and recipients. Categories: buyer name, email, optional phone, country; recipient name, phone, delivery address, place kind, note; card message and signature; schedule. Purpose: deliver a prepaid series of gifts. Basis: buyer Art. 6(1)(b); recipient Art. 6(1)(f) (`plan/07` §1.2), balanced by minimisation and the card notice. Retention: recipient and card data pseudonymised 90 days after the later of the last delivery date and cancellation; unpaid plans 7 days after expiry; the payment and invoice records kept for the accounting period (accountant confirms for the LLC). Processors: Neon, Railway, Cloudflare, Stripe, Resend. (2) **Cancel page submissions.** Plan reference, email, optional reason; basis Art. 6(1)(c) (a statutory cancellation route) and (b); kept with the plan's events, no separate table.
- **Consumer information (CRD Art. 6; UK CCRs 2013 Sch. 2).** The plan pre-contract block, server-rendered directly above the pay button, states: main characteristics (product, size, number of deliveries, every date), the total and the per-delivery price, VAT and delivery included, **the duration (n months) and that it does not renew** (CRD Art. 6(1)(o)), how to cancel and what is refunded, the perishable-goods position, trader identity (Flowers Overseas LLC, ADR-0021) and links to the terms and the cancellation page. S1 repeats it on a durable medium (Art. 8(7)).
- **Withdrawal and cancellation, researched position (lawyer confirms, ⚖️).** EU: flowers are goods "liable to deteriorate or expire rapidly", exempt from the 14-day withdrawal right (CRD Art. 16(d)); for a contract for regular delivery of goods over a defined period the withdrawal period runs from receipt of the **first** good (Art. 9(2)(b)(iii)); whether the perishable exemption also covers deliveries not yet prepared is not settled in the sources found. UK: perishable exemption CCRs reg. 28(1)(c), same regular-delivery rule. **Our contractual right is more generous than either reading:** the buyer may cancel the plan at any time and gets back, in full and at once, every delivery not yet being prepared. If a withdrawal right does exist for unprepared deliveries, this right satisfies it (nothing is withheld), and the cancel page is the withdrawal function CRD Art. 11a requires for online contracts from 19 June 2026 (Directive (EU) 2023/2673: two steps, confirmation on a durable medium), with labels the lawyer confirms. **Germany:** a prepaid 12-month plan of monthly deliveries is a continuing obligation concluded online, so BGB §312k's cancellation button applies to `de` buyers: a footer link "Verträge hier kündigen", a confirmation page without sign-in, the button "jetzt kündigen", and an immediate confirmation on a durable medium; the 12-month maximum sits inside BGB §309 Nr. 9's two-year limit. **UK DMCCA 2024 (subscription regime, contracts from 1 January 2027):** s.254 covers contracts where the consumer automatically incurs further liability (recurring payments, renewals, trials that convert); a single upfront payment for a fixed number of deliveries with no renewal does not appear to meet that test; the one-step cancel page means we would also meet its exit duty if the lawyer reads it otherwise (§13 Q7).
- **Price display (`plan/07` §4).** One total, VAT and delivery included, equal on the plan page, its schema, every checkout step and the charge; the per-delivery price shown beside it; no surcharge can apply; no "save" claim unless a discount is configured, and then only as the per-delivery price with and without the plan discount, never a fake strike-through (Omnibus Art. 6a does not reach a comparison with a different product, but we avoid the pattern). No countdown, no scarcity.
- **VAT (🧾, accountant gate).** VAT on a prepayment for identified goods falls due on receipt of the payment (EU VAT Directive Art. 65; UK VATA 1994 s.6(4) advance-payment tax point), at the rate of each delivery's supply. Each delivery's VAT is computed at purchase from the destination's rate (Poland 8 % for flowers under Model A, `plan/06` §4) and stored; the materialised order copies it and never recomputes it. A rate change between purchase and delivery is the accountant's call (§13 Q14). `subscriptions.open` stays off in production until `docs/compliance/vat-signoff.md` covers prepaid plans sold by the LLC (ADR-0021's gate); the runbook makes it the first flip precondition.
- **Payments (founder ruling 2026-10-14).** One automatic capture; no card is stored, no mandate is created, no charge is ever made off-session; a grep gate finds no `off_session` or `setup_future_usage` in `src/`.
- **Recipient minimisation (`plan/07` §1.3).** No recipient email anywhere; the recipient phone and address are held on the plan only until 90 days after its last delivery; no recipient field on the plan status page, in any event, outbox payload, log or URL.
- **Geo-blocking (EU 2018/302, ADR-0006).** Plan prices depend on the destination only; any buyer country may be chosen; the cancel page works from anywhere.
- **Brand voice.** No buyer-facing string says or implies florist acceptance, offers, routing, "partner", "network" or another florist (`RELAY_PATTERNS`, `BANNED_VOICE_WORDS`); no post-purchase message speaks of refunds or failure except S3 and spec 016's B5 and B6 (founder rule, 2026-10-14).
- **Accessibility (WCAG 2.1 AA).** §5.3; axe zero serious or critical on the plan page, checkout plan states, status, cancel and receipt pages in four locales; keyboard-only purchase up to the pay step and keyboard-only cancellation.
- **Logs and PII.** Logged: `request_id`, `subscription_id`, `delivery_id`, `order_id`, `mode`, `locale`, `country_iso`, job outcomes, error codes. Never: names, email, phone, address, card message, plan token, cancel-form values. Sentry scrubs bodies on plan routes; the logger's redaction list gains `token` (already) and `reason`.

## 13. Open questions

Each has a recommended default; "049 defaults" accepts all of them.

| # | Question | Recommended default |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Prepaid plans only, or also recurring monthly billing? | Prepaid only (3, 6, 12 deliveries, one charge, no renewal). Recurring billing needs a stored card and off-session charges (against the 2026-10-14 ruling) and the UK DMCCA regime from 2027; revisit only on a new founder decision |
| Q2 | What is delivered each month? | One "seasonal bouquet" plan product per country, a different mix each month in the chosen size; not any product of the buyer's choice (keeps the florist's work and our photos simple) |
| Q3 | Discount for longer plans? | 0 % at launch for every length (data in `plans.ts`); competitors discount 12-month plans, which we can add later as a per-length percentage without code |
| Q4 | Plan lengths | 3, 6 and 12 monthly deliveries |
| Q5 | Cadence | Monthly only; no weekly or fortnightly |
| Q6 | Card message | One message, printed on every delivery; no monthly editing in v1 |
| Q7 | Cancellation terms and legal reading | Cancel any time; full refund of every delivery not yet being prepared (until 06:00 destination time on its date); the lawyer confirms the Art. 16(d) reading, the Art. 11a and §312k labels, and that a prepaid plan sits outside DMCCA s.254 |
| Q8 | Skip, pause, address change by the buyer | Not in v1; the buyer asks us and the founder moves a date in admin; an address change is a cancel and a new plan |
| Q9 | Earliest first delivery | 2 days after today in the destination's time zone |
| Q10 | When each delivery becomes an order | 7 days before its date |
| Q11 | Print "Delivery 1 of 6" on the recipient's card? | No; the line appears in the buyer's emails only |
| Q12 | A separate "last delivery" email? | No; B4 for the last delivery says "Delivery 6 of 6", which is enough |
| Q13 | URL slugs | `en`/`en-gb` `flower-subscription`, `de` `blumen-abo`, `pl` `subskrypcja-kwiatow`; native review before first indexing |
| Q14 | VAT on prepaid plans | VAT accounted at payment for every delivery at the rate known then, one invoice per plan; the accountant confirms for the LLC (ADR-0021) before the production flag flips; a later rate change is the accountant's call |
| Q15 | Name in copy | "Monthly flowers" as the product name, "flower subscription" in titles for search, and "Paid once. No automatic renewal." beside every price |
| Q16 | The 14-day gap rule when a date must move | Never move a delivery to within 14 days of the next one; alert the founder instead |
