## 1. Problem

Phase 0 exists to sign the first three Warsaw florists (`plan/09` Phase 0, exit signal 9). Today a florist who hears about us has nowhere to go: the header and footer entry "For florists" is `published: false` in `src/config/site-links.ts` (owning spec 011), so TASK-173 hides it; there is no page that says what we do and what they do, what they receive, or how an order reaches them; there is no way to apply; and the demo the founder plans to show on calls, "a mock order lands in a florist's inbox and they accept it and upload the photo on their phone" (`plan/09` Phase 0 AC 4; `plan/10` §6), has no screen behind it. `plan/09` reserves `011` for exactly this set: the for-florists landing, the application form, the walkthrough and the mock inbox at `/demo/vendor-inbox`, with AC 5 requiring the landing in `en`, `pl` and `de` and the form writing to `partner_application`.

The founder also asked, on 2026-10-04, for the whole backend to be made ready for production, the florist portal included. This spec does not build the portal; that stays `026` (florist sign-in and organisation scoping in production) and `027` (the portal's full feature set), as `plan/09` Phase 2 schedules. What it does is build the Phase 0 inbox **on the portal's foundations** so that 026 and 027 extend it instead of rewriting it: a real Auth.js email-link sign-in on the sign-in core shared with admin (spec 012 task 1), organisation scoping by `partner_member` enforced by Postgres row-level security, every order status change through spec 010's `orderService.transition` (ADR-0009), and delivery photos through the R2 storage seam (TASK-017, TASK-082). The demo and the portal then differ in where they are mounted, which partners may sign in, and which features are switched on, not in how they work. One consequence needs saying plainly: the public half of this spec ships today with no database, but the inbox half cannot run until the database chain (TASK-017 → TASK-031) has landed and the R2 implementation (TASK-082) is unblocked (§12).

**The application form (`plan/05` #42; writes `partner_application`)**

- A plain `<form method="post">` handled by a Next server action, working with JavaScript disabled. Fields: name of the shop; contact name; email; phone or WhatsApp; country (the seeded destinations from `countries.ts`, in `collator(locale)` order); town or city; where you deliver (free text: districts, postcodes or a radius); orders a day you could take (integer 1–500); weekend delivery (yes / sometimes / no); registration number (optional); Instagram or website (optional, one URL); how we should contact you (email / WhatsApp / phone); language (en / pl / de, prefilled from the page locale). No upload field (§13 Q5) and no bank or payout field.
- Server-side validation by one zod schema (`FloristApplicationInputSchema`, `.strict()`). An invalid submission re-renders the form with an error summary that takes focus and links to each field, a message under each field, and every answer preserved.
- **Abuse controls, none of which reads an IP address or a user agent** (spec 004 §8's rule for every form): a honeypot field (a non-empty value answers as success and writes nothing); a per-instance fixed-window limit (`createRateLimiter`, `src/lib/csp-report.ts`; per instance is global on Railway's single replica, ADR-0018); a bounded body read (`readBoundedBody`, 64 KB). An edge rate-limit rule per client at Cloudflare is recommended in the runbook as a founder action, not built here.
- **The write** goes through an `ApplicationSink` seam (the `ReminderSink` precedent in `src/lib/reminders.ts`). `dbApplicationSink` inserts one `partner_application` row under `withDbContext({ role: "public" })`, with no `RETURNING` (the public role has no `SELECT`). Success answers with a redirect to the sent page, whose URL carries no query string.
- **The notice at collection** (GDPR Art. 13) sits beside the form: the controller line `company.controller` (spec 041 §2), purpose, legal basis, retention, recipients by category, rights, and how to use them (phone or WhatsApp). A link to the privacy notice replaces the controller line where `infoPageExists("privacy", locale)` is true. `privacy_notice_version` is stored on the row.
- **The founder alert:** each stored application sends one email to the address in `FLORIST_APPLICATIONS_NOTIFY_EMAIL` through the shared core's `Mailer` seam (below), carrying the application's fields (§13 Q8). A failed alert never loses the application: the row is already committed, and the failure is a Sentry warning.
- **The form switch:** `src/config/florists.ts` `applicationForm: "off" | "on"`. With `off` the apply page renders a contact-first block (phone and WhatsApp, the support line) and no `<form>`; it ships `off` with the public pages and flips to `on` when the production database has the migration of §5.1. This is the bounded config-file deviation from `CLAUDE.md`'s "data flip in admin" that specs 003, 004, 007, 008 and 041 record, retired by spec 012.
- **Retention:** `partner_application` rows are deleted 12 months after their last change **unless converted** (§13 Q9), by spec 002's `retention.sweep` extended with one policy row. This is the only copy of the rule; spec 012 uses it and keeps none of its own.

**Florist sign-in, on the shared sign-in core (spec 012 task 1; ADR-0015)**

- **One core, built by spec 012's task 1 and consumed here.** The core is Auth.js v5 with the Email provider (magic link) and **database sessions** in the TASK-022 tables, in one place, `src/lib/auth/`. It has one adapter: our own `Adapter`, each method one `withSystemContext("auth", …)` transaction, because `src/lib/db.ts` exports no raw client (spec 002 AC-3). It has one `Mailer` and one env-name set: `AUTH_SECRET`, `RESEND_API_KEY`, `EMAIL_FROM` and `MAILER`. It takes this spec's link design for every surface, and applies the send limit. This spec builds no second adapter, mailer or Auth.js configuration. It adds the **florist instance**, made by the core's instance factory, with its own path, cookie scope and session length (below).
- **Who may sign in** is a policy, not a route check: `signInPolicy(environment)` returns the partner statuses whose members may receive a link, and the florist instance passes it to the core as its allow predicate (with the `partner_member` lookup). Phase 0: `staging`, `preview`, `development`, `test` → `["demo"]`; `production` → `[]`. Spec 026 widens production to `active` (and `onboarding` if it chooses). A request for any other address gets the same "check your email" page and no email (no account enumeration).
- **The link carries no email address** (the core's design for every surface, taken from this spec). The emailed URL is `{FLORIST_BASE_PATH}/sign-in/confirm?t={token}`. A `GET` of it consumes nothing and renders one "Continue" button, so a mail scanner's or a chat app's link preview cannot burn the token. The `POST` resolves the identifier from the hashed token on the server, invokes Auth.js's email callback **in process** (the identifier never travels in a URL, a redirect or an access log), sets the session cookie and redirects to the inbox. Tokens are single-use and expire after one hour.
- **The florist instance:** Auth.js `basePath` `{FLORIST_BASE_PATH}/api/auth`; sign-in pages under `{FLORIST_BASE_PATH}/sign-in`; cookies `__Secure-fo-florist.session-token`, `__Secure-fo-florist.csrf-token` and `__Secure-fo-florist.callback-url`, each with `Path={FLORIST_BASE_PATH}`, `Secure`, `HttpOnly` and `SameSite=Lax`. The CSRF cookie is renamed from Auth.js's default `__Host-` name because a `__Host-` cookie must carry `Path=/`. Sessions last 30 days, rolling. A florist session never authenticates an admin request, and the reverse.
- **Session to database context:** `requireFloristContext()` reads the session, loads the member's `partner_member` rows (and each partner's status) under the system context, and returns a `DbContext` with `role: "partner"`, `userId` and `partnerIds`. Every inbox read runs under that context, so Postgres row-level security (TASK-023) does the scoping. Memberships are read on every request, so removing one ends access on the next request.
- **The `Mailer` seam** is the core's (`src/modules/notifications/mailer.ts`: `resendMailer` in `staging` and `production`, `capturingMailer` with `GET /api/internal/mail-capture` elsewhere). This spec adds two React Email templates under `emails/`, with a plain-text part, localised in the member's language: the florist sign-in email and the application alert. Spec 017 builds the rest of the transactional set on the same seam.

**The florist inbox v0 (`plan/05` #45)**

- **One mount point.** Every florist route derives from `FLORIST_BASE_PATH` in `src/config/florists.ts`: `/demo/vendor-inbox` in Phase 0. Routes: the inbox list, an order at `{base}/orders/{assignmentId}`, the photo step, and the sign-in pages under `{base}/sign-in`. No locale prefix (`plan/02` §4.1). In `production` every `{base}` route answers 404 while `signInPolicy("production")` is empty (§13 Q4).
- **The list** shows the signed-in member's assignments in three groups: waiting for your answer, in progress, delivered. Each item: product name and tier label, add-ons, delivery date, town, what the florist receives, and, for an open offer, the time to answer by, each date and time formatted in the destination's IANA zone. An empty state. A demo notice on every florist page while the member's partner is `demo`.
- **The order page** shows what the florist needs at each moment and nothing else: before acceptance, the product, add-ons, date, town and postcode, and what they receive; after acceptance, also the recipient's full name, phone (a `tel:` link), the address block (`formatAddressBlock`), the delivery note and the card message to print; after the photo, the recipient's contact details disappear again. The view model has **no buyer field at all**.
- **Actions**, each a no-JS form post: accept; decline with a reason from a closed list (we are full that day, we do not deliver there, we cannot get these flowers, we are closed that day, another reason); out for delivery; delivered, with the photo; add the photo (for a delivered order without one). Each action is legal only in the states `order_transition` allows; the page offers only those.
- **The delivery photo:** JPEG, PNG or WebP up to 15 MiB from `<input type="file" accept="image/*" capture="environment">`; decoded, rotated by its orientation, resized to at most 2000 px on the long edge and re-encoded to JPEG with **all** metadata dropped (EXIF, XMP, GPS, ICC beyond sRGB), then written through `src/lib/storage.ts` to `objectKey("delivery_proof", assetId, "original")` in a **private** bucket (`R2_PRIVATE_BUCKET`); a `media_asset` row (`kind = 'delivery_proof'`, `visibility = 'private'`, `exif_stripped = true`, `source = 'partner'`) and a `delivery_proof` row with `public_use_consent = false` (spec 002's trigger, TASK-020, enforces both). The florist sees their photo through a signed URL valid for at most 5 minutes. No public-use question is asked of the florist (§13 Q6).
- **Freshness without a reload:** a small client island on the inbox list calls `router.refresh()` every 5 seconds while the page is visible, and stops after 10 minutes without interaction (a "Refresh" link remains), so an inbox left open on a phone does not keep Neon's compute awake all day (ADR-0015).
- **Language:** the member's `partner_member.locale_code` (`en`, `pl` or `de`); a switch on every florist page updates it. Pages before sign-in take an `hl` query parameter among the three, default `en`. Never `Accept-Language`, never an IP (ADR-0006).

**The order engine: spec 010's (§13 Q1)**

- This spec **builds no engine**. Every status change goes through spec 010's `orderService.transition(orderId, event, actor, payload)` (its task 3), and status-preserving events through its `appendEvent`. The engine raises `ORDER_TRANSITION_ILLEGAL` for an illegal transition and writes nothing; it writes an `outbox` row only when the event has targets (none in Phase 0). It parses actors with spec 010's `ActorSchema`, so no actor here has a hyphen. The former kernel criterion of this spec is spec 010's AC-22.
- **What this spec adds to the engine:** its florist commands (below), and the versioned zod schemas of the events it writes, registered in spec 010's event-schema registry under the `plan/11` §2 catalogue names: `assignment.accepted`, `assignment.declined`, `order.no_partner_available`, `order.captured`, `order.in_production`, `order.out_for_delivery`, `order.delivered` and `order.photo_uploaded`.
- **Florist commands** in `src/modules/orders/` (`respondToOffer`, `markOutForDelivery`, `markDelivered`, `addDeliveryPhoto`): each first reads the assignment under the member's partner context (if row-level security hides it, the answer is 404), then opens a system transaction that locks the order and the assignment with `SELECT … FOR UPDATE`, re-checks the partner, the state and the deadline, updates `order_assignment` (`response`, `responded_at`, `reason`) where it applies, and calls `transition` with `actor = "partner:{partnerId}"` and `payload.by_user_id`. The partner role is read-only on every order table; the second read under lock removes the gap between check and write. A repeated submission finds the state already advanced and answers with the current page, writing no event.
- **What happens after accept is a seam,** `AcceptedOrderEffect`. In Phase 0 the demo effect handles an order whose **`order.mode` is `demo` or `test`**, outside `production`. That covers an order from spec 010's checkout as well as one from `placeDemoOrder`. The effect advances the order with `actor = "system:demo"` through the arrows `plan/11` §2 draws after acceptance (`order.captured`, then `order.in_production`), so the florist sees "being made". For a decline it records the exhausted arrow, `order.no_partner_available`. Spec 013 replaces the effect for `test` orders with its test-mode capture when it lands. In Phase 1, specs 013/015 supply the real effect for `live` orders (capture on acceptance, `plan/06` §2.2) and spec 016 the real re-offer, with no change to the commands. Nothing here reads `source`, which spec 002 keeps for telling seed rows from real ones.

**The demo (staging only)**

- `placeDemoOrder({ partnerId, productId?, tier?, deliveryDate? })` in `src/modules/orders/demo/`, refusing in `production`, is a **thin wrapper over spec 010's engine**. It builds a synthetic customer and recipient from shared fixtures (no real person) and calls `orderService.place` with `mode = 'demo'` and lines priced through spec 005's price provider, in integer minor units. It then calls `demoRoute(orderId, { partnerId })`, which authorises and routes the order and opens the `order_assignment` to the chosen demo partner with a 60-minute answer window (`plan/06` §5.2). It writes no order, event or assignment row itself. What the florist receives is read from the partner's seed `partner_catalog_mapping`, the same way for an order from spec 010's checkout. Spec 012's admin queue reads the same rows.
- A "Send a test order" control on the inbox list, shown only to members of a `demo` partner outside `production`.
- `pnpm florists:demo-member add|remove --email <e> --partner <code>`: links an email to a seeded demo partner (creating the `users` row) or removes the membership, the member's sessions, and the `users` row if no other membership remains. Refuses in `production`. No personal data is seeded.

## 3. Non-goals

- **The Phase 2 portal's features.** Named here so nobody builds them in this spec: production sign-in for `active` partners, owner/staff permissions, staff invites and a partner switcher (**026**); capacity and blackout calendar, substitution notes, the printed card PDF with QR, payout history and statements, ratings, coverage editing, product availability toggles, photo quality hints (**027**). WhatsApp templated notifications (**028**), the recipient's QR landing and public-use consent for photos (**029**), Wise payouts and statements (**030**).
- **Order notification emails and WhatsApp messages** (the order card with its link, reminders at +30 minutes, the photo reminder: `plan/11` §5.2 T7–T9) → **017**, which must sign the florist in and open the order in one tap (§12). In Phase 0 the founder shows the inbox directly.
- **The order engine** (`place`, `transition`, `appendEvent`, the event-schema registry, the outbox) → **010**. **The sign-in core** (Auth.js configuration, adapter, `Mailer`, the confirm step, the send limit) → **012** task 1.
- **Real routing, deadlines that expire on their own, re-offers to the next florist** → **016**. In v0 an offer past its time is shown as expired and cannot be accepted; nothing moves it.
- **Payments and capture on acceptance** → **013/015**. The demo effect advances demo and test orders (`order.mode`) only.
- **The checkout that places the mock order** → **010**; `placeDemoOrder` here is a thin wrapper over its `place` and `demoRoute`. **The admin queue and the applications list** → **012**; this spec ships the queries (`listFloristApplications`, `setFloristApplicationStatus`) and no admin screen.
- **Separate per-order links** (`/v/{token}`, `plan/06` §5.3; `plan/05` #59): replaced by the sign-in link if §13 Q2 is accepted; otherwise they become a spec 017 item and this spec is amended.
- **Photo upload in the application form** (§13 Q5), **an emailed copy to the applicant** (§13 Q12), **a CAPTCHA or Turnstile** (a third-party script, a processor and a CSP change for a form with no spam yet; the runbook says when to revisit).
- **The florist terms page** (`plan/05` #44) → Phase 1, with the lawyer (`plan/13` B3). **"Our florists" and florist profiles** (#26, #27) → Phase 1/2, only for florists who opt in.
- **A payout calculator** ("3 orders a week × …", `plan/10` §6): a number that reads as a promise of volume.
- **Changes to the corridor pages** (spec 007): links run from the landing to the guides, not back.

## 4. User stories

- As a **florist in Warsaw** who has heard about us, I read in Polish what Flowers Overseas does and what I would do, what I would receive for an example bouquet, when I would be paid and what I am never asked for, and I can phone a person before filling anything in.
- As that florist, I apply in a few minutes on my phone without giving bank details, and I am told plainly what happens next and what is done with my details.

- **Accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA).** Every field has a visible label, a help text tied by `aria-describedby`, the right `autocomplete` token (`organization`, `name`, `email`, `tel`, `address-level2`, `url`) and an error tied the same way; the error summary takes focus and links to each field; targets are at least 44 px; the honeypot is hidden from assistive technology and the tab order; the file field has a label and a text alternative for "take a photo"; the inert walkthrough screens are not focusable and their captions say what they show; states are never conveyed by colour alone. The one time limit, the sign-in link's hour, is a security exception (WCAG 2.2.1), and a new link is one tap away.
- **Logs and PII.** The logger's redaction list gains `contact_email`, `contact_phone`, `email`, `identifier`, `token`, `t`, `card_message`, `address_lines` (`*name` already covers `business_name` and `contact_name`). Application and sign-in logs are counters (`florist_application_received: 1`), never values. No email address in any URL (AC-19). Florist routes never log their query string. Signed URLs are never logged. Sentry's `beforeSend` scrubs request bodies on florist and form routes.
- **Security.** Server-action origin checks and Auth.js's CSRF token; constant-time token comparison inside Auth.js; single-use, one-hour links; database sessions revocable at once; the partner role read-only on order tables, writes only inside system transactions that re-check ownership under row locks; uploads typed by magic bytes, size-bounded while streaming, re-encoded; the private bucket has no public route.
- **`CLAUDE.md` non-negotiable rules, checked:** indexable pages server-rendered (§5.4) · locale in the URL, no IP redirects (§7) · status only through spec 010's `orderService.transition`, never a direct write (§5.2, AC-31) · no literal strings, no physical CSS, `Intl` for formatting (§7) · zod at every boundary (§5.2) · a versioned migration with its rollback (§5.1) · money in integer minor units with a currency (§5.2, `fo/no-float-money`) · price shown is price charged (the example's retail line) · a florist's go-live is a data flip (`fulfillment_partner.status`) once 026 opens production sign-in · no PII in logs, URLs or analytics (above) · secrets only in env (§12) · design artboards before `/plan-tasks` (Appendix B). **Two deviations, both named:** the application-form switch is a config flag until spec 012 (the recorded precedent), and §13 Q2 replaces `plan/06` §5.3's per-order links, which needs the ADR named in the header.

## 13. Open questions

Each has a recommended default, which this spec is written against; the founder's answer replaces the default.

1. **Does this spec build its own order engine?** Every change to an order must go through one function, `orderService.transition`. Spec 010's checkout is the first code to create an order, so it builds that function. *Default:* **no, use spec 010's engine.** This spec adds its florist actions and the record of each one to that engine, and builds no second one. Spec 015 later adds payments and notifications to the same code. The alternative is a separate engine here, which leaves two versions of the one rule that matters most.
2. **One way in for florists.** The plan has two mechanisms: separate links for each order with no account (`plan/06` §5.3), and accounts for the portal in Phase 2. *Default:* **yes to one sign-in, on one condition: spec 017's order emails sign the florist in and open the order in one tap.** The florist gets an email link that signs them in, the same sign-in the portal will use and the same core admin uses. Phase 2 then needs no second system. The condition is recorded as a requirement on spec 017 (§12), and the ADR named in the header is written before `/plan-tasks`.
3. **Email in Phase 0.** Sign-in links and application alerts need an email provider now, earlier than spec 017. *Default:* **yes, set up Resend now; admin needs it too.** Open a Resend account in the EU region, verify a sending subdomain (for example `mail.flowersoverseas.com`) on Cloudflare, accept its DPA, and send from an address you choose on that subdomain. It is set up once, for the sign-in core that admin and florists share.
4. **Where the demo inbox runs.** *Default:* only on staging, behind the staging password, and the live site answers "not found" for it. Demo orders placed on the live site show only in admin. A florist on a call opens the staging link on their phone, types the staging password, then signs in by email.
5. **Photos in the application form.** Letting anyone on the internet upload files is a risk to manage (storage, harmful content). *Default:* no upload. Ask for an Instagram or website link, and exchange photos after the first call.
6. **"May we show this photo publicly?"** The flow artboard asks the florist this. Only the recipient can agree to a photo of their door or of themselves being shown (`plan/07` §1.2). *Default:* drop the question. Photos stay private, and public use waits for spec 029's recipient consent.
7. **The payout example on the landing.** *Default:* one example, a Polish bouquet from our catalogue, showing "the buyer pays X (VAT and delivery included)" and "you receive 149 zł, agreed before you start". It is the same Polish example in all four languages, with no lines for VAT, fees or our share. Confirm the product and the 149 zł **only if you would really pay that figure**: a florist reads it as an offer. They are the seed figures, which `plan/13` B13 says the first florists will test. If you would not pay it, give the figure you would.
8. **New application alerts.** *Default:* each application is emailed to you in full, to an address you name, and stored in the database. Spec 012's admin lists them later.
9. **How long we keep applications.** *Default:* 12 months after our last contact, then deleted, unless the florist starts working with us.
10. **Polish and German copy.** *Default:* a native Polish reviewer checks the Polish landing, walkthrough, form and inbox before the first Warsaw demo call. The Polish slugs are confirmed in the same pass, before Google sees them. German stays a draft (shown with the "not yet checked" line and kept out of Google) until a German florist is in view.
11. **The word "partner" on the florist pages.** People search for "become a partner florist" and "Partner werden", but our voice rule bans "partner" from all copy. *Default:* keep the ban. The pages say "work with us" and "join our florists" and target "florists wanted" and "zlecenia dla kwiaciarni". The alternative is a narrow exception for these pages only.
12. **A copy of the application to the applicant by email.** Anyone could type someone else's address into the form, so this would let strangers make us email that person. *Default:* no copy. The confirmation page says what happens next, and you reply personally.
13. **The application fields.** *Default:* shop name, your name, email, phone or WhatsApp, country, town, where you deliver, orders a day, weekend delivery, registration number (optional), Instagram or website (optional), how to contact you, and language. No bank details, no ID, no photos.
