## 1. Problem

On 2026-10-03 the founder clicked through the live site and found the footer dead: "How it works", "The guarantee", "Help and contact", "Imprint", "Terms", "Privacy", "Cookies" and "Withdrawal and refunds" are all text, because the pages behind them do not exist. `src/config/site-links.ts` carries every one of them as `published: false`, and TASK-173 now hides an unpublished target instead of printing it as text, so until this spec ships the footer simply loses those items. Spec 007 §13 Q6 (resolved 2026-09-15) took these pages out of the corridor spec and said they "need their own spec"; this is it. The pages are `plan/05` #25, #28, #29, #32, #33, #34 and the legal set #46–#51 and #54.

They matter more than their traffic. `plan/04` §12 places the guarantee, the company identity and the substitution policy on every surface of the funnel; `plan/07` §2 and §6 make the trader's identity, the withdrawal position, the complaints path and the cookie policy pre-contractual duties; and `plan/13` C1 (2026-09-14) recorded the founder's own evidence that trust assets — a guarantee, a phone line, a real company — carry the incumbents more than search does. A visitor deciding whether to trust a new name with money looks for exactly these pages. Three facts shape everything below: **we are not taking orders yet** (no florist has signed, no payment method is on), **the company does not exist yet** (the OÜ is due within days, `plan/09`; the imprint, the terms and the privacy notice wait for it, §13 Q1), and **no lawyer has reviewed a word** (`plan/13` B4). So every page has to be useful now and true now, and every legal text has to say plainly that it is a draft.

- **Three merges, so no page is thin.** Contact (#34) is the first block of the help page, because the footer link is already "Help and contact" and a contact page in Phase 0 holds three lines. Company information (#54) is the imprint, because both are the same identity block. Delivery information (#29) and the payment-methods information `plan/07` §2.1 requires become one "Delivery and payment" page, because in Phase 0 each alone is a paragraph. The substitution policy (#51) is a section of the guarantee and has no URL of its own; nothing was ever published at `/legal/substitution`, so no 301 is needed (§3).
- **Three pages wait for the company (§13 Q1, resolved 2026-10-04).** `imprint`, `terms` and `privacy` carry `requiresRegistration: true` in the page registry, and `infoPageExists()` is false for them in every locale while `isCompanyRegistered()` is false. So until then they have no route (404), no sitemap entry, no footer item and no in-body link. Their files are written and approved in task 2 against a `registered: true` fixture. When `company.ts` gains the five registry fields and `supportEmail` and `registered` flips, all three switch on with the company's details, with no other edit. `cookies` and `cancellation` are not gated: neither needs the trader's postal address, and the cookie notice carries the controller identity the site needs today (below).
- **Top-level segments** for the six top-level pages (the five trust pages and the imprint) join `PATH_SEGMENT_KEYS` in `src/config/locales.data.ts` (`howItWorks`, `guarantee`, `help`, `about`, `delivery`, `imprint`), authored for every locale at once, as that file requires. The four legal documents are **leaves** under the existing `legal` segment; their per-locale leaf slugs are data in the page registry below, not segments. The Polish legal segment changes from `regulamin` to `informacje-prawne` (§13 Q5): `regulamin` means "terms", so the shipped value would make the Polish terms URL `/pl/regulamin/regulamin`. No URL under it has ever been published, so the rename costs nothing today and a redirect forever later.
- **No other URL shape exists.** `/{locale}/legal` (bare), `/{locale}/contact`, `/{locale}/legal/company`, `/{locale}/legal/substitution`, any casing variant and any unknown leaf answer 404 from the router (`dynamicParams = false`); a trailing slash answers 308 to the bare URL, as on the corridor (spec 007 §14 A6) and listing pages.

**The content model**

- **`content/pages/{locale}/{pageId}.md`** for the five trust pages and **`content/legal/{locale}/{docId}.md`** for the five legal pages: markdown body plus zod-parsed YAML frontmatter, the corridor model of spec 007 §2 applied again, for the same reason — this is prose a human edits and diffs, and `content/` is where `plan/03` §5 puts long-form copy.
  - Trust frontmatter: `seoTitle` (≤60 characters), `seoDescription` (≤155), `h1`, `intro`, `sections[]` (`{ id, heading, body }`), `faq[]` (`{ q, a }`, help page only, 10–14 items), `links[]` (the in-body link ids of §6), `honestyAllow[]` (below), `source` (`human` | `machine`), `reviewed`, `reviewedBy`, `reviewedAt`, `updatedAt`, and optional `extends: en` for an `en-gb` file that differs in nothing.
  - Legal frontmatter adds: `version` (semver string — the value spec 010 will write into `order.terms_version` for the `terms` document), `effectiveFrom` (ISO date or `null`), `inForce` (bool), `lawyerReviewed` (bool), `lawyerReviewedAt`, `regime` (`eu` | `uk`), `changeLog[]` (`{ version, date, summary }`, one sentence each). The body is the document; its `##` headings are the sections and become the generated table of contents.
- **`en-gb` legal variants are their own files** for `terms`, `privacy` and `cancellation`, because the UK regime differs (`plan/07` §2.2: CCRs 2013, CRA 2015, UK GDPR and the ICO); `cookies` and `imprint` may `extends: en`. A trust page in `en-gb` may extend `en` unchanged: `/en/` and `/en-gb/` are hreflang alternates, and these pages carry no price, currency or law that differs.
- **`src/config/info-pages.ts`** is the page registry: for each of the ten ids, its family, its segment key or legal leaf slug per locale, its site-link id, its schema types, its in-body link set and `requiresRegistration` (true for `imprint`, `terms`, `privacy`). No database is read and none may be (`pnpm check:no-db`).
- **`src/modules/content/`** (new module, §13 Q7) owns the provider seam (`staticInfoContentProvider` reading the committed files through a generated typed index, the corridor precedent: no `fs` in the bundle), the one existence predicate `infoPageExists(pageId, locale)` (a parsed content file exists for that page and locale, and, for a page with `requiresRegistration`, `isCompanyRegistered()` is true), `listInfoPages()`, and `infoPageView()` — the single view model the page, its JSON-LD, its metadata and the sitemap all read. A later `dbInfoContentProvider` (spec 012's admin) replaces the static one with no call-site change.

**The honesty gate (spec 007 §2, AC-19's forbidden-claim scan, applied to a new corpus)**

- **`pnpm pages:check`** (new CI job `pages-check`, `needs: typecheck`) fails, one line per problem naming file, field and rule, on: a file that does not parse; `source: machine` in an `en` or `en-gb` file; `reviewed: true` without `reviewedBy` and `reviewedAt`; a title or description over its limit; an FAQ outside 10–14 items; any of the nine banned words of `src/config/voice.ts` in any prose (both families); a digit-bearing claim of scale or a price literal in any prose (both families); and, **in trust pages only**, any of the claim patterns spec 007 forbids in a guide — delivery timing, cutoff, delivery date, florist count, rating, review, delivery photo — unless the exact sentence is listed in the file's `honestyAllow[]` with a one-line reason. An `honestyAllow` entry that no longer matches any sentence fails too, so the list cannot rot into a blanket licence. Legal documents are exempt from the claim patterns because a contract has to name delivery dates, cancellation periods and refunds; they keep the word, scale and price rules.
- **One list, two readers.** The claim patterns, the scale pattern and the price-literal pattern move out of `scripts/corridor-check.ts` into a shared module (`src/config/honesty.ts`, beside `voice.ts`, the precedent that file's header records) and both gates import it. `corridor:check`'s behaviour and its fixtures are unchanged, asserted by its existing cases.
- **The message keys these pages add are scanned the same way**: the existing banned-word test already covers every `messages/*.json` value; this spec adds the claim patterns over the new `infoPages.*` namespace except `infoPages.legal.*`, which follows the legal family's rules.

**The honest states (rendered from data, never from a template edit)**

- **Not open yet.** While no destination is operational (`anyDeliveryDatesOpen()` is `false`, the predicate spec 004 §14 A19 already uses), every trust page renders the shared sentence `infoPages.status.notYetOpen` directly under its `<h1>`. It disappears when the first country is operational, with no template change. The trust copy is written in the future tense where it describes a florist's work (decision 2026-10-03, TASK-166); the present-tense rewrite is a copy batch at go-live (§12).
- **Draft legal text.** A legal document with `inForce: false` renders `infoPages.legal.notInForce` above its first heading; one with `inForce: true` and `lawyerReviewed: false` renders `infoPages.legal.unreviewed`. The imprint renders neither, because it is a statement of fact, not a draft, and a mandatory disclosure labelled "draft" invites the reading that it is incomplete. Which line applies to which document in Phase 0: terms and cancellation are **not in force** (we sell nothing); privacy (from the day it exists, §13 Q1) and cookies **are in force** (they describe what the site does today: the consent record, the cookies, the hosting logs) and carry the unreviewed line. That is the "where the law allows" rule: a notice the law requires now is never labelled as not applying.
- **Untranslated draft.** A `de` or `pl` file with `reviewed: false` renders `infoPages.status.untranslated` under the `<h1>` ("not yet checked; the English version prevails").
- **Unregistered company (§13 Q1, resolved).** The about page's company block, the cookie notice's "who is responsible" section and the consent banner read `src/config/company.ts`. Before the OÜ exists, the about page renders the representative's name and the sentence `infoPages.company.notRegistered`. The cookie notice and the banner render the controller line `company.controller` (below). No postal address of any kind is rendered before registration: the founder publishes none. The imprint and the privacy notice do not exist until `registered` flips. From then on, they render the five registry fields, the representative, the phone and the support email. Never the canvas's bracketed slots, never an empty `<dd>` (spec 004 AC-9's rule, extended to these pages).
- **The consent banner before the privacy notice exists (§13 Q1 consequence, §13 Q8).** The banner has no privacy link today, and none can point anywhere until registration. While `infoPageExists("privacy", locale)` is false, the banner's body ends with one sentence, `company.controller`: it names the founder (`representative.name`) as the person who runs the site and gives the phone/WhatsApp number (`phoneDisplay`) for questions about cookie choices and personal data. This is the controller identity and contact that GDPR Art. 13(1)(a) asks for, and it needs no postal address. Once the privacy page exists in that locale, the sentence is replaced by `consent.privacyLink`, a link to it. The island gains one projected string and no logic. The `consentView()` projection decides which of the two applies, on the server.

**Page-specific behaviour**

- **Help and contact.** The contact block comes first: phone and WhatsApp from `company.ts` (`tel:` and `wa.me` from the E.164 value), the support line from `company.support.hours` ("Message us any time, 24/7 — we reply within a few hours.", approved by the founder on 2026-10-04, §13 Q2), and one line saying personal-data requests come to the same number. The privacy notice is linked from that line only where it exists. **No email address until the Google Workspace mailbox exists (§13 Q2).** `supportEmail` is optional while `registered` is false, and the help page, the footer and (later) the imprint render it only when it is present. No contact form (§3). Then 10–14 questions as visible, server-rendered headings and paragraphs — no accordion, no JavaScript.
- **Delivery and payment.** A table of the seven destinations from `countries.ts` in `collator(locale)` order, each with its state from `corridorState()` (never from `countries.ts`'s `status`, which says `live` for Poland as a design label — spec 007 §2) and a link to its guide where the guide page exists. An order-by column renders only for a country whose `operations` block is complete, which is none in Phase 0. The payment section lists only the methods `payment-methods.ts` marks `available`, which is none in Phase 0, and otherwise renders the sentence that we are not taking payments yet.
- **Cookies.** The register table rendered from `src/config/cookies.ts` (name, category, purpose, lifetime, whether it is set today or declared for later), the consent policy version from `CONSENT_POLICY_VERSION`, and a "Change cookie settings" button carrying `data-fo-consent-reopen`, which the existing consent island already binds through its delegated handler. No new island and no new client JavaScript. **Until the privacy notice exists (§13 Q1, Q8)**, the cookie notice also carries two short sections. "Who is responsible" holds the `company.controller` line. "What else this website records" covers hosting and edge logs (RoPA row 2), server-side error monitoring (row 1) and the consent records (row 3). For each record it gives what GDPR Art. 13(1)(c), (e) and (f) and 13(2)(a), (b) and (d) ask for. **Purpose and legal basis:** error monitoring, to detect and fix errors (legitimate interest, Art. 6(1)(f), row 1); hosting and edge logs, to serve the site and keep it secure (legitimate interest, Art. 6(1)(f), row 2); consent records, to be able to show what was chosen (legal obligation, Art. 6(1)(c) with Art. 7(1), row 3). **Who handles it:** Railway and Cloudflare (hosting and edge, as ADR-0018 left row 2), Vercel while it remains the cold fallback, and Sentry (error monitoring, EU region). **Transfers:** row 2's hosting and edge logs may be transferred to the United States, under the providers' data-processing terms (the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and/or the European Commission's standard contractual clauses, as row 2 records); rows 1 and 3 leave the EU for no one. **Retention:** as rows 1–3 record. **Rights:** access, erasure and objection, used by phone or WhatsApp. **Complaints:** to "the data protection authority where you live or work", with the Estonian authority (AKI) and, for UK visitors, the ICO given as examples. AKI is not named as *the* authority, because until the OÜ is established in Estonia the controller is a private individual. AC-25's processor-coverage test covers this section as well as the privacy files, so the interim notice cannot drift from the record. It is the minimum notice for what the site processes today, and it is replaced by a link to the privacy notice when that page exists.
- **Privacy.** Exists only once `registered` flips (§13 Q1). Built on `docs/compliance/ropa.md`: the processing the site does today (rows 1–5, with the hosting row as ADR-0018 left it — Railway behind Cloudflare, Vercel as cold fallback) in one section, the processing that starts with the first order (`plan/07` §1.2) in a second section headed as future, the recipient section `plan/07` §1.6 requires, rights, and the complaint bodies (Estonian AKI; the ICO in the `en-gb` variant).
- **Guarantee (§13 Q4, resolved 2026-10-04).** The freshness term is the **fresh-flower promise**: if the flowers don't arrive fresh and in good condition, the buyer sends a photo within 72 hours of delivery and we replace them or refund in full. This is the market standard (Bloom & Wild's terms, 5.2: a photo within 72 hours). It promises the condition on arrival, not how long the flowers last afterwards, which depends on the room, the water and the care. **No number of days of freshness appears anywhere**, on this page, in the three shipped strings that say "7-day" on every page, or on the product page (spec 009 §14 A11). A missed day means a full refund. Cancellation is free until the florist accepts. The page says the promise comes on top of the buyer's legal rights, not instead of them (Directive 2019/771 Art. 17(2); UK CRA 2015 s.30). Substitutions are "same or higher value" in every place they are described (A.2, A.3, A.4 Q5).

**Indexability, schema, sitemaps, links**

- **Ten new `SeoPageType`s registered as `byRule`** in `src/modules/seo/indexability.ts`, with no robots branch outside `modules/seo`. ## 3. Non-goals

- **A contact form, a callback request, a chat widget or a waiting-list field.** Each is a new personal-data flow with a lawful basis, a mailbox to staff and a RoPA row. Phone and WhatsApp need none of them, and neither will an email address (spec 007 §13 Q4's reasoning, applied again).
- **A support mailbox, and any postal address before the OÜ exists (§13 Q1, Q2).** The mailbox comes with Google Workspace. Its address then joins `company.ts` as `supportEmail`, a data edit that renders it everywhere at once. Google as a mail processor is a RoPA row and a DPA owned by the change that adds it, not by this spec.
- **Our florists page (#26), florist profiles (#27), reviews (#30), real deliveries gallery (#31), review policy (#53), accessibility statement (#52), partner terms (#44).** Each needs data we do not have or belongs to a later phase; they stay where `plan/05` puts them. The accessibility statement is Phase 1 and the next legal page to write; the review policy waits for the first review.
- **The legal text a lawyer approves.** This spec ships drafts and the machinery to publish a reviewed version; the review itself is `plan/13` B4, and its timing is §13 Q3.
- **Superseded legal versions at their own URLs.** Phase 0 publishes `0.x` drafts that no contract was ever formed under. Archiving a version that governed a real order starts with the first order (spec 010/013 write `order.terms_version`), and the URL scheme for it is decided then.
- **The German model withdrawal form (Muster-Widerrufsbelehrung) for non-perishable add-ons.** `plan/07` §11 item 3 is a lawyer question; the cancellation draft states the add-on position as open.
- **Any change to the checkout, the product page's own withdrawal sentence or the order confirmation email.** Specs 009, 010 and 017 link to these pages; they do not move here.
- **A redirect for `/legal/substitution`.** Nothing was ever published there, so it 404s like any other unknown leaf.
- **Database tables for page or legal-document content.** Spec 002 defines none; the static provider is the seam spec 012's admin replaces.

## 4. User stories

- As a **buyer in Manchester about to send flowers to my mother in Kraków**, I can find out who I am paying, what happens if the flowers are late or wrong, and how to reach a person, before I hand over my card.
- As a **buyer who wants to cancel**, I can read in one place that cancelling is free until the florist accepts, why the usual 14 days do not apply to fresh flowers, and how a refund reaches me.
- As a **German visitor**, once the company is registered I find an Impressum in the footer of every page with the company's registered facts. Before then, no page pretends to be one or prints placeholders.
- As a **visitor who accepted analytics cookies last week**, I can open the cookie page, see every cookie the site sets or plans to set, and change my answer.
- As **Googlebot**, I find complete server-rendered pages, an `FAQPage` that matches the visible questions word for word, no structured data claiming a business we are not, and legal drafts kept out of the index until they are final.
- As the **founder**, I approve one batch of English copy, and the next time a lawyer returns a document I bump its version and flip two flags; I do not touch a template.

## 5. Design

### 5.1 Data model changes (tables, columns, migrations + rollback)

**No migration.** Everything is committed content and config, read through provider seams; `pnpm check:no-db` covers every file this spec adds.

| Phase 0 (this spec) | Later owner | Contract |
|---|---|---|
| `content/pages/**`, `content/legal/**` + `InfoPageContentSchema`, `LegalDocumentSchema` | spec 012's admin editor (a CMS table it specifies) | the provider interface; `infoPageView()` is the only reader |
| `src/config/info-pages.ts` (registry) | stays config | `i18n:check` asserts every leaf slug is ASCII, lowercase, hyphenated and unique per locale |
| `src/config/company.ts` gains `representative` (`{ name }`, required: the person who runs the site before registration, the OÜ's management-board member after it; Phase 0 value `Sheikh Ahmad Hameed`, §13 Q1) and `supportEmail` (optional while `registered` is `false`, **required** once it is `true`, because the imprint and the trader identity need an email address; §13 Q2). There is **no** `operatorAddress`: no postal address exists in the file until the registered `address` arrives with the other registry fields | spec 002's company/settings data, when one exists | the existing `superRefine` adds `supportEmail` to what `registered: true` requires; `.strict()` rejects any address field outside `address` |
| legal `version` | spec 010/013 write it into `order.terms_version` (spec 002 §5.1) | the `terms` document's `version` is the one value checkout snapshots |

`representative` is the honest answer to "who runs this site before the company exists" (§13 Q1). The schema stores what the founder supplies and never invents a field. **Consequence of the flip's requirements:** `registered: true` needs the five registry fields **and** `supportEmail`. If the OÜ is registered before the Workspace mailbox exists, the flip waits for the mailbox. That is deliberate: an imprint without an email address is incomplete (DDG §5(1) no. 2; CRD Art. 6(1)(c)).

**Rollback** is `git revert` of the task commits plus setting the eight `site-links.ts` rows back to `published: false`. No state, no migration, no external resource. While `isIndexingEnvironment()` is false no URL here is in any index, so a revert orphans nothing.

## 6. SEO considerations (mandatory)

- **Indexability.** Trust pages: `index,follow` iff exists ∧ `reviewed` ∧ `isLocaleIndexable(locale)` ∧ `isIndexingEnvironment()`. ADR-0007 holds: these are true pages that sell nothing.

The `de` imprint is not an alternate of the `en` imprint until then either.
- **URL pattern and localised slugs.** §2's table; segments from `locales.data.ts`, leaves from `info-pages.ts`, all composed by `localePath()`; ASCII, lowercase, hyphenated, unique per locale, asserted by `i18n:check`.
- **Schema.** `BreadcrumbList` (all), `FAQPage` (help, from the visible list; `plan/02` §9 notes FAQ rich results are limited to authoritative sites, so this is for correctness, not for a rich result), `Organization` (about, fields we have). Asserted absent: `LocalBusiness`, `FloristShop`, `Product`, `Offer`, `AggregateRating`, `Review`, `HowTo`. The guarantee page is the `merchantReturnLink` target spec 009's future `Offer` will name (`plan/02` §9); nothing here emits it.
- **Sitemap membership.** `static.xml` per locale, indexable pages only, `lastmod` = the content file's `updatedAt`.
- **Internal links.** In: footer (eight links in every locale where the page exists; five of them before registration, when imprint, terms and privacy do not exist), the home's "Read the guarantee in full" and "All help topics" (`home.howItWorks.guarantee`, `faq.allTopics`, §2), and the product page's guarantee link when spec 009 adds it. Every link is rendered only where its target exists in that locale. Crawl depth from any locale home is ≤3.

## 7. i18n considerations (mandatory)

- **New message keys.** `infoPages.*` — status lines (`notYetOpen`, `untranslated`), legal lines (`notInForce`, `unreviewed`, `version`, `inForceSince`, `notInForceYet`, `appliesTo.eu`, `appliesTo.uk`, `contents`, `changeLog`), company lines (`notRegistered`, the imprint field labels), contact labels, `company.controller` and `consent.privacyLink` (§2, the banner), the cookie-table column headings and the "Change cookie settings" button, the destination-table headings, breadcrumb labels; plus `footer.link.deliveryAndPayment`. ICU, namespaced, typed, zero literals.
- **Two mechanisms, deliberately.** Chrome strings live in `messages/*.json` and count toward spec 003's 5 % unreviewed-share gate. **Long-form page copy lives in `content/` and does not**: roughly 6,000 words of draft legal text in `messages/en.json` would push the `en` unreviewed share past 5 % and make `isLocaleIndexable("en")` false — every English page on the site would drop to `noindex` the day the drafts merged. Content files carry their own `reviewed` flag, which gates only their own page. Asserted by AC-7.
- **Copy approval.** The new `en` keys and the `en`/`en-gb` content files go to the founder as **one batch** (Appendix A), with their exact text, and are marked reviewed only on his answer (decision 2026-10-03, "Copy review, the 5 % gate"). Nobody else marks them reviewed.
- **Locale formatting.** Effective dates through `formatDate` with the month name (`plan/03` §7); hours are prose in `company.support.hours` (a message key, as today); the phone number is data printed as the founder wrote it; country names from `destinations.*.name`; the destination table sorted by `collator(locale)`; lists via `Intl.ListFormat`.
- **RTL.** No physical CSS, no direction-carrying glyph; the `/ar-XB` pseudo-locale renders the help page in visual tests so the first RTL launch is a translation job.
- **Address and phone formats.** No address renders before registration. After it, the company's registered address renders through `formatAddressBlock` with the address's own country. The phone number is not reformatted. "24/7" in the support line is copy, not a formatted time.
- **Translation review and `noindex`.** `de`/`pl` files are machine drafts (`source: machine`, `reviewed: false`), queued for the `plan/13` B12 native reviewers; legal documents in `de`/`pl` additionally need the lawyer's sign-off on the German and Polish wording before they can be indexed or put in force. Glossary entries (`content/i18n/glossary.{locale}.md`) for "guarantee", "withdrawal", "imprint", "our florist" land with task 3.

## 8. Compliance considerations (mandatory)

- **Data flows → RoPA.** **None added.** No form, no input, no cookie, no processor; `ropa.md` gains a "spec 041: no new processing" note. One RoPA correction is a precondition of publishing the privacy notice: row 2 still describes Vercel as the host, and since ADR-0018 production runs on Railway behind Cloudflare. A test asserts that every processor named in an active RoPA row appears in the `en` and `en-gb` privacy files, so the notice cannot be truer — or less true — than the record (AC-25).
- **Lawful basis and consent.** Nothing on these pages needs consent to render. The cookie page re-opens the existing banner; it records nothing itself.
- **Trader identity (`plan/07` §6; CRD Art. 6(1)(c); DDG §5 for the German audience).** Once the OÜ is registered, the imprint is reachable from every page in one click, with the company's registered address, email and phone. **Before then there is no imprint (§13 Q1, the founder's choice of 2026-10-04: no address is published before registration).** The cost is accepted and stated here. DDG §5 asks a commercial website for a postal address, so the German-facing site has no Impressum until registration, which is days away. No order can be placed in that time, so no contract is formed without the CRD's pre-contractual identity. Inventing a registry number or VAT id stays forbidden (`company.ts` refinements).
- **Controller identity now (GDPR Art. 13(1)(a)).** The site already processes personal data: consent records (RoPA row 3), hosting and edge logs (row 2) and server-side error events (row 1). Until the privacy notice exists, the consent banner and the cookie notice name the founder as controller and give the phone/WhatsApp number. The cookie notice adds the minimum notice for that processing (§2): for each of rows 1–3 its purpose and legal basis (Art. 6(1)(f) for rows 1 and 2, Art. 6(1)(c) for row 3; Art. 13(1)(c)), the processors by name — Railway, Cloudflare, Sentry, and Vercel while it is the cold fallback (Art. 13(1)(e)) — the possible US transfer of row 2's logs and its safeguard, the Data Privacy Framework and/or standard contractual clauses (Art. 13(1)(f)), retention, rights, and the right to complain to "the data protection authority where you live or work", with AKI and the ICO as examples. The authority is not fixed as AKI because, until the OÜ is established in Estonia, the controller is a private individual and has no Estonian establishment. GDPR asks for the controller's identity and contact details, not a postal address. ⚖️ The lawyer confirms this interim notice in the B4 review. §13 Q8 asks the founder whether he prefers to publish the full privacy notice now with his name and number only.
- **Consumer information.** The guarantee, cancellation and delivery pages state the perishable-goods exemption (CRD Art. 16(d); UK CCRs 2013 reg. 28) in plain words, the voluntary free cancellation until the florist accepts (spec 013's manual capture: a held card is released, an immediately charged method is refunded), the substitution rule, and the remedies. The guarantee states that it comes on top of the buyer's legal rights, not instead of them (Directive 2019/771 Art. 17(2); UK CRA 2015 s.30), so the 72-hour photo window cannot read as a limit on those rights (AC-16). They are pre-contractual information pages, not offers: no order button, no contract formed.
- **Draft status (`plan/13` B4).** Every legal document shows its version and status. The terms and the cancellation page say they are not in force; the privacy and cookie notices say a lawyer has not reviewed them yet. None says or implies "reviewed by a lawyer", "compliant" or "GDPR certified".
- **Price display.** No price, no price literal (gate), no currency claim beyond "you pay in the currency shown". The delivery page's payment section names no method that is not `available`, so no processor's mark or name is used before its agreement exists (`payment-methods.ts` header).
- **Reviews and trust (`plan/07` §7; Phase 0 AC 6).** No review, rating, star, testimonial, Trustpilot mark, florist name, florist count, delivery photo or "trusted by" strip on any of the ten pages, asserted over all of them in every locale (spec 004 AC-15's assertion, extended).
- **Geo-blocking and ADR-0006.** Every page is the same for every visitor and reachable from anywhere; `/de/impressum` is linked from every locale's footer via the imprint link in that locale, and never forced on anyone.
- **Accessibility (EAA / WCAG 2.1 AA).** One `<h1>`; headings in order; the FAQ as headings and text, nothing hidden behind a disclosure; the legal table of contents as a labelled `<nav>`; the cookie register as a captioned table with header scope; the identity block as a `<dl>`; 200 % reflow; axe zero serious/critical on all ten pages. The accessibility statement itself is Phase 1 (§3).
- **Logs / PII.** No input is read, so no PII can reach a log. The founder's name is published deliberately, with his consent given in chat on 2026-10-04 (§13 Q1). Before registration it appears on the about page, in the consent banner and in the cookie notice; after it, on the imprint and in the privacy notice too. It is not "logged", and it appears nowhere in schema or analytics. No home or postal address of his appears anywhere.
- **Security.** No new dependency, origin or inline script; ADR-0016's policy is unchanged.
