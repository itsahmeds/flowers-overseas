## 1. Problem

A paid order today goes nowhere. Once spec 013 has secured the buyer's money and spec 015 has recorded it, nothing picks a florist, nothing tells the florist, nothing tells the buyer what happens next, and nobody notices when no florist answers. `plan/09` Phase 1 AC-1 asks for one real order "routed automatically, accepted by a florist via magic link, delivered, photo uploaded, buyer notified … zero manual DB edits", and AC-7 asks for alerts on SLA cascade exhaustion, webhook failure and `paid` without `routed` for more than 5 minutes. Without this spec the product — trust plus routing (`plan/00` §1) — has no routing.

The roadmap reserved two numbers for this: `016` (routing job, assignment SLA, timeouts, no-partner path) and `017` (notifications: Resend set, React Email, WhatsApp procedure, vendor magic links). They are one flow — an offer is a notification, an acceptance is a link click, an exhaustion is an alert — so the founder asked for one spec. Number `017` is folded in here and stays unused; roadmap references to `017` resolve to this file.

## 2. Scope

Each bullet is testable; §9 names the AC.

**Routing (the `orders` module, `plan/06` §5.2, `plan/11` §2)**
- A pg-boss job `order.route` starts when an order crosses spec 013's **paid boundary**: the event that says the buyer's money is secured — `authorised` for manual-capture methods, `paid_unrouted` for immediate-charge methods (`plan/11` §2, §13 Q1).
- It picks one florist with a pure, unit-tested ranking over **coverage** (recipient postcode zone or city), **catalogue** (can fulfil every product line), **blackout**, **capacity** on the delivery date, then **rating**, acceptance rate, payout and round-robin (§5.2).
- Each offer has an **SLA**: 60 minutes from 08:00 to 20:00 destination local time, 30 minutes for same-day orders, and orders paid at night are offered at 07:30 with the SLA ending 09:00 (`plan/06` §5.2).
- A reminder goes out at half the SLA; on decline or timeout the next candidate is offered (the **cascade**), up to 5 offers or the day's routing deadline.
- **No-partner path**: when no candidate is left, the order moves to `no_partner_available`, the founder is alerted, and after a grace window with no manual reroute the order is cancelled — authorisation voided or payment refunded through spec 013 — and the buyer is told by email.
- **Acceptance triggers capture** through spec 013. The recipient's details are shown to the florist only after capture succeeds.

**Florist magic links (`/v/{token}`, `plan/05` row 59, `plan/06` §5.3)**
- One HMAC-signed link per offer, scoped to that order and that florist, revocable, expiring, valid over email and WhatsApp.
- Actions: accept, decline (reason picklist), mark out for delivery, mark delivered, upload the delivery photo from the phone camera. Opening a link (GET) never changes anything; every action is a POST.
- The photo is re-encoded with EXIF/GPS removed, stored private in R2, and recorded as `delivery_proof` (`plan/01` §6, spec 002 AC-19).

**Notifications (the `notifications` module, ADR-0013, `plan/11` §5)**
- An outbox-driven sender: spec 015 writes the outbox row in the transition's transaction; a `notification.deliver` job renders and sends, retries with backoff, and dead-letters with an alert.
- Resend (EU region) behind an `EmailSender` interface, with a `log` transport for local and CI and a sandbox redirect so no non-production environment can email a real person.
- React Email templates for the Phase 1 set, in `en`, `en-gb`, `de` and `pl`, with plain-text parts: order received, florist accepted (contract confirmation), out for delivery, delivered with photo, cancelled — no florist, refund; the florist offer, reminder, withdrawn/cancelled and photo reminder; the founder's alerts and WhatsApp copies.
- The **WhatsApp Business app procedure** for Phase 1: the system writes the florist's WhatsApp message for the founder to paste (manual template), and email always goes in parallel.
- **Resend webhooks**: signature-verified, stored before processing, idempotent, order-independent, with contract tests.

**Alerts (`plan/09` AC-7)**
- Cascade exhausted; webhook processing failure (any provider, through one hook spec 013 also calls); `paid` boundary crossed with no offer after 5 minutes; a notification dead-lettered. Email to the ops address plus a Sentry event, so one failing channel does not silence the other.

- **Substitution proposals and buyer approval** (`plan/11` T3), **delivery failed** (T12) → a later Phase 1 spec (020 or a follow-up, §13 Q7). In Phase 1 a florist reports either by phone or WhatsApp to the founder.

- **Vendor portal** → specs 026–027.

## 4. User stories

- As a **buyer**, I get an email the moment my order is in, another when a florist near the recipient has taken it (and I have been charged), one when it is on its way and one with the photo when it has arrived, so I never wonder where my gift is.
- As a **buyer** whose order no florist could take, I am told promptly that I have not been charged (or have been refunded), so I can still send something else in time.
- As a **florist**, I get a short offer by email (and WhatsApp) with the product, date, district and what I will be paid, and I can accept or decline with one tap on my phone without an account.
- As a **florist who accepted**, I open the same link to see the delivery address, phone and card message, mark the order out for delivery and upload the photo from my camera.
- As the **founder**, I am alerted when no florist takes an order, when a webhook keeps failing, or when a paid order is not offered within 5 minutes, and I can reroute or cancel from the alert without a database edit.
- As the **founder on WhatsApp**, I receive the exact message to paste to each florist who prefers WhatsApp.
- As a **recipient**, my address and phone reach only the florist who delivers to me, and only once the order is certain.

### 5.2 Jobs, services and contracts

**Contracts this spec consumes** (named here so specs 013 and 015 are written to match):
- `orders.transition(orderId, event, actor, payload)` (spec 015). Actors: `system:order.route`, `system:assignment.timeout`, `partner:{id}`, `admin:ops-link`.
- `payments.capture(orderId)` → `captured | already_captured | failed` (spec 013; immediate-charge payments answer `already_captured`).
- `payments.releaseOrRefund(orderId, reason)` → voids an open authorisation or refunds a captured payment in full (spec 013).
- Transitions this spec drives (from `plan/11` §2; §13 Q9 covers any the seeded `order_transition` lacks): `authorised|paid_unrouted → routed` (route), `routed → routed` (decline, timeout → next offer), `authorised|paid_unrouted|routed → no_partner_available` (exhausted), `no_partner_available → manual_reroute → routed`, `no_partner_available → cancelled` (no-partner cancel), `routed → accepted` (accept), `accepted → paid` (capture), `accepted → cancelled` (capture failed), `paid → in_production → out_for_delivery` (start, dispatch), `out_for_delivery → delivered → photo_uploaded`.

**Routing (`src/modules/orders/routing/`)**
- `eligibleCandidates(order, partners, now)` — pure. A partner is eligible when: its status is `active` (a non-real order, `order.source <> 'real'`, routes only to `demo` partners, and a real order never does); a `partner_coverage` row matches the order's postcode zone or city; `partner_catalog_mapping.can_fulfil` is true for every product line and tier; no `partner_blackout` on the delivery date; accepted plus open offers on that date are below `min(partner.capacity_per_day, coverage.capacity_per_day)`; and it has not already been offered this order.
- `rankCandidates(candidates)` — pure, deterministic. Sort key, in order: inside the best-rating band (rating ≥ 95 % of the best candidate's) before outside it, then rating descending; acceptance rate over the last 90 days descending; partner payout for this order ascending; last offer time ascending (round-robin); partner id. A null rating counts as 4.50 and fewer than 5 offers in 90 days counts as 100 % acceptance (§13 Q2).
- `offerWindow(now, deliveryDate, zone)` — pure, computed in the destination country's IANA zone with spec 009's zone helpers. Same-day: offer now, SLA 30 min. Otherwise 08:00–20:00 local: offer now, SLA 60 min. 07:30–08:00: offer now, SLA to 09:00. 20:00–07:30: offer at the next 07:30, SLA to 09:00. The SLA never runs past the **routing deadline**: the delivery date at the country's same-day cutoff plus 2 hours (16:00 for PL; §13 Q3). Absolute instants are stored; DST is handled by the zone, never by adding hours to local times.
- `order.route` (payload `{ orderId }`, pg-boss `singletonKey = orderId`): lock the order's open assignment and the chosen partner row (`FOR UPDATE`) so two routes cannot double-book capacity; pick the top candidate; insert `order_assignment` (`attempt` + 1, `offered_at`, `sla_expires_at`); issue the link token; `transition(route)`; schedule `assignment.remind` at half the SLA and `assignment.timeout` at `sla_expires_at` (both keyed by assignment id). If the offer is deferred to 07:30, the job reschedules itself with `startAfter` and writes nothing else. With no candidate, 5 attempts made, or the deadline passed: `transition(exhausted)`.
- `assignment.timeout`: if the assignment is still unanswered, set `response = 'timeout'`, revoke its token, `transition(timeout)` and enqueue `order.route`. An answered assignment makes it a no-op.
- `order.route_guard`: scheduled when the paid boundary is crossed, at the expected first offer time plus 5 minutes; alerts if no assignment exists by then.
- `order.no_partner_expire`: scheduled on exhaustion at `min(now + 2 h, routing deadline)`; if the order is still `no_partner_available`, `transition(cancel)`, `payments.releaseOrRefund(orderId, 'no_partner_available')`, buyer email B5.
- Feature flag `routing.auto` (scope `country`, default on for `live` countries): when off, a paid order goes straight to `no_partner_available` and the founder routes it by ops link.

**Florist links (`src/modules/partners/links/`, routes `app/v/[token]/`)**
- Token: `v1.{base64url(id)}.{base64url(HMAC-SHA256(PARTNER_LINK_SECRET, "v1." + id))}`. Verification compares in constant time against `PARTNER_LINK_SECRET`, then `PARTNER_LINK_SECRET_PREVIOUS` if set (rotation), then loads the row and checks it is not revoked or expired and that its partner still holds the assignment. A bad signature, an unknown id and a revoked token all return the same 404 page, so the link is not an oracle.
- Expiry: at offer, `sla_expires_at`; at acceptance, extended to the end of the delivery date + 3 days. Revoked on timeout, decline, withdrawal and any cancellation.
- `GET /v/{token}` renders by state: **offer** (product, tier, add-ons, delivery date and window, city and postcode, payout in the florist's currency, SLA end in local time, Accept and Decline); **confirming** (accepted, capture pending: "we are confirming payment; the delivery details appear here in a minute"); **order card** (after capture: recipient name, address, phone, card message, delivery window, a print view, and the next action); **done**; **no longer yours** (timed out, declined, withdrawn, cancelled). No recipient detail is rendered before capture, or after the token expires.
- `POST /v/{token}/accept`, `/decline` (`reason ∈ capacity | out_of_area | product_unavailable | closed | other`, optional note ≤ 200 characters), `/dispatch` (runs `start` then `dispatch`), `/delivered`, `/photo`. Zod at every boundary; each POST locks the assignment row, re-checks state, and either transitions or renders "no longer yours". Accept and timeout racing on the same assignment: the first to lock wins, the other is a no-op.
- `POST /v/{token}/photo`: one image (`image/jpeg`, `image/png`, `image/webp`; ≤ 15 MB; the input's `accept` list makes iOS send JPEG, not HEIC). With `sharp`: decode, auto-orient, drop all metadata, longest edge 2000 px, JPEG quality 82. Stored via `Storage.put` at `objectKey('delivery_proof', orderId, uuid)`, `media_asset` with `visibility = 'private'`, `exif_stripped = true`, `source = 'partner'`, then `delivery_proof`, then `transition(photo)` — `deliver` first if the florist skipped "delivered".
- Every action writes the order event with `actor = partner:{id}` and `payload.via = 'link'`; `last_used_at` and `use_count` are updated.

**Ops links (`app/ops/[token]/`)** — sent only inside alert A1. Same HMAC scheme with a separate secret purpose prefix (`"ops1."`), 2-hour expiry, single use. `GET` shows the order's destination, date, products, the attempts so far and the partners still eligible if one is relaxed (blackout excepted). `POST` either offers to one named eligible partner (`manual_reroute` then `route`) or cancels and releases/refunds now. Actor `admin:ops-link`.

**Notifications (`src/modules/notifications/`, `emails/`)**
- `EmailSender` interface `{ send(message: EmailMessage): Promise<{ providerMessageId: string }> }`; `EmailMessage` is a zod schema (`to`, `from`, `replyTo`, `subject`, `html`, `text`, `attachments?`, `idempotencyKey`, `tags`). Adapters: `ResendEmailSender` (EU region; `Idempotency-Key` header = `{outboxId}:{templateKey}:{audience}`) and `LogEmailSender` (writes a redacted line and a test sink; never sends). Selected by `EMAIL_TRANSPORT`.
- **Sandbox rule**: when `APP_ENV` is not `production`, every `to` is rewritten to `EMAIL_SANDBOX_TO` before the adapter sees it. There is no switch to turn this off outside production.
- `notificationsFor(eventType)` — pure; the table below as data. The `notification.deliver` job (payload `{ outboxId }`) loads the outbox row, resolves each audience's address at send time (buyer → `customer.email`, partner → `fulfillment_partner.contact_email`, ops → `OPS_ALERT_EMAIL`), renders the template in the audience's locale, sends, and writes `notification_delivery` and `outbox_attempt`. Retries: 5 attempts with exponential backoff from 1 minute; the fifth failure marks the row `dead` and raises alert A4. An existing `notification_delivery` row for `(outbox_id, template_key, audience)` in a sent state makes the send a no-op.
- Feature flag `notifications.email` (global): when off, rows wait as `pending` — held, never dropped.

Subject lines never contain a person's name, an address or a phone number. Open and click tracking are **off** on the Resend domain: click tracking would rewrite the magic links through a third party.

**Resend webhooks (`app/api/webhooks/resend/route.ts`)**
- Verify the Svix signature (`svix-id`, `svix-timestamp`, `svix-signature`) with `RESEND_WEBHOOK_SECRET`; reject a timestamp more than 5 minutes off. A failure returns 401, logs `warn` and sends a Sentry event fingerprinted `webhook-signature:resend`.
- Store the raw body in `webhook_inbox` (`provider = 'resend'`, `event_id = svix-id`) before anything else; a duplicate `svix-id` returns 200 and does nothing. Respond 200, then process in `webhook.resend.process`.
- Processing updates `notification_delivery` by `provider_message_id` with a precedence order (`queued < sent < delivery_delayed < delivered`, and `bounced`, `complained`, `failed` are final), so events arriving out of order never move a status backwards. A bounce on a partner address raises an ops email ("florist unreachable by email"). `email.opened` and `email.clicked` are ignored.
- `alertWebhookFailure(provider, inboxId, error)` is the one hook for A2; spec 013's Stripe/Mollie processors call it too.

### 5.3 UI

- **`/v/{token}` pages** (florist, mobile-first): the five states of §5.2, each a server-rendered page whose actions are plain HTML forms that work without JavaScript; buttons at least 48 px tall; the photo step uses `<input type="file" accept="image/jpeg,image/png,image/webp" capture="environment">`; loading state on submit; error states for a too-large or unreadable photo; a print view of the order card. Rendered in the partner's locale.
- **`/ops/{token}` page** (founder, `en`): order summary, eligible partners, two actions, a used/expired state.
- **Email templates**: one layout (logo, body, footer with trader identity and support contact), the keys of §5.2, HTML plus plain text, `lang` and `dir` set per locale, symmetric spacing only (no physical CSS, `CLAUDE.md`).
- **WhatsApp procedure (Phase 1, manual)** — `docs/runbooks/whatsapp-phase1.md`: the company number and device (`plan/13` B10); each WhatsApp partner saved as a contact with the label "florist"; when a W email arrives, paste its block into that florist's chat; never accept or decline on a florist's behalf — ask them to tap the link; florists' replies with problems go to the ops link or a phone call. Email always reaches the florist in parallel, so a late paste never blocks an offer.
- **Design**: the `/v` states, the `/ops` page and the email layout get artboards in `docs/design/` (`/design`) before `/plan-tasks`, per `CLAUDE.md`.

### 5.4 Rendering & caching

`/v/*` and `/ops/*` are dynamic SSR, `Cache-Control: no-store`, `X-Robots-Tag: noindex, nofollow`, `Referrer-Policy: no-referrer`, outside the `[locale]` segment, and follow ADR-0016's rule for `no-store` routes (per-request nonce; if no earlier spec has shipped that path, task 2 ships it). They are never prerendered and touch no cache tag. Emails are rendered on the worker at send time. No indexable page changes; the database stays off the critical path of every cached page (spec 002 §6).

## 6. SEO considerations (mandatory)

- **Indexability**: `/v/*` and `/ops/*` are `noindex, nofollow` by header and `robots` meta, `Disallow` in `robots.txt`, absent from every sitemap, linked from no indexable page, and return 404 (not 401/403) for any bad token so nothing about them is discoverable.
- **Canonical / hreflang**: none — not indexable, not localised by URL (the locale comes from the partner row).
- **URL pattern**: `/v/{token}` per `plan/05` row 59; `/ops/{token}` new; no localised slugs.
- **Schema**: none. Emails carry no schema.org markup.
- **Internal links**: emails link to the buyer's locale home and the support page only; no tracking parameters.
- **Thin content**: not applicable (not indexable).
- **CWV**: no change to indexable pages; no new script on them. Routing and email run on the worker.

## 7. i18n considerations (mandatory)

- **Message keys**: namespaces `email.buyer.*`, `email.partner.*`, `email.ops.*` (ops in `en` only), `email.layout.*`, `whatsapp.partner.*`, `vendorLink.*` (the `/v` pages), `ops.*`. All four locales for buyer and partner keys; ICU plurals for stems and items.
- **Locale choice**: buyer messages in `order.locale_code`; partner messages and `/v` pages in `fulfillment_partner.locale_code`; ops in `en`.
- **Formatting**: dates, times and money through the `i18n` module's `Intl` formatters. Times shown to a florist are in the destination zone; the buyer sees the delivery date as a calendar date (no time zone conversion of a date). Money: buyer amounts in the buyer currency, payout in the partner's currency, integer minor units in, `Intl.NumberFormat` out.
- **Addresses and phones**: rendered from `order.recipient_snapshot` in the destination country's format (`plan/03` §8); phone shown as `tel:` link in E.164.
- **RTL**: none of the four locales is RTL; templates set `dir` from the locale config so an RTL locale later needs no template change.
- **Translation review**: `de` and `pl` email and page strings go through the human review gate of spec 003 before the PL flip; an unreviewed buyer key in a live locale fails `i18n:check`. Visual snapshots per template per locale (`plan/11` §5.3).

## 8. Compliance considerations (mandatory)

- **Data flows added (RoPA, `docs/compliance/ropa.md`)**: (1) **Resend** — processor, EU region (eu-west-1) chosen at domain creation; data: buyer email, florist email, ops email, message bodies (recipient name and city in B1, the delivery photo in B4); basis Art. 6(1)(b) for buyers and florists; Resend's message retention noted; DPA accepted and filed (founder action). (2) **Recipient data to the florist** — processor under the partner agreement's Art. 28 clauses (`plan/07` §1.1, B3); shown only to the florist holding an accepted, captured assignment, only on the `no-store` link page, only until the token expires; never in an email or WhatsApp message. (3) **WhatsApp (Meta)** — the founder's Business app; messages carry no recipient data (V1/V2/V4/V5 are minimised), only the florist's business phone. (4) **Delivery photo** — private R2 object, EXIF/GPS stripped, 2-year retention by spec 002's sweep, copied to Resend as the B4 attachment.
- **Lawful basis**: buyer messages — contract; recipient data — legitimate interest with minimisation (`plan/07` §1.2); florist data — contract. No message here is marketing; none carries an unsubscribe link, and none may be added without consent gating.
- **Minimisation**: florists who decline or time out never see the recipient's name, street or phone; ops alerts carry no personal data; `notification_delivery` stores no address or subject.
- **Consumer law**: B1 carries the `plan/07` §2.1 information (characteristics, total price, trader identity and address, delivery arrangements, the perishable exemption, free cancellation until acceptance, complaints path). B2 is the confirmation on a durable medium (CRD Art. 8(7), UK CCR 2013) and attaches the terms version the buyer accepted; the contract is formed on capture (`plan/07` §2.3), which is when B2 is sent. A no-partner order is never charged, or is refunded in full (`plan/06` §2.2).
- **Price display**: every amount in a buyer email equals the amount charged, in the charged currency.
- **Accessibility**: `/v` and `/ops` pages meet WCAG 2.1 AA (axe in CI, keyboard operable, labelled inputs, error text tied to its field); emails use semantic headings, alt text per locale on the photo, a plain-text part, sufficient contrast.
- **Logs / PII**: no address, name, phone, card message, email address, subject line or token in any log, Sentry event or analytics event. The logger gains `token` and `svixsignature` on the redaction list, and request paths `/v/…` and `/ops/…` are logged as `/v/[REDACTED]`, including in Sentry's `request.url`. Decline notes are free text and never logged.
- **Security**: tokens are bearer credentials — 128-bit ids, HMAC-SHA256 signatures, constant-time comparison, rotation by a second secret, rate limit 30 requests/minute/IP on `/v/*` and `/ops/*`, 404 for every failure. Resend webhook signature and timestamp checked before the body is trusted.

## 9. Acceptance criteria

**Routing**
- **AC-1** When an order crosses the paid boundary (`authorised` or `paid_unrouted`), exactly one `order.route` job runs for it, even if the event is delivered twice.
- **AC-2** `eligibleCandidates` excludes, each on its own, a partner that is not `active`, has no coverage for the order's postcode zone or city, cannot fulfil one product line, has a blackout on the date, is at capacity on the date, or was already offered this order; a non-real order is offered only to `demo` partners and a real order never to one.
- **AC-3** `rankCandidates` orders a fixture of five candidates exactly as §5.2's sort key says, including the 95 % rating band, the null-rating default of 4.50 and the under-5-offers default of 100 %.
- **AC-4** `offerWindow` returns the offer time and SLA end of §5.2 for each case — same-day, daytime, 07:30–08:00, night — in Europe/Warsaw and Europe/London, including the nights of 25 Oct 2026 and 28 Mar 2027, and never returns an SLA end after the routing deadline.
- **AC-5** An offer inserts one `order_assignment` with `attempt`, `offered_at` and `sla_expires_at`, issues one link token, moves the order to `routed`, and sends V1 (and W for a WhatsApp partner).
- **AC-6** With no answer, V2 is sent at half the SLA; at `sla_expires_at` the assignment becomes `timeout`, its token is revoked, V4 is sent, and the next candidate receives an offer.
- **AC-7** A decline records the reason code, revokes the token and offers the next candidate within one job run.
- **AC-8** Two concurrent routes for orders on the same date cannot take a partner past its capacity (integration test with two workers).
- **AC-9** With no candidate left, after 5 offers, or past the routing deadline, the order moves to `no_partner_available` and A1 is sent with a working ops link.
- **AC-10** If the order is still `no_partner_available` at the end of the grace window, it moves to `cancelled`, `payments.releaseOrRefund` is called once, and B5 is sent; if the founder rerouted it first, nothing happens.
- **AC-11** With `routing.auto` off for the country, a paid order goes straight to `no_partner_available` with A1.

**Florist links**
- **AC-12** A link with a changed signature, an unknown id, a revoked row or an expired row returns the same 404 page and body.
- **AC-13** A `GET` on any link state changes no row (the order, assignment and token rows are byte-identical before and after, except `last_used_at`).
- **AC-14** Accept moves the order `routed → accepted`, calls `payments.capture` once, and on `captured` or `already_captured` moves it to `paid`, sends B2 and V3, and extends the token to the delivery date + 3 days.
- **AC-15** Before capture succeeds, and after any decline, timeout or cancellation, the link page contains no recipient name, street, phone or card message (asserted on the HTML).
- **AC-16** When capture fails, the order moves to `cancelled`, `payments.releaseOrRefund` is called, V4 goes to the florist and A5 goes to the ops address.
- **AC-17** Accept racing the timeout on one assignment ends with exactly one outcome and one order event.
- **AC-18** Dispatch from `paid` records `start` then `dispatch` and sends B3; delivered records `deliver`; a photo upload from `out_for_delivery` records `deliver` then `photo`.
- **AC-19** An uploaded JPEG carrying EXIF GPS tags is stored with no EXIF, at most 2000 px on its longest edge, as a private `delivery_proof` asset; B4 goes to the buyer with the photo attached; a file over 15 MB or not an image is rejected with an error message and no row written.
- **AC-20** `delivered` without a photo sends V5 one hour later; a photo uploaded before then cancels it.
- **AC-21** An ops link offers the order to a chosen eligible partner, or cancels and releases/refunds it; a second use, or use after 2 hours, shows the used/expired state and changes nothing.
- **AC-22** Any cancellation of an order with an open or accepted assignment revokes the token and sends V4.

**Notifications**
- **AC-23** For each outbox event type in the §5.2 table, `notificationsFor` returns exactly the listed templates and audiences, and nothing for any other event type.
- **AC-24** A send failure is retried with backoff; the fifth failure marks the outbox row `dead` and sends A4 and a Sentry event; a re-run after success sends nothing (one Resend call per `(outbox, template, audience)`, same idempotency key).
- **AC-25** Outside production, every send goes to `EMAIL_SANDBOX_TO`, whatever the order's address.
- **AC-26** Every template renders in `en`, `en-gb`, `de` and `pl` with an HTML and a plain-text part, no missing message key, money and dates through `Intl`, and a visual snapshot per locale.
- **AC-27** V1, V2, V4 and V5 and every W copy contain no recipient name, street, phone or card message; no subject line contains a name, address or phone.
- **AC-28** B1 contains each `plan/07` §2.1 information item listed in §8; B2 attaches the terms PDF of `order.terms_version`.
- **AC-29** With `notifications.email` off, outbox rows stay `pending` and are all sent once it is turned on.

**Webhooks and alerts**
- **AC-30** The Resend webhook rejects a bad signature or a timestamp more than 5 minutes off with 401, stores a valid event in `webhook_inbox` before processing, and answers a duplicate `svix-id` with 200 and no second processing.
- **AC-31** Resend events applied in any order leave `notification_delivery.status` at the highest-precedence status received; a bounce on a partner address sends an ops email.
- **AC-32** A webhook inbox row whose processing exhausts its retries sends A2 and a Sentry event, for Resend here and through the same hook for spec 013.
- **AC-33** A paid order with no offer 5 minutes after its expected first offer time sends A3 (fault injection: the route job disabled); a night-deferred order does not alert at night.

**Logs**
- **AC-34** Across the integration suite, no log line or Sentry event contains an email address, a phone, a recipient name, a link token, or a `/v/` or `/ops/` path segment after the prefix.

## 11. Observability

- **Order events** (spec 015's table): every routing step is an event — `assignment.offered`, `.accepted`, `.declined` (reason code), `.timed_out`, `order.no_partner_available`, `order.cancelled` — with `actor` as in §5.2. The timeline of any order answers "who was offered, when, and why it moved".
- **Logs** (JSON, `request_id`/`job_id`, `order_id`, never PII): `routing.evaluated` (candidate counts per exclusion rule, chosen partner id), `routing.deferred` (offer time), `notification.sent` / `.failed` / `.dead` (template key, audience, outbox id, provider message id), `webhook.received` / `.processed` / `.rejected` (provider, event type).
- **Alerts**: A1–A5 by email to `OPS_ALERT_EMAIL` and as Sentry events with stable fingerprints (`alert:no-partner`, `alert:webhook-failure:{provider}`, `alert:unrouted`, `alert:notification-dead`, `alert:capture-failed`) so a Resend outage still reaches the founder through Sentry's own notification; Sentry rule for `webhook-signature:*` at 3 events in 15 minutes. `docs/runbooks/alerts.md` gains one section per alert: what it means and what to do.
- **Compute note**: every job here is event-driven on the existing pg-boss worker (ADR-0018); nothing polls the database on its own schedule.

## 13. Open questions

- **Q1 — What starts routing: money secured, or money captured?** The brief says "on the paid event"; `plan/01` §8 says "on `paid`"; `plan/11` §2, the canonical machine, routes from `authorised` (and from `paid_unrouted` for BLIK, P24 and the other immediate-charge methods) and captures only on acceptance. **Recommendation: route as soon as money is secured (`authorised` or `paid_unrouted`)** — that is the only reading in which "capture on acceptance" works and the buyer is never charged for an order no florist takes.
- **Q2 — How do we rank florists who have no history yet?** At launch every partner has no rating and few offers. **Recommendation: treat a missing rating as 4.50 and fewer than 5 offers as 100 % acceptance**, so round-robin decides among new florists and work is shared fairly from the first order.
- **Q3 — Until when on the delivery day do we keep offering, and how long do we wait for you before cancelling?** **Recommendation: no new offer after the same-day cutoff + 2 hours (16:00 in Poland), and a grace window of 2 hours (never past that deadline) before an unrouted order is cancelled and released.** Longer windows risk a buyer learning at 18:00 that nothing will arrive.
- **Q4 — Should the recipient's address, phone and card message appear in the florist's email?** **Recommendation: no — only on the link page, and only after capture.** Florists who decline never see them, and nothing personal sits in inboxes or WhatsApp chats; the page has a print view for the driver. The cost is one extra tap for the florist.
- **Q5 — How do you reroute or cancel before the admin order queue (spec 020) exists?** **Recommendation: a signed, single-use, 2-hour ops link inside the no-partner alert**, offering "send to florist X" or "cancel and refund". The alternative is a command-line script run against the production database.
- **Q6 — Is the Phase 1 WhatsApp procedure right?** **Recommendation: yes — for florists who chose WhatsApp, the system emails you the ready-to-paste message for every offer, reminder, withdrawal and photo reminder; florists always get the email too; buyers get email only until spec 028.** Start Meta Business Verification as soon as the OÜ exists (`plan/13` B10) so spec 028 is not waiting on it.
- **Q7 — Is it right to leave out substitutions, delivery-failed, review request, payout statements, login and privacy emails?** **Recommendation: yes** — they belong to specs 018–022 and the payouts spec; in Phase 1 a florist with a substitution or failed delivery calls or messages you. This keeps the build near two weeks.
- **Q8 — Where does the terms PDF attached to the confirmation come from?** The confirmation on a durable medium must carry the terms the buyer accepted. **Recommendation: one static PDF per terms version and variant (EU, UK; per locale), uploaded to R2 when the legal text is final; a missing PDF for a live country's terms version blocks the go-live flip, not this build.**
- **Q9 — What if a dependency is not ready?** **Recommendation: write specs 013 and 015 next and start tasks 1–2 after 015's `transition` merges; tasks 3–4 start now. If TASK-082 (R2 `put`) is still blocked when task 2 starts, task 2 takes over its `put` and `getSignedUrl` half against the same contract suite. Any transition in §5.2 missing from spec 002's seeded `order_transition` (for example `authorised → no_partner_available` and `accepted → cancelled` on capture failure) is added by spec 015, not here.**
- **Q10 — How does the delivery photo reach the buyer: attached, or a link?** A signed link expires and breaks the email later; an attachment copies the photo to Resend. **Recommendation: attach a 1200 px JPEG inline**, because the photo is the moment the buyer trusts us, and it should still be there next month.
