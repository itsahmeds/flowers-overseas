## 1. Problem

The founder is signing florists in Poland and wants one place, visible only to us, that answers five questions per florist and across the business: what does this florist charge us for each bouquet, how much have they sold through us, how much do we owe them and how much have we paid, what did each order cost us, and what did we keep. Today the answers are scattered or missing. Florist prices sit in `partner_catalog_mapping` (spec 012b shows a margin per product and size before a sale, never after). Payout lines, statements and payments arrive with spec 030a, with no view of sales or margin beside them. Refunds (spec 013, 020a) and payment disputes are rows with no cost view. Stripe's processing fee and its currency conversion are not recorded anywhere: spec 013 stores the captured amount, not what reached our balance. FX rates exist as dated ECB rows (TASK-071), but nothing converts a PLN payout and a GBP sale into one comparable figure.

The founder also asked for "a CRM" for florists: who the contact person is, what was said on the last call, which follow-up is due, how the florist is rated and when they last had an order. `plan/11` §4.2 recommends HubSpot free as a founder tool for the florist pipeline; the founder now wants the relationship record next to the money, in admin. This spec gives a light layer on the partner record we already keep (notes, a contact log, a contact name, follow-ups) and nothing more (§3).

Everything here is for the founder and admins. Florists never see it (their own statements are spec 030a's emails and later spec 027b's portal), and buyers never see any of it. Because it is admin-only, "florist", "partner" and "payout" are the right words here (spec 012 §2.2 exempts `messages/admin/`).

## 2. Scope

Each bullet is testable; §9 names the criterion.

**2.1 Access (admin only for money)**
- Four permissions join `ADMIN_PERMISSIONS`: `finance.view` and `finance.export` (admin only), `partner.crm.view` and `partner.crm.write` (admin and ops). Every money figure added by this spec (sales, refunds, VAT, fees, FX, florist prices and costs, owed, paid, margin) needs `finance.view`. Ops see the relationship record and no money. Florists, buyers and the public see nothing (RLS, §5.1).

**2.2 Where the payment fee comes from: Stripe balance transactions, triggered by the webhook inbox**
- When spec 013's `webhook.process` has applied a `payment_intent.succeeded`, a succeeded `refund.updated` or `charge.refunded`, or a `charge.dispute.created` or `charge.dispute.closed`, it enqueues `payments.balance_fetch` for that payment and source. The job asks the provider for the **balance transactions** of the source (`GET /v1/balance_transactions?source=ch_…|re_…|dp_…`; the charge id is read from the PaymentIntent's `latest_charge`) and stores each one in `payment_balance_txn`: the presentment amount and currency, the settled amount, fee and net in the settlement currency (EUR), Stripe's exchange rate as received text, our integer effective rate, and the date. No charge object is fetched, so no billing detail ever reaches our process.
- An hourly sweep re-enqueues any captured payment, succeeded refund or dispute that has no row an hour later; a payment still without a fee row after 24 hours raises an alert. Rows are facts from the provider and are never updated or deleted.
- Mollie (spec 014) supplies the same facts through its own adapter later; until then a Mollie payment's fee shows as "not available for this provider".

**2.3 One calculation of an order's money, in integer minor units**
- Per paid order (`live`, or `test` in the separate test view), a pure function computes, in the order's own currencies and in the reporting currency (EUR): gross sale; the sale at the ECB rate; what Stripe settled; the FX cost (the two differ by Stripe's conversion); refunds settled; VAT included in the price, net of VAT reversed by refunds; net sale; florist cost; payment fees; dispute losses; margin; and margin as a percentage of net sale. Each converted figure carries its rate, the rate's date and its source (ECB or Stripe).
- Purchase-level amounts (a charge, its fee, a dispute) are split across the purchase's orders in proportion to each order's total, by largest remainder, so the parts add up to the whole exactly. A refund belongs to the order it names (spec 013 §14 A-founder-2026-10-22).
- An order's figures are **final** or **provisional**; a provisional order lists its reasons from a closed set: `fees_pending`, `fees_unsupported_provider`, `refund_pending`, `florist_not_assigned`, `florist_cost_pending`, `adjustment_open`, `fx_rate_missing`.
- `test` orders are never mixed with `live` figures; `demo` orders take no money and never appear.

**2.4 VAT shown, never decided**
- The VAT figure is the VAT included in the buyer's price, read from the order's `vat_breakdown` (net of the VAT on succeeded refunds, read from spec 018's credit note when one is issued, else estimated pro rata and labelled). While any tax question in `src/config/finance.ts` is open (supply model, whether florist amounts include the florist's VAT, self-billing: `plan/06` §4 items 1 and 8, spec 030a §13 Q1 and Q2), every finance page carries an "Accountant to confirm" banner naming them; an order whose `supply_model` is null or `agent` carries the same label on its own row. Marking a question confirmed needs a sign-off file in `docs/compliance/` (§5.2).

**2.5 Portfolio overview** (`/admin/finance`)
- For a date range (default the last 30 days; at most 366 days; dates in the viewer's `admin_profile.time_zone`; basis "paid on" or "delivery date"): totals, a row per destination country, a row per florist, a VAT estimate per country, counts of final, provisional and rate-missing orders, and the owed and paid position across florists. Native-currency subtotals are never summed across currencies; EUR totals add the per-order EUR figures.
- `/admin/finance/orders`: one row per order with its figures, status and reasons, filterable by country and florist.
- CSV export of the order list, the florist table or the country table.

**2.6 Per florist** (tabs on spec 012's `/admin/partners/[code]`)
- **Prices**: every catalogue mapping (product, size, can fulfil, the florist's price and currency), the open retail price per country beside it with spec 012b's `marginView` figures, the add-on line "no agreed price on file" (spec 030a §13 Q3), and the price history read from the audit log.
- **Money**: for the date range, the florist's orders by outcome, sales, refunds, florist cost, fees, margin; **owed now** (spec 030a payouts in `draft` or `statement_sent`), **earned, awaiting photo** (fixed amounts with no payout line yet), open adjustments (count; their amount is undecided), and **paid** in the range and all time, with statement numbers and dates.
- **Relationship**: contact person, email, phone, channel, language, status and signed terms (spec 012's), the 90-day rating and acceptance rate (spec 022a), last accepted order date, notes (pinned first) and the contact log with follow-ups.

**2.7 CRM writes** (`partner.crm.write`)
- Notes: add, edit, pin, delete. Contact log: add an entry (when, channel, direction, topic, summary, optional follow-up date), edit it, mark its follow-up done. Contact name on the partner. Free text that contains an email address or a phone number is refused, with the hint to use the contact fields.
- `/admin/partners` gains the columns last accepted order, rating and (for `finance.view`) owed now, and a "Follow-ups due" list.

**2.8 The order detail** (spec 012's `/admin/orders/[id]`) gains a read-only **Money** panel for `finance.view`: the order's waterfall from gross to margin, each line with its rate and date.

## 3. Non-goals

- **Florists seeing their own figures** (prices, statements, payout history) → spec **027b** (portal). Phase 1 florists get spec 030a's statement emails.
- **Accounting records, the VAT return, invoices and credit notes as legal documents, the accounting export** → spec **018** (documents) and spec **039** (accounting export). This spec's figures are management figures; the CSV says so in its file name and first column header is the order reference, not an invoice number.
- **Wise's transfer fee and the actual EUR cost of a PLN payout** → spec **039** (030a records no EUR cost of a transfer). Florist costs here are converted at the ECB rate (§13 Q6) and labelled.
- **Mollie fees** → spec **014**, through the same provider method.
- **Marketing cost, CAC, ad spend and overheads.** Margin here is contribution after VAT, florist, payment fees and disputes, before marketing (`plan/06` §3).
- **Charts, forecasts, cohorts and scorecards** → `plan/11` §7 (Phase 4). Pages show tables and totals only.
- **Editing prices, payouts, refunds or florist status from these pages.** Prices: spec 012b; payout terms, lines, statements and payments: spec 030a; refunds: spec 020a; status: spec 012. Every page here is read-only except the CRM writes of §2.7.
- **A recruiting pipeline for florists who have not applied** (outreach lists, deal stages, tasks, reminders by email). Applications keep spec 011 and 012's flow; prospects stay in the founder's outreach sheet or HubSpot free per `plan/11` §4.2 (§13 Q10).
- **The customer CRM** (Brevo, `plan/11` §4, roadmap `032`) and any sync of florist records to an outside CRM.
- **Sending messages to florists from the CRM.** The contact log records conversations; it sends nothing.

## 4. User stories

- As the **founder on a Monday**, I open one page, pick last week, and see what we sold, what each country and each florist brought in, what we owe florists and what we kept, with the orders whose figures are not final listed with the reason.
- As the **founder before a call with a Warsaw florist**, I open their page and see their bouquet prices, our margin on each, their rating, the last thing we discussed, the follow-up I promised, what we owe them and when we last paid them.
- As the **founder after a refund**, I see that order's margin go down by exactly the refund and the fee we did not get back, and the florist's cost stay what the agreement says.
- As the **founder**, I see what Stripe took in fees and in currency conversion for each order, with the rate and its date, rather than a guess.
- As the **accountant**, I get a CSV of the period's orders with every figure, its currency and its rate, clearly marked as management figures, and the VAT questions still open are named on every page.
- As an **ops helper**, I can read and add notes and log calls with florists, and I never see a price, a payout or a margin.
- As a **florist**, nothing I can open shows our margin or the founder's notes about me.
- As a **buyer**, nothing changes; no figure or florist word reaches me.

**Pure functions, exactly:**
- `allocateByWeight(total: bigint, parts: { id, weight: bigint }[])` → `Map<id, bigint>`: each part gets `floor(|total| × weight ÷ Σweight)`; the remaining units go one each to the largest fractional remainders, ties to the lower id; the sign of `total` is applied last. The parts always sum to `total`. Throws `ALLOCATION_WEIGHTS_ZERO` when every weight is zero.
- `toReporting(amountMinor: bigint, fromExponent, toExponent, eurToFromRatePpm: bigint)` → `bigint`: `amountMinor × 10^toExponent × 1 000 000 ÷ (eurToFromRatePpm × 10^fromExponent)`, rounded half away from zero, using EUR-based `fx_rate` rows directly (no inverse-rate rounding, no FX buffer: spec 005's `convert` is a buyer-price function and is not used here).
- `rateOn(rateBook, currency, date)`: the newest `fx_rate` row for `EUR → currency` with `as_of` between `date − rateLookbackDays` and `date`, else `null`; EUR is rate 1 000 000 on every date.
- `effectiveRatePpm`: settled minor × 10^presentmentExponent × 1 000 000 ÷ (presentment minor × 10^settlementExponent), half away from zero; `null` when the two currencies are equal.
- `refundVatEstimate(refundMinor, vatBreakdown)`: `Σ vat × refundMinor ÷ gross` per rate, half away from zero; used only when the refund has no credit note.
- `orderMargin(facts, rateBook, config)` → `OrderMarginSchema`, in this order (B = buyer currency, C = florist payout currency, R = EUR):
  1. **Gross** `G` (B) = `buyer_total_minor`. **At ECB** `G_R = toReporting(G, rateOn(B, paid date))`.
  2. **Settled** `S_R` = this order's share (`allocateByWeight` over the purchase's orders by `buyer_total_minor`) of the `charge` rows' `amount_minor`. With no `charge` row: `S_R = G_R` and reason `fees_pending` (or `fees_unsupported_provider` for Mollie).
  3. **FX cost** = `G_R − S_R` (Stripe's conversion against the ECB rate).
  4. **Refunds** `Rf_R` = the absolute sum of `refund` rows of refunds naming this order, minus `refund_failure` rows; a succeeded refund with no row counts at `toReporting` of its amount on its `succeeded_at` date and adds `fees_pending`. A `requested` or `pending` refund is not subtracted and adds `refund_pending`.
  5. **VAT** (B) = the `vat_breakdown` VAT total minus, per succeeded refund, its credit note's VAT total (spec 018 snapshot) or else `refundVatEstimate` (shown as an estimate). `V_R` = `toReporting` at the paid-date rate.
  6. **Net sale** `N_R = S_R − Rf_R − V_R`.
  7. **Florist cost** (C) = the order's `payout_line` amounts of every kind (`order`, `adjustment`, `goodwill`); with none and an accepted assignment, the fixed `order_line` amounts and reason `florist_cost_pending`; with no accepted assignment, zero, plus `florist_not_assigned` unless the order is in a terminal cancelled or refunded status; an `open` `payout_adjustment` adds `adjustment_open`. `P_R` = `toReporting` at the rate of the payout's `paid_at` date when paid, else the newest available rate, its date shown (§13 Q6).
  8. **Fees** `F_R` = this order's share of every row's `fee_minor` (charge and dispute rows by weight; refund rows to their order).
  9. **Disputes** `D_R` = this order's share of `dispute` minus `dispute_reversal` amounts (absolute, fees excluded).
  10. **Margin** `M_R = N_R − P_R − F_R − D_R`; `marginBp = M_R × 10 000 ÷ N_R` half away from zero, `null` when `N_R ≤ 0`.
  11. Any rate needed and missing makes every `_R` figure of the order `null` with reason `fx_rate_missing`; native figures still show.
  12. **Final** exactly when the reasons list is empty.
- `rollUp(orders, by: 'all' | 'country' | 'partner')`: sums `_R` figures over orders whose figures are not null, native figures per currency only, and counts final, provisional and rate-missing orders.

## 13. Open questions

Each has a recommended default; implementation can start on the defaults.

- **Q1 (founder): who sees money?** *Default:* `admin` only (`finance.view`, `finance.export`); the `finance` role keeps spec 030a's payout pages and gets margins later by one matrix change when a finance person exists.
- **Q2 (founder): may ops read and write the relationship record?** *Default:* yes, with no money figure (they handle florists on order days).
- **Q3 (founder): reporting currency.** *Default:* EUR (our settlement and accounting currency, `plan/06` §2.2).
- **Q4 (founder): which date puts an order in a period?** *Default:* the day it was paid, in the viewer's zone, with "delivery date" as a switch.
- **Q5 (founder): margin percentage of what?** *Default:* of net sale (after refunds and VAT), consistent with spec 012b's payout share of net; `plan/06` §3's 23 to 29 % example is of gross retail, so the page names its base.
- **Q6 (founder): at which rate is a florist's PLN amount counted in EUR?** *Default:* the ECB rate of the day the payout was marked paid; before payment, the newest ECB rate, with its date shown; Wise's actual conversion and fee come with spec 039.
- **Q7 (accountant): the VAT figure.** *Default:* the VAT included in each price per the order's `vat_breakdown`, converted at the ECB rate of the paid day, shown as an estimate with "Accountant to confirm" until the three tax questions (supply model, florist VAT in the agreed amount, self-billing) have a sign-off file in `docs/compliance/`.
- **Q8 (founder): splitting one card payment across several deliveries.** *Default:* the charge, its fee and any dispute are split in proportion to each delivery's total, by largest remainder; refunds stay with the delivery they name.
- **Q9 (founder): what to show before Stripe's fee is known.** *Default:* "Fee pending" and a provisional margin, never an estimate from the rate card (no invented number).
- **Q10 (founder): prospects who have not applied.** *Default:* not in admin; they stay in the outreach sheet or HubSpot free (`plan/11` §4.2) until they apply through `/for-florists`, when spec 011 and 012's application flow takes over.
- **Q11 (founder, then lawyer with the florist agreement): how long to keep notes and the contact log.** *Default:* 24 months after the florist is offboarded; while a florist is active, until the founder deletes them.
- **Q12 (founder): refuse emails and phone numbers in notes.** *Default:* yes; contact details belong in the contact fields, which keeps personal data structured and minimal.
- **Q13 (founder): florist legal names in the CSV.** *Default:* no; partner code and city label only (legal names of sole traders are personal data and the founder knows the codes).
- **Q14 (founder): `plan/11` §4.2 recommends HubSpot free for the florist pipeline; this spec keeps a light relationship record in admin.** *Default:* record it in `docs/decisions-log.md` as a bounded addition (notes, contact log, follow-ups on florists who already have a partner record; no pipeline, tasks, email or sync) with no ADR, since ADR-0010 decides to buy the customer CRM and this builds none; an ADR only if the founder later wants pipeline stages in admin.

## 14. Self-check

Written by the spec writer from the four angles of the 2026-10-22 order (no advisor agent).

**Building.** Every number comes from data other specs already write: order, `vat_breakdown`, purchase, payment, refund with its order, dispute, payout lines and adjustments, the mapping, the rating columns and dated `fx_rate` rows. The only new money data is the provider's own balance transaction, stored immutably and fetched by source so no charge object (with billing details) enters the process. All arithmetic is `bigint` minor units in pure functions with exact fixtures (F1, F2), half-away-from-zero rounding and largest-remainder allocation whose parts always sum to the whole; there is no float on any money path (scanned). No order status is written (ADR-0009); every page is read-only except the CRM, whose writes go through spec 012's guard, audit trigger and concurrency check. Risks: the dependency order (§12), Stripe's `balance_transactions?source=` filter recorded once in the contract fixture so a change in the API shows as a red contract test, the DST night in the range filter (pinned by AC-20), and the query budget (AC-22) as order volume grows.

**Google ranking.** Nothing public changes: admin routes are disallowed, `noindex` by header and meta, `no-store`, bypassed at the edge, with no inbound links; the CSV is `noindex` and never stored. No script, font or page reaches an indexable URL, so CWV, sitemaps, hreflang and structured data are untouched.

**Law and compliance.** No tax rule is invented: VAT is the figure already in each price, every page names the open accountant questions until a sign-off file exists, and accounting records stay with specs 018 and 039. The florist relationship record is new personal data, handled with a RoPA row, contract and legitimate-interest bases, access limited to admin and ops with reads logged, masked audit values, refusal of contact details in free text, a 24-month post-offboarding retention, DSAR coverage through spec 019, and RLS that keeps every note and figure away from florists. The CSV carries no names of buyers, recipients or florists and is neutralised against formula injection.

**The customer.** Buyers and recipients see nothing new: no wording, no figure, no florist word reaches a buyer surface, and no buyer or recipient field appears in any finance view or export. Florists are paid exactly what spec 030a fixes; this spec only shows those amounts to the founder, converts them for comparison without changing them, and keeps the founder's notes about a florist out of the florist's reach. The founder gets one honest view of what each order earned, with provisional figures marked as such, which is what lets pricing stay fair to both buyers and florists.
