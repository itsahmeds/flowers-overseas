## 1. Problem

On the few days a year that matter most (Valentine's Day, Women's Day in Poland, each country's Mother's Day, the days before All Saints in Poland, the week before Christmas) every buyer wants the same date, each florist can make only so many bouquets, and the rest of the year's rules stop being true. Today the site has no idea of any of that. Spec 016 caps each florist at a fixed `capacity_per_day` when routing, so an order on a full day is taken, charged at once (founder ruling 2026-10-14) and then cancelled and refunded when no florist can take it: the exact "capacity-caused charge" `plan/09` Phase 2 says must be zero. The product page offers every open date whatever the load. Peak surcharges exist only as one `country_price` row per product per country per window, generated in code from a single `PEAK_DAYS` list that gives every destination the same two dates (14 Feb, 8 Mar 2027), so Women's Day is a peak in the UK and Poland's Mother's Day (26 May) is not a peak anywhere. Peak cutoffs (`plan/06` §7: "cutoff pulled forward, 12 Feb 18:00 for 14 Feb") cannot be expressed at all. And every new florist bouquet entered in admin (spec 012b) would need its own surcharge rows by hand.

The founder's rule of 2026-10-24 settles the shape: "we can't just keep updating countries one by one". So every peak day, surcharge, earlier cutoff, closure and capacity in this spec is a row that admin (or the florist) edits, read by code that has no country, date or amount in it. The buyer sees only true facts: the exact fee on the date before choosing it, the earlier order-by time, and "Fully booked" when it is. No countdown, no "only 3 left", no florist wording.

The pattern is consistent: incumbents either add peak fees late (at date choice or checkout), blur the date ("Saturday or Sunday"), or keep taking orders and fail on the day. What we take: **the fee on the chip before selection** (unoccupied, benchmark summary item 2), **an explicit earlier order-by time per peak day** (Interflora AU does this, in copy), and **stop selling a date when it is full instead of excusing late delivery** (the Poczta Kwiatowa lesson, which also maps to our refund-heavy failure mode). What we refuse: a "no guarantee on peak days" clause, a date range in place of a date, and any fee that first appears after the date is chosen.

## 2. Scope

**Data (admin-editable, per country, no code per country)**
- A **country calendar day** row per (country, date) of kind `peak` or `closed`. A `peak` row may carry: an occasion key (for the label), a **date surcharge** (integer minor units in the country's currency), an **earlier order-by** (days before plus a local time), and an **order cap** (a ceiling on orders for that date). A `closed` row (blackout) makes the date unselectable. Christmas Day is already closed through the public holiday rows (Poland: 24, 25 and 26 Dec in `seed/data/holidays.json`); `closed` rows cover the days around it that are not holidays, such as 27 Dec or 31 Dec. Rows are superseded, never edited in place.
- **Per-florist capacity per date**: an override row per (florist, date), set by the florist in their area or by admin, on top of the florist's default `capacity_per_day`. Existing `partner_blackout` rows stay the florist's days off.
- **`occasion_country.peak`**: a per-country flag marking which observed occasions are peaks there (Women's Day in PL, not in UK), used only to suggest next year's peak rows.
- **`order_status.holds_capacity`**: which order statuses use up a delivery slot, as data.

**Capacity**
- Florist effective capacity on a date: the override if set; otherwise the default on the country's regular delivery weekdays; otherwise 0 (a Sunday opened only because it is a peak day needs an explicit override). A florist day off is 0.
- Country day capacity: the sum of eligible florists' effective capacity, capped by the peak row's order cap if set. Held: the count of same-mode orders for that country and date in a holding status. Remaining: capacity minus held, floored at 0. One order is one delivery, whatever it contains.
- The product page and the checkout step 1 calendar mark a date **Fully booked** when capacity is above 0 and remaining is 0, and "We do not deliver on this day" when no florist works it. Only in the `live` picker state; `preview` and `unavailable` are spec 009's, unchanged.
- **Placement is atomic**: spec 010's `place` checks remaining capacity for the (country, date) under a transaction lock before any payment is created, so two buyers cannot both pay for the last slot. Refused: step 1 says so with the existing `checkout.coverage.noCapacity` text and names the next open date, never auto-selecting it.
- **Routing** (spec 016) uses the per-date florist capacity instead of the fixed default.

**Peak surcharges and price**
- One **date surcharge** per date: the peak row's surcharge if it has one; otherwise the Sunday surcharge on a Sunday; never both. It applies to every product in the country, including bouquets created after the row.
- Shown on the date chip **before** selection as the delta of two projected totals (spec 009 §13 design Q3), included in the summary total, the tier options, `Offer.price`, the signed quote and the charged total: one integer.
- The **"from" price** on cards is the cheapest tier on the cheapest selectable date in the window: the base price whenever any selectable date has no surcharge, otherwise base plus the smallest surcharge on offer, so a headline never understates what can actually be bought (UK CMA price transparency guidance; §8).

**Earlier cutoffs**
- A peak row with an order-by makes its date selectable only until that local wall-clock instant in the destination's zone, and the chip shows "Order by {date}, {time} {city} time" while it is open. No countdown, no relative day label (spec 009 §13 Q4 holds).

**Florist area, admin, alerts**
- Florist "Your days" screen (en, pl, de): default capacity, the next 60 days with booked and capacity per day, busy-day markers by occasion name, per-date capacity and days off, with refusals that protect accepted orders.
- Admin calendar per country: month view with state per date, florist breakdown, peak and closed row editor, order caps, and "Suggest peak days for {year}" from `occasion_country.peak`.
- Ops alerts at 80 % and 100 % of a date's capacity, and a peak readiness digest 14 and 3 days before each peak row.
- Cache purge of tag `delivery:{iso2}` only on state changes and cutoff instants, never per order.

## 3. Non-goals

- **City-level date greying on the PDP and a city capacity dashboard, manual re-route board** → spec **031**. The PDP is country-scoped (`plan/02` §4.2); a postcode with no free florist is caught by spec 016's `canServe` at checkout step 1 and at placement, as today.
- **Delivery windows** (morning/afternoon), **a "guaranteed 13 to 14 Feb window" product**, **"florist's choice red" tier switching**, **the All Saints product set and cemetery address fields** (`plan/06` §7) → later catalogue and checkout specs. Peak days here are about dates, money and capacity only.
- **Florist peak payout uplift** (paying florists more on peak days) → the payouts spec (030). A florist sees "Busy day: Valentine's Day", never our surcharge.
- **Moving the Sunday surcharge off `country_price`** → kept as is; only `peak_day` moves (§5.1). Benchmark item 18 (Sunday rows unreachable where `sundayDelivery` is `none`) stays the founder's data question.
- **Holiday editing** (`country_holiday`) → spec 012b slice B. Closures that are not public holidays use this spec's `closed` rows instead.
- **A peak-day block on corridor and country occasion pages** ("Valentine's Day in Poland: order by Fri 12 Feb, 18:00") → a later amendment to specs 007/008 (§13 Q11). Good for SEO; it needs its own artboards.
- **A Merchant Center or other product feed** → none exists. Rule recorded for whoever builds one: a feed price equals `Offer.price` at generation and the feed is regenerated on every `delivery:{iso2}` purge.
- **Waitlists, "notify me when a date opens", reserving capacity for VIPs, dynamic (demand-based) pricing.** Surcharges are fixed per date, set in advance.
- **Editing `CLAUDE.md`, `plan/*` or an accepted ADR.**

## 4. User stories

- As a **buyer in London on 10 Feb sending roses to Warsaw**, I see Sunday 14 Feb with "+£5.00" printed on it, "Valentine's Day", and "Order by Fri 12 Feb, 18:00 Warsaw time". When I pick it, the total moves by exactly £5.00, and that is what I pay.
- As the **same buyer on 13 Feb**, the 14th says "Ordering closed at 18:00 in Warsaw" and Monday 15 Feb says "included". Nobody takes my money for a day that cannot happen.
- As a **buyer when Warsaw's florists are full for the 14th**, the date says "Fully booked" before I choose anything. I am not told how many are left, I am not hurried, and no florist is mentioned.
- As a **florist in Kraków**, I open "Your days" on my phone, set 40 for Sunday 14 Feb, mark 27 Dec as a day off, and see 12 of 40 already booked. I cannot set 10 for a day where I already accepted 12.
- As the **founder in January**, I open Poland's calendar, press "Suggest peak days for 2027", accept Valentine's, Women's Day and Mother's Day, add 30 and 31 Oct by hand for All Saints, set 25 zł and "2 days before, 18:00" for the 14th, and cap it at 120 orders. Germany later is the same screen, no code.
- As the **founder on 12 Feb**, I get one email when Poland's 14 Feb passes 80 % and one when it is full, and a digest on 31 Jan and 11 Feb listing which florists have set their Valentine's capacity.
- As **Googlebot**, the PDP's `Offer.price` equals the visible total on load, its availability is true, and no new URL exists.

**Schemas.** `CountryCalendarDaySchema` (the row; refinements mirror the CHECKs, so admin gets the message before Postgres does). `CalendarDayInputSchema` (`.strict()`: `countryIso`, `date`, `kind`, `occasionKey?`, `surcharge?: { amountMinor: int ≥ 0 }` (currency is the country's, never typed), `orderBy?: { daysBefore: 0..14, localTime: "HH:mm" }`, `orderCap?: int ≥ 0`, `note?`). `FloristDayCapacityInputSchema` (`{ date, capacity: 0..500 }`), `FloristDefaultCapacityInputSchema` (`{ capacity: 1..500 }`), `FloristDayOffInputSchema` (`{ date, reason: enum }`), `DayCapacitySchema` (`{ date, floristCapacity, orderCap?, effective, held, remaining, state: 'open' | 'full' | 'noFlorists' }`), `PeakSuggestionSchema`.

**Read functions (pure where possible).**
- `floristCapacityOn({ partner, date, override?, dayOff, regularDay })` → integer: day off 0; override if present; else default on a regular delivery weekday; else 0. Pure.
- `dayCapacity({ countryIso, from, to, mode })` → `DayCapacity[]`: one aggregate query per call (eligible florists by status for the mode and coverage in the country; overrides; days off; peak caps; held counts by `order.delivery_date` and `holds_capacity` for the same `order.mode`). Implements the amended `CapacityProvider`. `nullCapacityProvider` (static providers, or the `delivery.capacity` flag off for the country) returns `null`, meaning unmodelled: no date is ever full from it (`null` is not `0`).
- `dateSurchargeOn(countryIso, date)` → the one-surcharge rule. `unavoidableSurcharge(window)` → `Money | null`: `null` if any selectable date in the window carries no surcharge or none is selectable, else the smallest surcharge among selectable dates. Lives in `geo`; the listing composer in `app/` passes it to `fromPrice()`, so `catalog` never imports `geo` internals (`plan/01` §5).
- `deliveryGrid()` (spec 009, amended): peak rows feed `peakDates` (for `sundayDelivery: 'peak'`) and per-date order-by; `closed` rows and `noFlorists` dates reason `delivery.reason.notDeliveryDay`; `full` dates reason `delivery.reason.fullyBooked`. Reason precedence: `beforeEarliest`, `pastCutoff` (peak-aware), `publicHoliday`, `notDeliveryDay` (closed row), `sundayClosed`, `notDeliveryDay` (weekday rule or no florists), `fullyBooked`, `notOrderable`. Capacity is consulted **only** in `live`.
- Order-by for a date D: the regular rule (spec 009, same day before `sameDayCutoffLocal`) unless a peak row sets one; then D is open while the destination wall clock is before (D minus `daysBefore`) at `localTime`, compared wall clock to wall clock as `cutoffAt()` already does (DST safe, AC-6 of 009).

**Commands** (each in one transaction, zod at the boundary, audit row by trigger, `invalidate()` after commit):
- `upsertCalendarDay(adminCtx, CalendarDayInput)`: retire the active row for (country, date) if any, insert the new one. A surcharge change is allowed on a date that already has orders: those orders keep the amount on their own order lines, and only new quotes see the new amount. An `order_cap` below the date's current held count is refused with `CAP_BELOW_HELD`. Invalidates `delivery:{iso2}` and `catalog:{iso2}`.
- `retireCalendarDay(adminCtx, id, reason)`.
- `suggestPeakDays(adminCtx, iso2, year)` → `PeakSuggestion[]` (no write): one per `occasion_country` row with `peak = true` and `observed = true`, dated by `occasionDate(rule, year)`; status `new`, `exists` (an active row already on that date) or `closedHoliday` (the date is a closed public holiday, as 1 Nov in PL: shown as a warning, never proposed as a peak row). `applyPeakSuggestions(adminCtx, iso2, year, dates[], defaults)` writes the chosen ones through `upsertCalendarDay`.
- `setFloristDayCapacity(ctx, input)`, `clearFloristDayCapacity(ctx, date)`, `setFloristDefaultCapacity(ctx, input)`, `addFloristDayOff(ctx, input)`, `removeFloristDayOff(ctx, date)`: callable with a florist context (own partner only, RLS) or `capacity.edit` admin context. Under a row lock on the partner, they refuse a capacity below the florist's **committed** count for the date (accepted assignments plus open offers) with `CAPACITY_BELOW_COMMITTED { committed }`, and a day off on a date with committed orders with `DAY_OFF_HAS_ORDERS` for a florist; an admin may add that day off with a reason, and the response lists the affected assignments for spec 020's reroute (nothing is withdrawn automatically; no buyer message changes, founder rule 2026-10-14).
- `assertDateHasCapacity(tx, { countryIso, date, mode })`: takes `pg_advisory_xact_lock(hashtext('capacity:' || iso2 || ':' || date))`, recomputes that one date, throws `DATE_FULLY_BOOKED` when remaining is 0 (or `noFlorists`). Called by spec 010's `place` inside its transaction **before** the payment intent is created; a `null` (unmodelled) provider skips it. Transaction-level advisory locks work under Neon's pooled connection because the lock lives in the one transaction.

**Jobs** (pg-boss on the Railway worker, ADR-0018; versioned zod payloads):
- `capacity.watch` (`{ countryIso, date }`, `singletonKey = iso2:date`): enqueued after commit by placement, by every move into a status with `holds_capacity = false` (cancel with its full refund, abandon, payment failure), and by every capacity or calendar edit. Recomputes the date; when its state changed between `open` and `full` (or `noFlorists`), calls `invalidate(['delivery:{iso2}'])` once; when held first reaches 80 % or 100 % of effective capacity, sends the ops alert once per (country, date, threshold) (idempotency key in `notification_delivery`, spec 016).
- `peak.readiness` (daily 07:00 UTC): for every active `peak` row exactly 14 or 3 days ahead in a `live` country, one ops digest: florists, effective capacity, whether each set an override, cap, held; a flag when effective capacity is 0 or no florist set an override.
- `delivery.cutoff_purge` (scheduled per live country): at destination midnight, at the regular cutoff minute, and at each peak order-by instant in the next 48 hours, `invalidate(['delivery:{iso2}'])`. Instants are computed in the destination zone (DST is the zone's job).

**Events and logs.** `capacity.date_full`, `capacity.date_reopened`, `capacity.threshold_crossed { iso2, date, threshold, held, effective }`, `checkout.date_full_refused { iso2, date, step }`, `calendar_day.changed { iso2, date, kind, actor }`. No postcode, name, email or order contents in any of them.

**Ops alert templates** (spec 016's ops path, `messages/admin/en.json`, English only): `A2 alert-capacity-threshold`, `A3 alert-peak-readiness`. Content: country, date, numbers, link to the admin calendar. No personal data.

### 5.4 Rendering & caching (per `plan/01` §3)

- PDP stays ISR (spec 009: 300 s in a live country) and gains tag `delivery:{iso2}`; country-scoped category and shop pages that print a "from" price gain the same tag. A cache hit reads nothing; an ISR miss runs one `dayCapacity` aggregate for the window and one calendar-day read (spec 002 §5.4 holds: the database is never on a cache hit's path).
- **Purges are rare by design**: on a date's state change, on calendar or capacity edits, and at cutoff instants. A placement that leaves remaining above 0 purges nothing. Stale window: at most the 60 s `invalidate()` lag of spec 012b; placement is the authority, so a stale "open" chip can never become a charge for a full date.
- Checkout, the florist area and admin are `no-store`. `?date=` variants keep spec 009's edge rule and are covered by the tag.
- No response varies by cookie or header; capacity numbers never enter HTML, so cached bodies stay identical for every visitor.

## 8. Compliance considerations (mandatory)

- **Price display (EU)**: the Price Indication Directive defines the selling price as "the final price ... including VAT and all other taxes" ([Directive 98/6/EC Art. 2(a)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/1998/6/oj/eng)), and CRD Art. 6(1)(e) requires the total price with all delivery charges before the buyer is bound (`plan/07` §2.1, §4). The date surcharge is printed on the chip before selection, added to the one all-in total, and never appears for the first time at checkout. The "from" price never sits below the cheapest configuration actually buyable (AC-10).
- **Price display (UK)**: the DMCC Act 2024 bans drip pricing from 6 April 2025; the CMA's price transparency guidance (18 November 2025) treats a charge as mandatory when the product cannot be bought without it and says mandatory delivery charges belong in the total price, and where all delivery options are paid the headline price must include the cheapest one ([CMS summary](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/price-transparency-guidance-lands-as-cma-launches-first-consumer-law-investigations-using-its-new-dmcc-act-powers), [Boyes Turner](https://www.boyesturner.com/news-and-insights/drip-pricing-ban-new-rules-now-force)). Our mapping: a date surcharge is avoidable when an unsurcharged date is selectable, so the headline may be the base; when every selectable date is surcharged, the headline includes the smallest surcharge (AC-10). ⚖️ the lawyer confirms this reading for `en-gb`.
- **No fake urgency or scarcity** (`plan/07` §4; Directive 2005/29/EC Annex I point 7, which bans falsely stating that a product will be available only for a very limited time; founder rule): "Fully booked" is shown only from real capacity, never a remaining count, "only N left", "selling fast", a countdown or a viewer count. `PRESSURE_PATTERNS` joins `RELAY_PATTERNS` in `src/config/honesty.ts` and is asserted on every buyer surface in four locales (AC-27). The order-by line is a fixed absolute time, not a timer.
- **Delivery promise** (the Poczta Kwiatowa lesson, §1.1): we never accept an order for a date we have no capacity for, and the terms (spec 041) carry no "no guarantee on peak days" clause. ⚖️ lawyer confirms the terms say nothing of the kind.
- **Charge at once, no capacity-caused charge** (founder 2026-10-14; `plan/09` Phase 2 AC): capacity is checked atomically before the payment intent exists (AC-20), so a full date cannot be charged. Remaining capacity-caused failures (a florist falling ill) keep spec 016's no-partner path; no post-purchase message gains refund or failure framing (founder rule 2026-10-14).
- **Geo-blocking (EU 2018/302, ADR-0006)**: surcharges, capacity and cutoffs are keyed on the destination only; spec 005's signature test (AC-18) extends to `dayCapacity`, `dateSurchargeOn`, `unavoidableSurcharge` and the calendar provider (AC-32).
- **VAT** 🧾: the surcharge is taxed at the product line's rate (treated as part of the single supply of delivered flowers); `vatBreakdown()` stays exact. The accountant confirms (§13 Q9); a different answer is a data change on the order line's rate, not a schema change.
- **Omnibus 30-day lowest price**: unaffected; surcharges are not reductions and no "was/now" exists. Superseded calendar rows keep history for audit.
- **Data flows → RoPA**: one new flow, florist capacity settings (`partner_capacity_day`: who set which capacity, `set_by` user id). Subjects: florist members. Basis: Art. 6(1)(b) (the florist agreement). Retention: 2 years after the date, then deleted by `retention.sweep` (new `retention_policy` row). No buyer or recipient data is added anywhere. `docs/compliance/ropa.md` gains the row.
- **Accessibility (EAA, WCAG 2.1 AA)**: §5.3; reasons in words, not colour; keyboard-operable florist and admin editors.
- **Logs and PII**: capacity events carry country, date and counts only; alert emails carry no personal data; no postcode in any capacity log (spec 010 AC-50's rule).
- **`CLAUDE.md` rules checked**: countries and peak days are data flips (no code per country, AC-6); money is integer minor units plus currency; price shown equals price charged; zod at every boundary; migration with a rollback; status only through `orderService.transition` (this spec writes no status); no IP redirect; no literal strings; logical CSS; `Intl` only. No rule is broken.

## 13. Open questions (each with a recommended default)

1. **Which dates are peaks, and the amounts?** Recommended: PL 14 Feb, 8 Mar, 26 May, 30 and 31 Oct 2027 at 25 zł; UK 14 Feb and Mothering Sunday 14 Mar 2027 at £5; DE 14 Feb and Muttertag 9 May 2027 at €6; `occasion_country.peak` set to match. Other countries at their go-live, in admin.
2. **Earlier cutoffs and Sunday peaks.** Recommended: Valentine's and Women's Day order by 2 days before at 18:00 (`plan/06` §7); Mother's Day and All Saints days 1 day before at 18:00; set PL `sundayDelivery` to `peak` so Sunday 14 Feb 2027 can open, and only for florists who set an override.
3. **Peak on a Sunday: one surcharge or both?** Recommended: the peak surcharge replaces the Sunday one (one date surcharge per date).
4. **Do unpaid `placed` orders hold a slot (up to spec 013's 2 hours)?** Recommended: yes, so a date can never be oversold; the cost is a slot held by an abandoned 3DS page for at most 2 hours.
5. **Default order cap on peak days?** Recommended: none by default (florist sum); the readiness digest suggests a cap and the founder sets it per date.
6. **May florists change their own default daily capacity?** Recommended: yes, never below committed orders; admin sees every change in the audit log.
7. **Should florists see our peak surcharge or get a peak payout?** Recommended: no surcharge shown; payout uplift decided in the payouts spec.
8. **Alert thresholds and digest timing.** Recommended: 80 % and 100 %; digests 14 and 3 days before.
9. 🧾 **VAT on the surcharge.** Recommended (pending the accountant): same rate as the flowers it delivers (PL 8 %); listed on the invoice as its own line.
10. **Preselected date when the earliest is a peak day.** Recommended: keep spec 009's earliest selectable date (it is usually the date the buyer came for), with its fee printed; the alternative (earliest date without a fee) risks a buyer ordering for the 15th by mistake.
11. **Peak block on corridor and country occasion pages** ("Valentine's Day in Poland: order by Fri 12 Feb, 18:00"). Recommended: a small amendment to specs 007/008 after this spec, with its own artboards.
12. **New buyer copy** (founder approval, four locales): "Fully booked"; "Order by {date}, {time} {city} time"; "Every date shown is fully booked. The next date we can deliver in {country} is {date}."; "The next date we can deliver is {date}." Recommended: approve as written.
