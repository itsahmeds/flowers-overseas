## 1. Problem

Phase 0 ends when **three Polish florists have verbally committed after seeing the demo** (`plan/00` §4, `plan/09` Phase 0 AC 9). Nothing on the site speaks to a florist today. The header and footer already draw "For florists" (`src/config/site-links.ts`, id `for-florists`, `published: false`, owning spec `011`), and the design canvas already holds the landing, application and walkthrough wireframes and the florist journey (`docs/design/wireframes/for-florists-*.dc.html`, `docs/design/flows/florist-journey.dc.html`), but there is no page, no form, no table row and no inbox to show on a call. The founder will be on demo calls from 13–16 October (`plan/09`), so this has to ship in about two weeks.

The page has two jobs and one constraint. It recruits: a florist must learn in one screen what we are, what they get paid, what we handle and what we ask (`plan/10` §6), and be able to apply in their own language. It proves: on the call, the founder shows a seeded inbox where an order arrives and is accepted, so the florist sees what working with us will look like (`plan/05` #45). The constraint is honesty. We have no florist, no order and no delivery yet. Reviews and testimonials are real-only (decisions log 2026-09-08), no demo florist appears on an indexable page (`plan/09` AC 6, `plan/10` §2.4), and anything that does not exist yet is written in the future tense (decisions log 2026-10-03, "The florist sentence"). Telling a florist who would be our first that we already have many is the quickest way to lose them.

## 2. Scope

Each bullet is testable; §9 names the AC.

**Pages (`plan/05` #41–#43, `plan/02` §4.1)**

- **Landing** `/{locale}/{forFlorists}`: `/en/for-florists`, `/en-gb/for-florists`, `/pl/dla-kwiaciarni`, `/de/fuer-floristen` (the slugs already in `src/config/locales.data.ts`). Static, server-rendered, indexable by rule. Sections, in the order of the wireframe: hero with the two calls to action (apply; see how an order arrives) · what we handle / what you handle · what you will be paid (one worked example, §13 Q1) and when · what we do not ask for · the four-step "how an order will arrive" summary linking the walkthrough · talk to a person (the existing support line from `src/config/company.ts`) · FAQ (6–10 visible items: exclusivity, minimums, fees, payout timing and currency, saying no, substitutions, the recipient's data, the agreement, when we open) · application call to action.
- **Walkthrough** `/{locale}/{forFlorists}/{walkthrough}`: the four steps (it arrives → accept or decline → make and deliver → one photo) with an example screen for each, rendered from the same components and seed data as the mock inbox and labelled "Example screen". Static, indexable by rule.
- **Application** `/{locale}/{forFlorists}/{apply}` plus two result pages, `…/{apply}/{sent}` and `…/{apply}/{notSent}`. Static, always `noindex,follow`.
- New path-segment keys `forFloristsWalkthrough`, `forFloristsApply`, `forFloristsApplySent`, `forFloristsApplyNotSent` in `PATH_SEGMENT_KEYS`, authored by hand per locale (defaults: en `how-orders-work`, `apply`, `sent`, `not-sent`; pl `jak-dzialaja-zamowienia`, `zgloszenie`, `wyslane`, `nie-wyslane`; de `so-funktionieren-auftraege`, `bewerbung`, `gesendet`, `nicht-gesendet`; `en-gb` inherits `en`), resolved by the existing `resolveLocalePath()` so the depth-1/2/3 route files stay the only route files.
- All four launch locales render all pages (the header link exists in every locale). Indexability per locale is decided by the engine (§6), never by a branch here.

**The application (`plan/05` #42, `plan/10` §6, spec 002 `partner_application`)**

- A plain HTML `<form method="post">` to `POST /api/florist-applications` that works with JavaScript off. Fields (zod `FloristApplicationSchema`, §5.2): business name · registration number · country (select: the destinations in `src/config/countries.ts`, Poland first) · city · districts or postcodes you deliver to · orders a day you could take · your name · email · phone / WhatsApp · how you would like orders to reach you (WhatsApp or email) · Instagram or website (optional) · a required, unticked acknowledgement box. The page locale is sent as a hidden field. No photo upload (§13 Q3). No bank or payout detail of any kind.
- Every field carries the HTML constraints that mirror its zod rule (`required`, `maxlength`, `type="email"`, `type="tel"`, `inputmode`, `min`/`max`, `autocomplete`), so the browser catches what the server would reject before anything is sent.
- **Server**: the handler checks `Origin` against the site origin, drops honeypot submissions silently (answering as if they succeeded), applies a per-instance rate limit of 5 submissions per hour per truncated IP (in memory; one replica under ADR-0018), parses with `FloristApplicationSchema`, normalises the phone to E.164 with the chosen country as the default prefix, inserts one `partner_application` row with `status = 'new'`, and answers **303** to the localised `sent` page. Any rejection (validation, rate limit, origin, database) answers **303** to the localised `notSent` page. No personal data is ever placed in a URL, a log line or an error report.
- **One small client island**, `ApplicationFormEnhancer` (≤2 048 B Brotli, apply page only, **no network access**): on submit it runs the browser's own constraint check and, instead of the browser's bubbles, shows our localised message under each invalid field (messages embedded in the server HTML), an error summary at the top, and moves focus to the first error. Submission stays the native form post. With JavaScript off the page still works, with the browser's own messages.
- **Result pages** say what happens next and by when (§13 Q9): `sent` — we read it, we reply within the promised working days whether yes or no, a call before anything else, a test order before a real one, "you are never live because a page went up"; `notSent` — it was not sent, go back to the form, or call or WhatsApp us on the support line.
- **Reading applications** before spec 012's admin exists: `pnpm florists:applications` prints `new` and `contacted` applications from the database to the founder's terminal and can set a status (`contacted`, `rejected`, `converted`). It runs under `withAdminContext`, never in a request path, and prints nothing to logs.

**The mock florist inbox (`plan/05` #45, `plan/10` §2.4 and §6)**

- `/demo/vendor-inbox` — a two-link language chooser (Polski · English), like spec 003's `/`. Under it, `/demo/vendor-inbox/{lang}` (`lang ∈ pl, en`) is the inbox, `/demo/vendor-inbox/{lang}/{ref}` one order, and `/demo/vendor-inbox/{lang}/{ref}/{step}` with `step ∈ accepted | declined | photo | done` the screens after a choice. Every screen is static HTML built from seed data; every button is a link or a `GET` form to the next screen, so it works on a florist's phone with JavaScript off and nothing is ever sent or stored.
- **Seed data**: three sample orders in `src/modules/partners/demo/inbox.data.ts`, zod-validated (`DemoInboxSchema`): a same-day Warszawa bouquet, a next-day bouquet with an add-on, and a funeral piece. Products are referenced by real catalogue SKU (names, tiers and images come from the catalogue, not retyped); recipients and messages are invented and every screen carries a fixed banner: "Sample order. Nothing you press here is sent." No demo florist is named: the inbox belongs to "your shop".
- **What each order shows** (`plan/06` §5.3, `plan/07` §1.3): date and delivery window, district, product and tier with stems, add-ons, the substitution rule for that product, the card message to print, the recipient's full name, phone and address, the payout for this order in złoty, and the "answer by" time. **Never** the buyer's name, email or phone. Decline shows the reason picklist and "no penalty". Accept shows "make and deliver by…" and the photo step. The photo step asks two separate questions — the delivery photo (demo: "nothing is uploaded") and whether we may show it publicly (default no). Done says the buyer will receive the photo and the payout will appear on that week's statement.
- **Gated by environment**: `ENABLE_DEMO_INBOX` (`"true"` | `"false"`, absent = off), refused in `production` by `validateEnv()` exactly as `ENABLE_DEV_UI` is; with it off every `/demo/*` path answers 404. Staging and previews keep the existing `STAGING_BASIC_AUTH` password (§13 Q2). Every `/demo/*` page answers `noindex,follow` through the engine (the site's only non-index directive), and `/demo/` joins `DISALLOWED_PATHS`.
- **A seam for spec 010**: the inbox reads its orders through `DemoInboxSource` (`list(lang)`, `get(lang, ref)`), shipped with one implementation, `seedDemoInbox`. Spec 010's mock checkout adds a source with the order a buyer just placed, without changing a page.

**Links, schema, sitemap**

- `site-links.ts` `for-florists` flips to `published: true` (header end cluster and footer). The landing links out to the Poland destination page (`plan/02` §11 "For florists → corridors for countries where we recruit"), the walkthrough and the application; the walkthrough links back to the landing and the application.
- JSON-LD: `WebPage` + `FAQPage` (visible Q&A only) + `BreadcrumbList` on the landing; `WebPage` + `BreadcrumbList` on the walkthrough; nothing on the apply, result and demo pages. Never `JobPosting`, `LocalBusiness` or `FloristShop` (`plan/02` §9).
- The landing and walkthrough join the locale's `static.xml` child through the existing registry in `src/modules/seo/sitemap/statics.ts`, membership decided by `pageIndexability()` only.

**Honesty and copy**

- Copy speaks as Flowers Overseas in the first person and keeps spec 004 §14 A5's nine banned words out of every value, the florist pages included: "the florists we work with", "work with us", never "partner", "network" or "vendor" (§13 Q8). `/demo/vendor-inbox` is a route, not copy.
- Future tense for everything that does not exist yet: orders, payouts, statements, the agreement page, opening dates. No count of florists, orders, deliveries or countries, no testimonial, no shop logo, no stats strip (the wireframe's placeholder stats row is removed in the design round, §12).
- New English strings go to the founder as one batch with their exact text and are marked reviewed only on the founder's approval (decisions log 2026-10-03, "Copy review, the 5 % gate"). Polish is written by hand, not machine-echoed, and is marked reviewed only after a native speaker's review (`plan/13` B12; §13 Q6).

## 3. Non-goals

- **The real magic-link flow** (`/v/{token}` accept, decline, photo upload, routing, SLAs) → specs 016 and 017. The demo shows it; it does not build it.
- **A mock order placed in checkout landing in the inbox within 5 s** (`plan/09` Phase 0 AC 4) → spec 010, through `DemoInboxSource` (§2).
- **Admin review of applications** (list, status, notes, convert to `fulfillment_partner`) → spec 012. Phase 0 has the CLI only.
- **Emails** — no confirmation to the applicant, no notification to the founder (§13 Q7). Resend arrives with spec 017.
- **Photo upload in the application** (§13 Q3), **bank or payout details**, **the partner agreement page** (`plan/05` #44, Phase 1, lawyer-gated `plan/13` B3), **"Our florists" and florist profiles** (`plan/05` #26/#27, Phase 1/2, opt-in).
- **A payout calculator** ("3 orders a week × 149 zł", `plan/10` §6) — one worked example is enough for the demo; a calculator is an island on an indexable page for little gain.
- **GA4 events** `florist_lead_start` / `florist_lead_submit` (`plan/04` §12) — GA4 stays off until the founder supplies the measurement id (decisions log 2026-09-16). §11's server log line counts submissions meanwhile.
- **A florist link on the Poland destination page** — the header and footer carry the link; editing the corridor guide is spec 007's content and can follow.
- **A reviewed German translation** — `de` renders the pages and stays `noindex` (§13 Q6).
- **A CAPTCHA.** Honeypot, origin check and rate limit only.

## 4. User stories

- As a **florist in Warszawa** who found us on Google or through the founder's message, I read in Polish what you do, what I would be paid and what you would ask of me, so I can decide whether to talk to you.
- As a **florist on a demo call**, I open a link on my phone and see an order arrive, accept it and add the photo, so I know exactly what working with you will look like before I agree to anything.
- As a **florist who wants to apply**, I fill in a short form with no bank details, on my phone, with or without JavaScript, and I am told what happens next and by when.
- As the **founder**, I see every application in the database the moment it is sent and can mark it contacted, rejected or converted, so I can follow up the same day.
- As **Googlebot**, I find a server-rendered, self-canonical page with honest copy and visible FAQ markup in each reviewed locale, and I never find the demo inbox or the application form in a sitemap.

### 5.1 Data model changes (tables, columns, migrations + rollback)

`partner_application` is defined by spec 002 §5.1 and is not yet created (TASK-018, `todo`). This spec needs it now and needs four more columns. Three amendments to spec 002, recorded in its §14 by the task that ships them:

1. **Columns.** `partner_application` gains `contact_name text NOT NULL`, `privacy_notice_version text NOT NULL`, `acknowledged_at timestamptz NOT NULL`, `created_at timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now()`, `updated_at timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now()` (the `0001` updated-at trigger) and `status_changed_at timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now()`. `preferred_channel` gets `CHECK IN ('whatsapp','email')`; `capacity_per_day` gets `CHECK (capacity_per_day BETWEEN 1 AND 200)`; `contact_email`, `contact_phone` (E.164), `business_name`, `city`, `country_id`, `language_code` become `NOT NULL`; `media_asset_ids` stays, nullable, always empty in Phase 0. No `email` index, no free-text column without a length `CHECK`.
2. **Where it is created (§13 Q4 default).** This spec's backend task creates `partner_application` alone in the next free migration version (`NNNN_partner_application.sql` + `.down.sql`, `SET LOCAL ROLE app_owner;` preamble, FKs to `country` and `locale` from `0002`), and TASK-018's partners migration no longer creates it; spec 002's later planned versions shift by one. Rollback: `DROP TABLE partner_application;` in the `.down.sql`, round-tripped by `db:migrate` → `db:rollback` → `db:migrate` on an empty PG16.
3. **Access and retention.** When TASK-023's RLS migration lands, `partner_application` is insert-only for `app_web` (no `SELECT`, `UPDATE` or `DELETE`) and readable and writable under the admin context only. TASK-021's `retention_policy` seed gains a row: applications not `converted` are deleted 12 months after `status_changed_at` (§13 Q5), applied by TASK-025's sweep; until that job exists, `docs/runbooks/florist-applications.md` holds the manual step.

No other table changes. The demo inbox and the walkthrough read no table.

### 5.2 API / server actions / jobs / events (zod schemas named)

- **`FloristApplicationSchema`** (`src/modules/partners/application.ts`): `businessName` 1–120 · `registrationNumber` 3–40 · `countryIso2` ∈ the destinations of `countries.ts` · `city` 1–80 · `coverageNote` 1–500 · `capacityPerDay` integer 1–200 · `contactName` 1–120 · `contactEmail` email ≤254 · `contactPhone` 6–32, normalised to E.164 · `preferredChannel` ∈ `whatsapp | email` · `link` optional `https:` URL ≤200, routed to `instagram_url` when its host is `instagram.com` or `www.instagram.com`, else `website_url` · `locale` ∈ launch locales · `acknowledged` literal `"on"` · `website` (honeypot) must be empty. Every string is trimmed and NFC-normalised; control characters are rejected. `toPartnerApplicationRow()` projects a parsed value onto the §5.1 columns, pinned by a unit test on the column list.
- **`POST /api/florist-applications`** (`src/app/api/florist-applications/route.ts`, thin; the logic is `submitFloristApplication()` in `src/modules/partners`): `application/x-www-form-urlencoded` only, body ≤16 KB, `Origin` must equal the deployment origin, `Cache-Control: no-store`. Outcomes: `sent` (row inserted) → 303 to `sent`; `honeypot` → 303 to `sent`, no row; `invalid` | `rateLimited` | `badOrigin` | `unavailable` → 303 to `notSent`. The outcome is an enum, `FloristApplicationOutcome`, and it is the only thing logged (§11).
- **`DemoInboxSchema`** (`src/modules/partners/demo/`): `{ ref, lang, deliveryDate, window, district, sku, tierKey, addons[], cardMessage, recipient: { fullName, phoneE164, addressLines[], postalCode, city }, payout: { amountMinor, currency }, answerBy }`. `sku` and `tierKey` must exist in the catalogue; `payout` is integer minor units plus ISO currency. `DemoInboxSource` interface + `seedDemoInbox`. `src/modules/partners/demo/` is its own entry point that imports no database client, and joins `check:no-db`'s `SCANNED_PATHS`, so the static pages that read it never pull the client in.
- **`FloristPitchSchema`** (`src/config/florist-pitch.ts`): the one record the landing's worked example and the demo's payouts read — `exampleSku`, `exampleTierKey`, `buyerPays` and `payout` as `{ amountMinor, currency: "PLN" }`, and `replyWorkingDays` (§13 Q1, Q9). The example's buyer price must equal the catalogue's PL price for that SKU and tier, asserted by a unit test, so the page cannot quote a price the shop does not charge.
- **CLI** `pnpm florists:applications [--status new|contacted] [--set <id> <status>]` (`scripts/florist-applications.ts`): reads and updates under `withAdminContext`; status changes set `status_changed_at`.
- No job, no event, no outbox row.

## 6. SEO considerations (mandatory)

- **Indexability — one engine.** Register four page types in `PAGE_TYPE_POLICY`: `forFlorists` and `forFloristsWalkthrough` as `"byRule"`; `forFloristsApply` (apply and both result pages) and `demoInbox` as `"never"`. For the two `byRule` types: `exists` = the page renders in that locale; `reviewed` = every key of the page's message namespace (`forFlorists.*`, plus the shared `forFlorists.steps.*` for the walkthrough) is reviewed in that locale's meta manifest; `localeIndexable` = spec 003's `isLocaleIndexable()`; `indexingEnvironment` = spec 007's gate. No `noindex` literal outside `modules/seo`.
- **What that means in Phase 0.** `en` and `en-gb` become indexable at spec 007's indexing flip once the founder approves the English batch. `pl` needs both the Polish copy reviewed **and** `isLocaleIndexable('pl')` (the whole Polish catalogue ≤5% unreviewed), so it may stay `noindex` after the flip; that does not block the exit criterion, which is about calls, not rankings. `de` stays `noindex` (§13 Q6).
- **Canonical** self, absolute, without parameters. **hreflang** from `alternatesFor()` over the localised path template, so a locale appears only where indexable, reciprocal by construction, `x-default → /en/for-florists`.
- **URL pattern** per `plan/02` §4.1 (`/pl/dla-kwiaciarni`, `/de/fuer-floristen`), child slugs authored by hand per locale (§2), never machine-translated (decisions log 2026-10-03, SPEC-008 A12).
- **Titles** ≤60 characters, descriptions authored per locale. Search intent targeted in copy where it reads naturally: "florists wanted", "zlecenia dla kwiaciarni", "Aufträge für Floristen". "Florist partnership" is not targeted: "partner" is a banned word (§13 Q8).
- **Schema** per §2: `WebPage` + `FAQPage` + `BreadcrumbList`; `FAQPage` only for Q&A visible on the page; validated by the existing `seo:validate` fixtures.
- **Sitemap**: landing and walkthrough in `static.xml` only when indexable; apply, result and demo pages never.
- **Robots**: `/demo/` added to `DISALLOWED_PATHS`. The apply pages are **not** disallowed, so their `noindex` can be seen.
- **Internal links**: in from the header end cluster and the footer "Company" column in every locale (crawl depth 1 from every page); out to the Poland destination page, the walkthrough and the application.
- **Thin content**: the landing carries ≥500 words of florist-specific copy plus 6–10 FAQ items, and the walkthrough four steps of real mechanics; neither repeats the buyer "how it works" page.
- **CWV**: no new image beyond one optional hero photo slot (founder-supplied, through the existing media pipeline, or the placeholder); no island on indexable pages; the landing joins the Lighthouse URL set at ≥95 mobile.

## 7. i18n considerations (mandatory)

- **Namespaces**: `forFlorists.*` (landing, FAQ, walkthrough, apply, results, page metadata), `forFlorists.steps.*` (shared by the walkthrough and the inbox), `demoInbox.*`, `form.*` (generic field and error strings for the new primitives). Message keys only; no literal in a component (`fo/no-literal-strings`).
- **Polish plurals** through ICU (`1 zamówienie / 2 zamówienia / 5 zamówień`); informal "Ty" register in UI per spec 003 §13 Q2; the glossary gains "kwiaciarnia" and its forms.
- **Formatting**: money via `formatMoney` from integer minor units (`149 zł`), dates via `formatDate(…, "deliveryDate")`, times via `formatTimeInZone` with `Europe/Warsaw` named. No float, no hand-built date.
- **Phone**: the field accepts the international form and the narrow local shapes of spec 001 A20 (PL 3-3-3), normalised to E.164 with the chosen country as the default prefix; the hint says "with country code if you are outside Poland".
- **Addresses**: none collected in the form (city and a free-text coverage note only). The demo recipient address is formatted with spec 003's PL address format.
- **RTL**: no RTL locale at launch; logical properties only, checked by the `pseudo-rtl` screenshot of the landing.
- **Review and gating**: English to the founder in one batch; Polish hand-written and native-reviewed (`plan/13` B12); German left to `i18n:draft` and unreviewed. `sourceHash` drift re-flags a changed English source; the `i18n-check` step summary shows `forFlorists` per locale. Gating is §6's engine.
- **Demo language**: `pl` and `en` only, chosen by path; the inbox is drafted in Polish first because the people who see it are Polish florists.

## 8. Compliance considerations (mandatory)

- **New data flow → RoPA row** in `docs/compliance/ropa.md`: *Florist applications* — data subjects: florists and their contact person; data: business name, registration number, city, coverage note, capacity, contact name, email, phone, preferred channel, optional link, language, acknowledgement time and notice version; purpose: assess the application and take steps before a possible agreement; basis: **Art. 6(1)(b)** (steps at the request of the data subject before entering a contract) for sole traders, **Art. 6(1)(f)** for a company's contact person; recipients: Neon (processor, Frankfurt, row 6); retention: 12 months after the last status change unless converted, then the partner agreement's retention (`plan/07` §1.2 "Florist personal data … contract"); no transfer outside the EU beyond row 6's terms.
- **Consent wording.** The basis is not consent; the required box records that the applicant read the notice and agrees to be contacted about the application, with `privacy_notice_version` and `acknowledged_at` stored. No marketing opt-in on this form. The short notice beside the button says what is stored, why, for how long, that it is never shared with buyers, and where to ask for deletion (the support line until a privacy address exists), and links the privacy policy when it is published (`plan/13` B4).
- **Minimisation**: no bank, payout, ID or tax-residence data; no photos; no free-text "anything else" box. The demo inbox shows the florist only what delivery needs and never the buyer (`plan/07` §1.3), modelling the real view.
- **No PII in logs, URLs or analytics**: the handler logs the outcome enum, the new row's id, the country and the locale only; the result pages carry no query string; Sentry scrubbing (spec 001 AC-13) already drops request bodies. A test posts a full application and asserts that no submitted value appears in any log line.
- **Honesty (UCPD / Omnibus)**: no testimonials, reviews, logos or counts; the payout example is labelled as an example, not an average; everything not yet live is in the future tense; the demo carries its "sample" banner on every screen; demo recipients are invented and say so.
- **Price display**: the example's buyer price equals the catalogue's all-in PL price (VAT and delivery included) for that product and tier (§5.2), so a florist and a buyer see the same number.
- **Accessibility (WCAG 2.1 AA, `plan/07` §8)**: labels, hints and errors programmatically linked; error summary with focus management; `autocomplete` tokens (`organization`, `name`, `email`, `tel`, `address-level2`, `url`); the form is fully keyboard-operable; the result pages have a heading that says what happened; demo screens are real headings and links, not images of screens; axe clean on every new page in both directions.
- **Partner agreement**: the landing promises the agreement will be shown before anything is signed; the agreement itself (Art. 28 processor clauses, payout, SLA, photo, substitution) is `plan/13` B3, lawyer-gated, out of scope.

## 13. Open questions

Each carries the default this spec is written against; the spec stays `draft` until the founder rules.

- **Q1 — What payout do we show publicly, and how?** The plan disagrees with itself: `plan/10` §2.3 gives a florist share of 55–62% by tier (classic 58%), `plan/06` §3 says 55–65% of the price *before* VAT, and the same section's worked example and the current wireframe show **149 zł on a 219 zł bouquet (68%)** — and 219 zł is not a price we charge: Poland's classic ladder is 199 / 229 / 259 zł (`src/config/catalogue/prices.data.ts`). **Recommended: one worked example, a real Polish classic-tier bouquet at its catalogue price, showing only "the buyer pays" and "you are paid" as fixed złoty amounts, with no percentage and no "what we keep" line** (that line depends on the VAT model the accountant has not yet confirmed, `plan/06` §4). Default amount: **133 zł on a 229 zł bouquet** (58%, `plan/10`'s classic share, rounded to the złoty). Whatever you choose is also the seed payout the demo shows and the number the first three florists validate (`plan/13` A4, B13). If you would rather lead with **149 zł on 229 zł** (65%), say so: it is more attractive to a florist and leaves us about €13 a bouquet before marketing, against about €17 at 133 zł (8% Polish VAT on flowers, card and payout fees as in `plan/06` §3, 4.3 zł to the euro; the VAT figure assumes the accountant confirms Model A).
- **Q2 — Where does the demo inbox live?** **Recommended: staging and PR previews only**, behind the existing staging password; production answers 404. You give the florist the link and the password on the call. Alternative: production with its own password — one more secret and one more surface on the public domain for no gain.
- **Q3 — Photos of their work in the application?** `plan/10` §6 asks for three. **Recommended: not in Phase 0; an optional Instagram or website link instead.** Uploads need storage writes, file checks, EXIF stripping and a deletion path before the first florist has applied; the call is where you see their work. Alternative: up to three photos through the R2 pipeline — about two more days.
- **Q4 — Where is the application stored?** The `partner_application` table belongs to a schema task that has not started (TASK-018, behind TASK-017). **Recommended: this spec creates that one table itself in the next migration version, and spec 002's later versions shift by one** (§5.1). Alternative: wait for TASK-017 and TASK-018 — the form cannot ship until both do.
- **Q5 — How long do we keep an application that does not become a florist we work with?** **Recommended: 12 months after its last status change, then deleted.** Long enough to come back to a florist who applied before we opened their city; short enough to defend.
- **Q6 — Polish and German.** **Recommended: Polish is written by hand by the fleet, reviewed by a native speaker before the first demo call, and indexable once reviewed; German renders from the machine draft and stays `noindex` until you commission a review.** The roadmap lists German for this page (`plan/09` AC 5); the first florists are Polish. Who is the Polish reviewer, and can they turn about 120 strings around by 12 October?
- **Q7 — How do you hear about a new application?** **Recommended: no email in Phase 0; you run `pnpm florists:applications` (or ask the orchestrator to) once a day while recruiting, and spec 012's admin replaces it.** Alternative: an email to you per application with only its id and city — needs a Resend key and the sending domain verified now rather than in spec 017.
- **Q8 — May the florist pages say "partner"?** It is one of the nine banned words, and the ban covers every message value. **Recommended: keep the ban; write "the florists we work with" and "work with us" (Polish "współpraca").** We lose "florist partnership" as a search phrase; "florists wanted" and "zlecenia dla kwiaciarni" remain. Alternative: exempt the `forFlorists.*` namespace from the scan for that one word.
- **Q9 — What do we promise about replies and contact?** **Recommended: "we reply within 3 working days, yes or no", and the existing support line and hours from `company.ts`**, not the wireframe's separate florist hours (07:30–17:00) and not a `florists@` address that does not exist yet. Tell us a different number of days, or whether a florist mailbox should be created.
