## 1. Problem

We sell gifts, paid by card, delivered to someone who is not the cardholder, often in another country and often the same or next day. That is the profile fraudsters look for: a stolen card buys something that leaves our hands before the real cardholder sees the statement, and the chargeback arrives weeks later with the bouquet long delivered and the florist long paid. Card testers also probe any public pay step, and their declines hurt the approval rate of every honest buyer. Stripe's own guidance says a "ship to" name that differs from the cardholder is "an additional dispute risk", and rush delivery is a classic fraud signal. Under the founder's ruling of 2026-10-14 we charge at once and never hold a card (spec 013 §14), so the money moves at placement and the only thing left to control before a loss is **the moment we hand the order to a florist**.

Spec 013 ships Radar metadata, 3DS on every card and the dispute rows, and points everything else here (013 §3: "Our own fraud pre-checks, the manual-review queue, chargeback evidence packs → spec 021"). Spec 016 expects this spec to "hold the payment-secured hand-off" (016 §3). `plan/06` §2.3 lists the signals; `plan/09` Phase 1 acceptance 5 needs Radar rules active and a review queue exercised before the phase closes. Nothing of this exists yet.

The catch for us: a buyer in London paying with a UK card for a bouquet in Kraków is our normal order, not a suspicious one. Every rule below is written so that a mismatch between card, buyer and recipient country never counts on its own.

## 2. Scope

**A. What Radar does, and the rules we add**
- A documented Radar rule set (§5.2 "Radar"), entered by the founder from the runbook (Radar Plus is on from the first live order, spec 013 §13 Q3). A test checks the runbook's rule block against the metadata spec 013 sends and against the geo-blocking guard.

**B. Pay-step pre-checks (before any money moves)**
- Disposable email refused at checkout step 1 in `test` and `live`, with privacy relays allowed.
- Blocked buyer email refused at the pay step with no PaymentIntent created.
- At most 5 declined attempts per order; then the order is closed unpaid.
- An email whose last 3 orders in 24 hours ended unpaid is refused at the pay step.
- A site-wide card-testing detector that alerts the founder and pauses new payments for 30 minutes in `live`.

**C. The risk gate before routing (after the money is taken)**
- A pure scoring function over non-personal inputs, a versioned rule set, and three decisions: **pass** (route now), **hold** (the order waits for a human, with a deadline tied to the delivery cutoff), **cancel** (cancel and refund in full, automatic only for decisive signals).
- The gate wraps spec 016's `routing` outbox handler; spec 016's routing logic is unchanged.
- Shadow mode (`risk.gate` off): assess and record, never act.

**D. The admin review queue**
- `/admin/risk` and `/admin/risk/[reviewId]`: approve (route now), cancel and refund, or cancel, refund and block. Every status change goes through spec 013's `cancelOrder` and `refund`, which call `orderService.transition` (ADR-0009).
- Deadline enforcement and alerts.

**E. After routing**
- Early fraud warnings (EFW) from the card network, handled by the order's state.
- A chargeback with reason "fraudulent" blocks the buyer's email and card for later orders.

**F. The chargeback evidence pack**
- A PDF built on demand from immutable records (timeline, 3DS result, delivery photo, delivery address, terms shown at checkout), for the founder to upload in Stripe's dashboard.

**G. Privacy records**
- Keyed hashes instead of raw identifiers, retention rows, a legitimate interest assessment, RoPA rows, and the privacy-page paragraph on automated decisions (requirement on spec 041).

## 3. Non-goals

- **Holding a card or delaying the charge.** Never (founder, 2026-10-14). The hold here is on the florist offer only; the buyer is already charged.
- **Any buyer-facing sign of a check.** No "security review", "verification" or "pending" state anywhere a buyer looks (§5.3, AC-29, AC-30).
- **Contacting the buyer to verify identity** (calls, ID upload, selfie). The founder may do it by hand from the order detail; no flow is built. Later fraud tuning is `plan/09` Phase 4 ("fraud tuning").
- **Reading the visitor's IP address or a device fingerprint in our code.** Device and IP velocity are Radar's, which sees them through Stripe.js (spec 013 §8). We store neither (spec 002 §8 keeps "no full IP or device fingerprint column").
- **Submitting dispute evidence through Stripe's API.** Phase 1 is a downloaded PDF uploaded by the founder (§13 Q8). The restricted key keeps "Disputes read" only (spec 013 §8).
- **Closing Stripe's own Radar reviews from our queue.** The runbook says to close them in the dashboard; our queue is the decision of record.
- **Mollie's fraud tooling** → spec 014, which must call the same `prePayCheck` and feed the same gate (§12).
- **Alert channels** (WhatsApp, email routing of Sentry alerts) → spec 025. This spec raises tagged Sentry alerts the way spec 013 does.
- **A third-party fraud vendor, CAPTCHA of our own, address-type lookup** (hotel, hospital, prison). Stripe's Payment Element already brings its own CAPTCHA triggers during card-testing attacks. Revisit after 100 live orders (§13 Q9).
- **Refund amounts other than full** for a risk decision. Partial refunds stay spec 020's.

## 4. User stories

- As a **buyer** sending flowers to my mother in Poland, I pay once, see "Thank you. We have your order.", and never notice that a check ran, even when my card, my address and her address are in three countries.
- As a **buyer whose order was stopped by mistake**, I am told plainly that the order could not be delivered and that I have the full amount back, with nothing that suggests I did something wrong; I can call or message us and a person looks at it.
- As a **buyer who typed a throwaway email**, I am asked kindly for an address I will keep, because that is where my confirmation and updates go.
- As a **cardholder whose card was stolen**, the fraudster's order is refunded before a florist makes it, so I do not need to file a chargeback.
- As the **founder**, I see a short queue of held orders with the reasons in plain words and a deadline, I decide with one tap on my phone, and an order I do not get to in time is decided by a safe default.
- As the **founder facing a chargeback**, I download one PDF with everything the bank needs and upload it in Stripe.
- As a **florist**, I am never offered an order that is about to be cancelled for fraud.
- As **Googlebot**, I see nothing new.

## 5. Design

### 5.1 Data model changes (tables, columns, migrations + rollback)

One migration of this spec, `NNNN_risk_021.sql` with `NNNN_risk_021.down.sql` (next free number when it merges). New module `risk`, so the tables are new and touch no table of spec 002 or 013 except through A11 below.

1. **`risk_identity`**: `id uuid PK`, `order_id uuid FK → orders(id)`, `kind text CHECK IN ('email','card','recipient_address')`, `hash bytea NOT NULL CHECK (octet_length(hash) = 32)`, `created_at timestamptz NOT NULL DEFAULT now()`, `expires_at timestamptz NOT NULL`; `UNIQUE (order_id, kind, hash)`; index `(kind, hash, created_at)`. The hash is HMAC-SHA-256 under `RISK_HASH_KEY` of the normalised value (§5.2). No raw email, card fingerprint or address is stored anywhere in this module.
2. **`risk_assessment`**: `id uuid PK`, `order_id FK`, `stage text CHECK IN ('routing','post_routing')`, `mode text CHECK IN ('test','live')`, `rules_version integer NOT NULL`, `score smallint NOT NULL CHECK (score BETWEEN 0 AND 100)`, `decision text CHECK IN ('pass','hold','cancel')`, `enforced boolean NOT NULL` (false in shadow mode), `signals jsonb NOT NULL` (validated by `RiskSignalsV1`: an array of `{ id, points }` with ids from §5.2's table and no values), `liability_shift boolean NULL`, `radar_risk_level text NULL`, `radar_risk_score smallint NULL`, `radar_outcome text NULL`, `created_at`.
3. **`risk_review`**: `id uuid PK`, `order_id FK`, `assessment_id FK → risk_assessment`, `kind text CHECK IN ('pre_routing','post_routing')`, `status text CHECK IN ('open','approved','cancelled','auto_released','auto_cancelled','auto_refunded','superseded')`, `recommendation text CHECK IN ('approve','cancel','refund')`, `deadline_at timestamptz NULL` (null only for a post-production review), `due_soon_alerted_at timestamptz NULL`, `decided_at timestamptz NULL`, `decided_by text NULL` (an `ActorSchema` value), `decision_reason text NULL CHECK IN ('looks_genuine','buyer_contacted','card_testing','stolen_card_suspected','blocklist_match','efw','deadline_default','superseded','other')`, `created_at`. Partial unique index: one open review per order, `UNIQUE (order_id) WHERE status = 'open'`. `CHECK ((status = 'open') = (decided_at IS NULL))`.
4. **`risk_blocklist`**: `kind text CHECK IN ('email','card')`, `hash bytea` (32 bytes), `source text CHECK IN ('dispute','admin','efw')`, `created_at`, `last_matched_at timestamptz NULL`, `expires_at timestamptz NOT NULL`; `PRIMARY KEY (kind, hash)`.
5. **`risk_pause`**: `id uuid PK`, `cause text CHECK IN ('card_testing','admin')`, `starts_at`, `ends_at` (`CHECK (ends_at > starts_at)`), `created_at`. Read by `prePayCheck`; written by `risk.monitor` (automatic) or by the `risk.pay_pause` flag's admin toggle (spec 012's flags page records that toggle; the row is the runtime fact).

RLS on all five, policies for `system` and `admin` only; partners never see them. Spec 012's `audit_row_change()` trigger is attached to `risk_review` and `risk_blocklist` (the two tables admin writes). No money column: the thresholds live in config as integer minor units per currency (§5.2).

**Spec 002 §14 A11 (proposed; raised by this spec, shipped by this migration):**
- (a) Two **status-preserving** events in the catalogue, written only by `appendEvent`: `order.risk_held` and `order.risk_released`. The catalogue grows to 39 names (28 transition, 11 status-preserving); no `order_transition` row is added, spec 010 AC-22's 28 transition events are unchanged, and spec 002 AC-14's fixture lists the two names.
- (b) `refund.reason` `CHECK` gains `risk_cancel` (a precautionary cancel; no provider fraud flag) and `fraud_confirmed` (Stripe's refund `reason: 'fraudulent'`, which Stripe documents adds the card fingerprint and the payment's email to Radar's default block lists).
- (c) `retention_policy` rows: `risk_identity` email and card, 12 months (`plan/07` §1.2 fraud row); `risk_identity` recipient address, 90 days (aligned with recipient contact data, `plan/07` §1.3); `risk_assessment` and `risk_review`, 12 months; `risk_blocklist`, 12 months after `last_matched_at` or `created_at`; `risk_pause`, 30 days.
- (d) Only if §13 Q7 is answered yes: the recipient pseudonymisation sweep keeps the recipient's name and delivery address (not phone, not card message) until 180 days after delivery, for the evidence pack.

**Rollback** (`NNNN_risk_021.down.sql`): if any `refund` row carries `risk_cancel` or `fraud_confirmed`, raise `A11_ROLLBACK_REASON_IN_USE` and change nothing (a refund is part of the accounting record, as spec 013's A7 guard). Otherwise: detach the audit triggers, drop the five tables, restore the `refund.reason` check, delete the A11 retention rows. The two catalogue names are fixture and registry data with no database object. Spec 002's migrate, rollback, migrate round trip runs over it.

`app/` imports `risk`'s public `index.ts` only; `risk` imports only the public `index.ts` of `orders`, `payments`, `notifications` (none at launch) and `admin` (`plan/01` §5).

**Normalisation and hashing (pure, unit-tested)**
- `normaliseEmail(email)`: trim, NFC, lowercase; IDN domain to punycode; remove a `+tag` from the local part for every domain; remove dots from the local part for `gmail.com` and `googlemail.com` only, and treat `googlemail.com` as `gmail.com`.
- `normaliseAddress({ countryIso2, postcode, line1 })`: country uppercase; postcode uppercase with spaces and hyphens removed; line 1 NFKD with combining marks removed, plus an explicit fold for letters NFKD does not decompose (`ł → l`, `ß → ss`, `ø → o`, `đ → d`), casefolded, punctuation removed, whitespace collapsed. Name, phone and line 2 are not part of it.
- `identityHash(kind, value) → Buffer`: HMAC-SHA-256 under `RISK_HASH_KEY` (32 random bytes, base64, validated by `lib/env.ts`, in `.env.example`), over `kind + ':' + value`. The card value is Stripe's `card.fingerprint`. Rotating the key forgets history; the runbook says so.

**What Radar already does (no work here)**
- Scores every card payment with Stripe's machine-learning model (`risk_level`: `normal`, `elevated`, `highest`; `risk_score` 0 to 100) and blocks `highest` by default.
- Card-testing protection on the Payment Element: rate limiters, models and CAPTCHA triggers during an attack, shown as "Blocked by Stripe". Stripe's frames render the CAPTCHA, so spec 013's step-3 CSP is expected to hold; task 1 confirms it on staging.
- Device and IP signals through Stripe.js (`__stripe_mid`, `__stripe_sid`, spec 013 §8), velocity counters per card, email, IP and customer, EFW notifications, and default block lists fed by refunds marked fraudulent.
- 3DS: spec 013 already sends `request_three_d_secure: 'any'` on every card (013 §13 Q1), so no "Request 3DS" rule is needed.

**Radar rules we add** (entered by the founder from `docs/runbooks/fraud-review.md`, verbatim; Radar's rule editor preview is run on each before saving and any syntax the editor rejects is reported in the task, not improvised):

| # | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | `Block if :declined_charges_per_ip_address_hourly: >= 5` | Card testing from one connection |
| R2 | `Block if :card_count_for_ip_address_daily: >= 4` | Many cards from one connection |
| R3 | `Block if :declined_charges_per_card_number_daily: >= 4` | One card tried again and again |
| R4 | `Block if :email_count_for_ip_address_hourly: >= 4` | Identity cycling |
| R5 | `Block if :dispute_count_on_card_number_all_time: > 0` | A card that has disputed with us before |
| R6 | `Block if :efw_count_on_card_all_time: > 0` | A card reported for fraud before |
| R7 | `Review if :risk_level: = 'elevated'` | Elevated risk reaches our gate as a hold |
| R8 | `Review if :is_anonymous_ip: and ::is_first_order:: = 'true' and :amount_in_eur: >= 100` | Anonymised connection on a first, larger order (VPN alone is normal and is not a rule) |
| R9 | `Review if :card_country: in @fo_review_card_countries and not :is_3d_secure_authenticated:` | The list holds **only countries outside the EEA**, empty at launch (geo-blocking, §8) |
| R10 | Only if §13 Q6 is yes: `Block if :is_disposable_email:` and `Review if :card_count_for_email_daily: >= 3` | Radar sees the email only through a Customer object or `receipt_email`, which spec 013 does not send today |

Radar's "review" action does not stop the charge (Stripe: "Stripe still processes these payments and charges the customer"). Under automatic capture it is a signal only: our gate reads the outcome and holds the order (signal S2). No rule blocks on card country, on a country mismatch, or on destination.

**Pay-step pre-check** (`prePayCheck(orderId) → PrePayVerdict`, zod `PrePayVerdictSchema` = `{ ok: true } | { ok: false, reason: 'blocked_email' | 'failed_orders' | 'paused' }`). Spec 013's `confirm(orderId)` calls it before `startPayment` creates any PaymentIntent (requirement, §12). In order:
1. An active `risk_pause` (`now` between `starts_at` and `ends_at`) or the `risk.pay_pause` flag on → `paused`. The island shows spec 013's existing `payments.error.unavailable` ("Payments are not available right now. Nothing was charged. Please try again in a few minutes."), which is then true.
2. The order's buyer email hash in `risk_blocklist` (kind `email`, not expired) → `blocked_email`; `last_matched_at` is set. The island shows spec 013's `payments.error.generic`.
3. The buyer email hash appears on 3 or more orders that ended `payment_failed` or `abandoned` in the last 24 hours → `failed_orders`, shown as `payments.error.generic`.
On a refusal, `prePayCheck` calls spec 013's `endPaymentAttempts(orderId, 'system:risk')` (requirement, §12), which closes the unpaid order the way spec 013's sweep (b) does. No intent exists, so nothing is charged. A refusal writes a log line `risk.prepay_refused` with `order_id` and `reason` only.

**Attempt cap.** When spec 013's reconcile records an order's 5th declined attempt (`payment.attempts = 5`, AC-5 of spec 013), it calls `endPaymentAttempts(orderId, 'system:risk')` (requirement, §12): the intent is cancelled at the provider and the order becomes `payment_failed` (A6 row 3). The island shows `checkout.risk.attemptsExhausted` (§7). Four declines and a success is an ordinary paid order (signal S12 may still add points).

**Disposable email at step 1.** Spec 010's step-1 action, in `test` and `live` only, refuses an email for which `isDisposableEmail` is true with `checkout.risk.emailLasting` on the field and keeps every other field (requirement on spec 010, §12). `isDisposableEmail` checks the normalised domain and its parent domains against `disposable-domains.txt` (a vendored copy of the public-domain `disposable-email-domains` list, updated by reviewed PR), minus `relay-allowlist.txt`, which holds at least `privaterelay.appleid.com`, `icloud.com`, `mozmail.com`, `duck.com`, `simplelogin.com`, `simplelogin.co`, `aleeas.com`, `passmail.net`. Privacy relays forward to a real inbox and are never treated as disposable. `prePayCheck` does not repeat this check; the gate scores it (S14) in case the list changed in between.

**Card-testing detector** (`risk.monitor`, every minute, `live` only for the pause). Reads spec 013's payment attempt counts through `payments.attemptStats(since)` (requirement, §12: declined and succeeded attempt counts in a window, no identifiers). When the last 10 minutes hold at least 10 declined attempts **and** declines are at least 50 % of attempts: one `risk.card_testing_suspected` alert per 10-minute window, and in `live` a `risk_pause` row of 30 minutes (`cause = 'card_testing'`) unless one is active (§13 Q5). In `test` it alerts only. Nine declines, or 10 declines out of 25 attempts, do nothing.

**Site velocity alert** (same job, `plan/06` §2.3): more than 5 `live` orders paid in the last hour by email hashes never seen before, on a date the destination's occasion calendar does not mark as a major occasion → `risk.velocity_site` (alert only; the founder may turn on `risk.strict`).

**The risk gate** (`riskGatedRouting(inner) → OutboxHandler`). Spec 016 registers its `routing` handler; the outbox registry is composed as `routing: riskGatedRouting(routingHandler)` (requirement, §12), so spec 016's routing code does not change. For `test` and `live`:
- `order.captured` from `placed` (A6 row 2): `assess(orderId, 'routing')`, then act on the decision (below). Row 14's `order.captured` (from `accepted`) is passed to `inner`, which ignores it as today.
- `order.risk_released`: if the order is still `paid_unrouted`, call spec 016's `startRouting(orderId)` (requirement, §12: the function its handler already runs for row 2, including the `routing.auto` off path). Otherwise nothing.
- Every other event is passed to `inner` unchanged.
This spec adds one route to spec 013's `outboxTargets`: `order.risk_released` → `routing` (`test`, `live`).

`assess` gathers the inputs, calls the pure `scoreOrder(RiskInputV1)`, writes `risk_assessment` and the order's `risk_identity` rows (email, card, recipient address; `expires_at` per A11 (c)), and returns the decision:
- **Inputs** (`RiskInputV1`, no personal data): mode; order total in minor units and its currency; delivery date is today in the destination zone; first order (no earlier `live` order with this email hash that reached `delivered` or later without a dispute, within the identity retention); card-message match (boolean from `cardMessageMatches`, computed in memory over `card_message` and `sign_as`, never stored or logged); Radar facts from spec 013's `riskFactsFor(orderId)` (requirement, §12: `riskLevel`, `riskScore`, `outcomeType`, `threeDsResult`, `liabilityShift`, `cardCountry`, `cardFingerprint`, `wallet`, `efwReceived`); declined attempts on this order; the velocity counts below; blocklist match; disposable email; peak day (the destination's occasion calendar marks the delivery date as a major occasion).
- **Facts unavailable:** if `riskFactsFor` fails, one retry after 2 seconds; if it fails again, the assessment proceeds without Radar facts and adds S16, which forces a hold. A provider outage therefore delays a florist offer instead of killing the outbox delivery.

| S13 | Card country outside both the buyer's declared country and the destination (a **modifier**: counts only when another signal with points fired, and never for an EEA-issued card with liability shift) | +10 |
| S14 | Disposable email (list changed since step 1) | +40 |
| S15 | Card message or "sign as" matches a payment, money-transfer, crypto or gift-card pattern (IBAN shape, crypto address shapes, the words of `card-message-patterns.ts` in en, de, pl) | always hold |
| S16 | Radar facts unavailable after one retry | always hold |
| D1 | Email or card hash on `risk_blocklist` | **decisive: cancel** |
| D2 | EFW already received on the charge | **decisive: cancel** |

Never a signal: card country different from the destination; buyer's declared country different from the destination; billing address different from the delivery address; delivery to another country. On a peak day, the counts of S7, S8 and S11 need 2 more to fire (a family sends to mother, grandmother and aunt on Mother's Day; siblings each send to one mother) (§13 Q11).

**Decision bands:** a decisive signal → `cancel`. Otherwise an "always hold" signal, or a score of 40 or more → `hold`. Otherwise `pass`. With `risk.strict` on (global flag, admin-only): hold at 20 or more, and every first order at or above the strict band (minor units: EUR 10 000, GBP 8 500, PLN 43 000) holds. A score of 70 or more is **high**; it only changes the deadline default (below). Every threshold is a constant with a test at the boundary (§13 Q9).

**Acting on a decision** (`risk.gate` on; with it off the assessment is recorded with `enforced = false` and the order goes to `inner` whatever the decision):
- **pass:** call `inner` with the event; spec 016 routes the order.
- **hold:** in one transaction, under the order row lock: insert `risk_review` (`kind = 'pre_routing'`, `status = 'open'`, `recommendation` = `cancel` when the score is high, else `approve`, `deadline_at` from `reviewDeadline`), append `order.risk_held` (`OrderRiskHeldV1`: `{ version: 1, mode, review_id, score, rules_version }`, actor `system:risk`) and raise `risk.review_opened`. The order stays `paid_unrouted`; nothing is offered to a florist; the delivery is marked `sent`.
- **cancel:** `cancelOrder(orderId, 'system:risk', 'fraud_confirmed')` (A6 row 13 from set E; spec 013 refunds the full captured amount through its refund path with Stripe reason `fraudulent`); the email and card hashes go on `risk_blocklist` (`source = 'efw'` for D2, unchanged for D1); `risk.auto_cancelled` is raised.

**Deadline** (`reviewDeadline(order, heldAt) → Date | 'decide_now'`, pure, destination zone helpers of spec 009):
- `latestSafeOfferAt = routingDeadline(order) − 3 h` (`REVIEW_ROUTING_MARGIN`), where `routingDeadline` is spec 016's "delivery date at the country's same-day cutoff plus 2 hours" (requirement: spec 016 exports it, §12).
- `deadline = min(heldAt + 12 h (REVIEW_MAX_HOLD), latestSafeOfferAt)`.
- If `deadline − heldAt` is under 30 minutes (`REVIEW_MIN_WINDOW`), the result is `decide_now` and the default below applies at once.
- Absolute instants are stored; DST is the zone's job, never added hours.

**Deadline default** (`risk.monitor`, every minute; also on `decide_now`): for an open `pre_routing` review whose deadline has passed: if the payment has liability shift and the score is under 70 → release (`order.risk_released` by `system:risk.deadline`, status `auto_released`); otherwise → `cancelOrder(orderId, 'system:risk.deadline', 'risk_cancel')` (status `auto_cancelled`), with no blocklist entry. Either way `risk.review_auto_decided` is raised (§13 Q2). At `deadline − 60 min`, an open review raises `risk.review_due_soon` once (`due_soon_alerted_at`); a review opened with less than 60 minutes left says so in `risk.review_opened` instead.

**Review actions** (server actions under `/admin/risk`, zod `ReviewDecisionSchema` = `{ reviewId: uuid, decision: 'approve' | 'cancel' | 'cancel_block', reason: decision_reason, expectedStatus: 'open' }`; first statement `requirePermission('risk.review')`, then `withAdminContext({ action: 'risk.review.<decision>' })`; actor `admin:{userId}`):
- All three lock the order row, then the review row, and re-read both. If the review is not `open`, the action changes nothing and reports what happened. If the order has left the review's allowed states (`paid_unrouted` for `pre_routing`; `accepted` or `paid` for a post-routing review), the review becomes `superseded` (`decision_reason = 'superseded'`) and nothing else happens. A buyer cancellation or a chargeback during a hold therefore needs no special path.
- **approve:** status `approved`, append `order.risk_released` (`OrderRiskReleasedV1`: `{ version: 1, mode, review_id, outcome: 'approved' | 'auto_released' }`); the `routing` route of that event starts routing (above).
- **cancel:** `cancelOrder(orderId, actor, 'risk_cancel')` for a `pre_routing` review (status `cancelled`); for a `post_routing` review on a `paid` order, `refund({ orderId, amount: remaining captured balance, reason: 'risk_cancel', actor })` (A6 row 31 → `refunded_full`; spec 016's withdrawal tells the florist to stop, 016 §5.2).
- **cancel_block:** as cancel with reason `fraud_confirmed`, plus the order's email and card hashes on `risk_blocklist` (`source = 'admin'`). Needs `risk.blocklist` too.
- This module never calls `transition` directly and never writes `orders.status` (AC-23 scans for it). The cancellation and refund rows, their events and the money invariants are spec 013's.

**After routing: early fraud warnings** (`handleEarlyFraudWarning(EarlyFraudWarningV1)`; zod over `{ id, charge, fraud_type, actionable, livemode, created }`; spec 013's `webhook.process` routes `radar.early_fraud_warning.created` here after mapping the charge to the order, requirement §12). By the order's status when it is processed:

| Order status | What happens |
|---|---|
| `placed` | nothing yet; the gate will see `efwReceived` (D2) |
| `paid_unrouted` with or without an open review | decisive cancel (above); an open review becomes `auto_cancelled` |
| `routed`, `no_partner_available`, `manual_reroute` (set E) | `cancelOrder(orderId, 'system:risk', 'fraud_confirmed')`; spec 016 withdraws the offer; blocklist; `risk.auto_cancelled` |
| `accepted` | the handler throws `ORDER_IN_HANDOVER`; spec 013's `webhook.process` marks the inbox row `failed` and retries with its backoff until row 14 makes the order `paid` (row 14 follows acceptance within one dispatcher tick) |
| `paid` | open a `post_routing` review, `recommendation = 'refund'`, `deadline_at = productionStartAt(order) − 60 min` (spec 013's function; `decide_now` if under 30 minutes away); at the deadline with no decision: full refund with `fraud_confirmed` (status `auto_refunded`) |
| `in_production` or later | open a `post_routing` review with no deadline and no default (the bouquet is being made or delivered); `risk.efw_after_routing`; the founder decides whether to refund or to keep the evidence ready |
| `chargeback_open`, cancelled or refunded states | nothing; logged |

An EFW is actionable only before a dispute and before a full refund; refunding it early avoids the dispute and its fee, as Stripe's EFW documentation suggests.

**Chargebacks** (`riskOutboxHandler`, new outbox target `risk`; rows: `order.chargeback_opened` → `risk` for `test` and `live`; `OutboxTarget` gains `risk`, so `HandlerRegistry` gains its handler, spec 013's type test enforces it): reads the dispute through `payments.disputesFor(orderId)` (requirement, §12); for reason `fraudulent`, puts the order's email and card hashes on `risk_blocklist` (`source = 'dispute'`) and closes any open review as `superseded`. Radar's R5 already blocks the card at Stripe; our list also covers the email and a future Mollie path.

**Evidence pack** (`buildEvidencePack(orderId) → Uint8Array`, PDF through spec 018's renderer, A4 portrait, body text at least 12 pt, at most 4.5 MB and 19 pages, Stripe's and Mastercard's limits). Content, in this order, each with a one-line caption:
1. Summary: order reference, amount and currency (via `formatMoney`), product name and tier, order date, delivery date, outcome ("Delivered on {date} at {time}").
2. Proof of authorisation: 3DS result and liability shift as Stripe reported them, card brand and last 4, the buyer's declared country.
3. Timeline from `order_event` (immutable), each time in UTC and in the destination zone: placed, paid, being made, out for delivery, delivered, photo uploaded; plus the time the order confirmation email was sent (spec 016's `notification_delivery`).
4. Delivery: recipient name and full delivery address (Stripe asks for the full address, not only city and postcode), the delivery photo with its upload time (spec 011's private asset, EXIF already stripped).
5. What the buyer agreed to: the checkout's cancellation and perishable-goods sentence in the order's locale, and the terms version of the order.
Never in it: the card message, "sign as", recipient phone, buyer phone, florist name or contact, internal statuses, risk score or signals. Generated on demand by `GET /admin/risk/evidence/[orderId]` (permission `risk.evidence`, admin only; `Cache-Control: no-store, private`; `Content-Disposition: attachment`; the read is logged with `logAdminRead('order.evidence', orderId)`); never stored by us. The founder uploads it in Stripe's dispute page. A dispute alert (`payment.dispute_opened`, spec 013) links to the order's admin page, where the button lives.

**Jobs** (registered in `src/jobs/index.ts`, versioned payload schemas): `risk.monitor` (every minute: deadlines and defaults, due-soon alerts, card-testing detector, site velocity). Retention runs in spec 002's `retention.sweep` from A11 (c).

No `notifications` route exists for either (no buyer or florist message; requirement that spec 016's `notificationsFor` has no row for them, §12).

## 8. Compliance considerations (mandatory)

**Data flows added (RoPA, `docs/compliance/ropa.md`, new rows):**
1. **Fraud prevention: pre-checks, scoring and review.** Subjects: buyers, recipients. Categories: keyed hashes of the buyer's normalised email, of Stripe's card fingerprint and of the recipient's normalised street, postcode and country; per-order assessment (score, signal ids, decision, Radar risk level and score, 3DS result); review decisions and their admin actor; blocklist hashes. The card message is read in memory for a yes or no answer and never stored here. Purpose: prevent payment fraud and protect cardholders. Basis: **Art. 6(1)(f)**, fraud prevention being a recognised legitimate interest (Recital 47); `plan/07` §1.2 "Fraud signals". Processors: Neon (row 6). Retention: A11 (c) (email and card hashes 12 months, recipient-address hashes 90 days, assessments and reviews 12 months, blocklist 12 months after last match). Spec 012's audit log keeps admin decisions for 3 years (masked).
2. **Chargeback evidence.** Subjects: buyer, recipient. Categories: as §5.2's evidence pack, including the recipient's name, full delivery address and delivery photo. Recipients: Stripe (processor, and independent controller for dispute handling) and, through Stripe, the card network and the issuing bank (independent controllers). Basis: **Art. 6(1)(f)**, establishing and defending legal claims; only on a dispute, only these fields. This is the one place recipient data reaches Stripe; spec 013 §13 Q4 keeps it out of every payment. The privacy policy's recipient section (spec 041) says so.
3. **Spec 013's Stripe row** gains the EFW webhook type (no personal fields: charge id, fraud type) and, only if §13 Q6 is yes, a Stripe Customer object holding the buyer's email (no name).

**Legitimate interest assessment:** `docs/compliance/lia-fraud-prevention.md`, written in the docs task (AC-34): purpose (stop stolen cards and card testing, protect cardholders and florists), necessity (each signal tied to a known pattern; hashes instead of raw values; no IP or device data of our own; Radar's processing under Stripe's own controllership), balancing (buyers expect fraud checks on a card payment; recipients' data is reduced to an address hash kept 90 days; no marketing or profiling beyond the order), plus a DPIA screening note (small scale, no special categories, no systematic monitoring of public areas; a full DPIA is not expected, the lawyer confirms).

**Automated decisions (Art. 22).** A hold is decided by a person (the founder) with authority to approve or cancel, not by the machine. Two paths are solely automated and can cancel an order: the decisive signals (blocklist match, EFW) and the deadline default. They rely on Art. 22(2)(a) (necessary for entering into and performing the contract, a card payment that must be genuine) with the Art. 22(3) safeguards: the buyer can contact us by phone or WhatsApp (the help line on every page), a person reviews the order and the blocklist entry, and can lift it. The privacy page explains the logic in plain words (Art. 13(2)(f)) (requirement on spec 041, §12). The lawyer confirms with the legal set (`plan/13` B4; §13 Q12).

**Geo-blocking (Regulation (EU) 2018/302, ADR-0006).** No rule reads the visitor's location; methods, prices and checkout access are unchanged. Art. 5(1) forbids different payment conditions by the place a card was issued within the EU when authentication succeeds. So card country is never a block in our code or in Radar, signal S13 is only a modifier and never applies to an EEA-issued card with liability shift, and R9's list may hold only countries outside the EEA (AC-37 checks both). Art. 5(2) allows withholding delivery, where objectively justified, until the payment is confirmed as properly initiated; a fraud hold of at most 12 hours before routing is read as that, for the lawyer to confirm (§13 Q12).

**3DS and SCA policy (EU and UK).** Every card payment asks for 3DS (`request_three_d_secure: 'any'`, spec 013 Q1): the issuer decides whether to challenge, and an authenticated payment moves fraud liability to the issuer, our main defence on a gift sent to someone else. Apple Pay and Google Pay authenticate on the device; where Stripe reports no liability shift for a wallet payment, we count it as not shifted (S3). We request no SCA exemption (low value or transaction risk analysis) at launch; revisit after 100 live orders with the founder. A failed authentication is a decline, shown by spec 013's messages. The acquirer's region follows the company entity decision (founder, 2026-10-23, its ADR pending); the policy above holds either way, and the runbook records whether SCA is mandatory or best effort for each card region once the Stripe account exists (§13 Q13).

**Charge at once, never hold a card.** Unchanged: the money is taken at placement (spec 013 §14). A hold delays the florist offer only, for at most 12 hours and never past the safe offer time (§5.2). A risk cancellation is always a full refund to the original method (spec 013's refund path; `plan/07` §9 AML rule).

**Consumer information and price display.** No price, fee or surcharge is added; the price shown is the price charged. A cancelled order is refunded in full; the buyer sees the existing cancellation wording and keeps every statutory right. A held order shows exactly what an unheld one shows ("Thank you. We have your order." and the arrival line), which stays true: the order is real and paid, and the delivery date is unchanged unless we cancel.

**No accusation, positive framing after purchase.** No buyer surface, email, catalogue key or PDF the buyer may see mentions fraud, suspicion, review, verification, flags, blocks or risk (`ACCUSATION_PATTERNS`, AC-30). After payment nothing negative appears for a held order; a cancelled order reads "We could not deliver this order. We have refunded {amount}." (the founder's own wording, spec 013 Appendix A).

**No partner or relay wording to buyers.** The two new keys and every buyer state of this spec pass `RELAY_PATTERNS` (spec 041 §14). The evidence pack is issuer-facing and names no florist.

**Data subject rights.** Access: spec 018's export already excludes `risk_score` and fraud signals; this spec keeps that and adds its tables to the exclusion; the lawyer confirms the legal ground per regime (UK DPA 2018 crime-prevention exemption; Art. 23 member-state law in the EU). Erasure: spec 018's erasure deletes the person's `risk_identity` rows unless a blocklist entry or an open dispute concerns them (Art. 17(3)(e), legal claims); requirement on spec 018 (§12).

**Logs, URLs, analytics (`CLAUDE.md`).** Allowed log fields (§11) are ids, codes, scores and counts. Never logged or sent to Sentry: email, hashes, card fingerprint, address, card message, names, phones. URLs carry review and order ids only. No analytics event is added.

**Accessibility (WCAG 2.2 AA, `plan/07` §8).** The step-1 error and step-3 notice use spec 010's and 013's accessible patterns (`aria-live`, focus, `aria-describedby`); admin pages are keyboard-operable, dialogs trap and return focus, axe zero serious or critical.

**Security.** `RISK_HASH_KEY` only through `lib/env.ts`; zod on every action and on the EFW payload; admin actions guarded by `requirePermission` and audited; optimistic `expectedStatus` on decisions; the blocklist cannot be written outside admin context and the risk module.

## 13. Open questions

Each has a recommended default; the body, ACs and tests are written against the defaults.

- **Q1. May the system cancel and refund without a person, on a blocklist match or an early fraud warning before routing?** These are the strongest signals we get (a card or email that disputed with us, or the bank telling us the card was used without permission). **Recommended: yes**, with the Art. 22 safeguards of §8 (a person reviews on request and can lift the block). Alternative: hold them too, which risks a bouquet being made at night before anyone looks.
- **Q2. What happens to a held order nobody decides in time?** **Recommended: release it if the card was authenticated by the bank (liability shift) and the score is under 70; otherwise cancel and refund in full, with no block.** With liability shift, a fraud chargeback falls on the card issuer, so releasing is the safer default for the buyer and for us. Alternatives: always cancel (safest money, loses honest orders at night) or always release (best conversion, full loss on a stolen unauthenticated card).
- **Q3. Hold limits.** **Recommended: at most 12 hours, ending at least 3 hours before spec 016's routing deadline, and decided at once when under 30 minutes remain.** Alternatives: 6 hours (less waiting, more defaults) or 24 hours (more founder sleep, but tighter for next-day orders).
- **Q4. Refuse disposable email addresses at step 1?** `plan/06` says block. **Recommended: yes**, with the friendly message of §7 and privacy relays (Apple Hide My Email, Firefox Relay, DuckDuckGo, SimpleLogin, Proton Pass) always allowed. Alternative: accept and score (S14 only), which keeps the sale but leaves us without a reliable address for the confirmation and updates.
- **Q5. Pause new payments for 30 minutes automatically during a card-testing burst (`live`)?** **Recommended: yes**: it protects our approval rate while the founder sleeps, and the buyer reads Stripe's true "Payments are not available right now" message. Alternative: alert only and pause by hand with `risk.pay_pause`.
- **Q6. Give Stripe the buyer's email on a Customer object so Radar's email signals and block lists work?** Today spec 013 sends no `receipt_email` and no Customer, so Radar's disposable-email check, email velocity and the email half of its fraud block list see nothing. The email already reaches Stripe through the payment's billing details (spec 013 §8), so the privacy cost is small. **Recommended: yes**, email only, no name, one Customer per order; it amends spec 013 AC-1 and adds R10. Alternative: no, and rely on our own email checks.
- **Q7. Keep the recipient's name and delivery address for 180 days after delivery, for chargeback evidence?** Card schemes allow about 120 days to dispute and Stripe asks for the full delivery address, but `plan/07` deletes recipient contact data after 90 days. **Recommended: yes for name and address only** (phone and card message still go at 90 days), as spec 002 §14 A11 (d). Alternative: keep 90 days and accept weaker evidence for late disputes.
- **Q8. Submit evidence by hand in Stripe's dashboard in Phase 1?** **Recommended: yes**: one download, one upload, and the key keeps read-only dispute access. Alternative: submit through the API (needs Disputes write on the key and a review step in admin).
- **Q9. The thresholds.** **Recommended: as in §5.2** (hold at 40, high at 70, every order of EUR 300 or more reviewed, first orders of EUR 150 or more score +20), reviewed with the daily counter line after 100 live orders. Alternative: start stricter (`risk.strict` on for the first month).
- **Q10. Should a risk cancellation reuse "We could not deliver this order. We have refunded {amount}."?** **Recommended: yes**, no new copy: it is the founder's own wording, true from the buyer's side, and accuses nobody. Alternative: a dedicated line that invites the buyer to call us, which some honest buyers would welcome and a fraudster would use to probe.
- **Q11. Relax the velocity counts on major occasions?** **Recommended: yes, 2 more** for S7, S8 and S11 on dates the destination's occasion calendar marks as major (Mother's Day, Women's Day, Valentine's), because families send several bouquets that day. Alternative: no relaxation, more holds on peak days, when the founder has the least time.
- **Q12 (for the lawyer, with the legal set, `plan/13` B4; does not block `test` mode).** Confirm three readings: the short fraud hold before routing is a justified withholding under Geo-blocking Art. 5(2), and card country used only as a modifier (never for authenticated EEA cards) is not a different payment condition under Art. 5(1); the two automated cancellations rely on Art. 22(2)(a) with the §8 safeguards and the privacy-page wording; the exclusion of fraud data from access requests is lawful in the EU and UK. **Recommended: proceed as designed**, adjusting copy or the modifier if the lawyer says so.
- **Q13 (not blocking).** The company entity changed (founder, 2026-10-23; ADR pending). Whether SCA is mandatory or best effort for EU and UK cards depends on where the Stripe account is acquired. **Recommended: keep 3DS on every card whatever the answer**, and record the region in the runbook once the account exists.
