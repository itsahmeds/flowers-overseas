## A country that buys flowers for itself

The first thing to understand about giving flowers here is that it is entirely
ordinary. A bunch bought on the way home from work, for no occasion, ends up in a
vase on a kitchen table every week of the year. That habit shapes what the shops
are good at. A Blumenladen builds a loose, natural-looking hand-tied bunch, wrapped
in paper, with grasses and twigs and berries doing as much work as the blooms. Ask
for something in season and you will be given better flowers, arranged with more
care, than if you insist on roses in January.

The second thing is that two flowers carry a meaning you cannot talk your way out
of. Chrysanthemums are cemetery flowers. So, for most people, are white lilies and
callas. Around 1 November the cemeteries fill with them and the association is
renewed every year. A generous armful of white lilies sent to a new flat will be
put in water and quietly wondered about.

The third is the red rose, which says one thing and only one thing. There is no
such thing as a friendly dozen red roses here. Thank a host, a teacher or a
colleague with tulips, gerberas, ranunculus, anything at all with colour in it,
and save the roses for the person you mean them for.

Counting is a smaller worry than it is further east. An even number is fine. A
dozen is fine. Thirteen is the number an older recipient might rather not be
handed, and that is the extent of it.

## The calendar that matters

Muttertag, the second Sunday in May, is the heaviest day of the year for every
shop in the country, and it is worth remembering that it does not fall on the
March date that British families keep. Vatertag is stranger: it lands on Christi
Himmelfahrt, the Ascension holiday, always a Thursday, 14 May in 2026 and 6 May in
2027, and it is a day of walks and handcarts rather than of bouquets. What it
certainly is, for our purposes, is a public holiday with the shutters down.

Then there are the days of the dead. Allerheiligen on 1 November is a public
holiday in Bavaria, Baden-Wuerttemberg, North Rhine-Westphalia, the Rhineland
Palatinate and the Saarland, and a day of grave visits everywhere. Volkstrauertag
in the middle of November and Totensonntag at its end are quieter versions of the
same thing. Grave care is a proper trade here, with its own shops beside the
cemetery gates.

Advent runs through the four Sundays before Christmas: 29 November in 2026, 28
November in 2027. The wreath with four candles arrives first, then amaryllis,
mistletoe, and the white Christrose. A wreath is a more natural gift in December
than a bouquet is.

The fixed public holidays to plan around are 1 January, 1 May, 3 October for the
Tag der Deutschen Einheit, and 25 and 26 December. The moving ones are Karfreitag
and Ostermontag, 3 and 6 April in 2026 and 26 and 29 March in 2027, and
Pfingstmontag, 25 May in 2026 and 17 May in 2027. Catholic regions add 6 January,
Fronleichnam and 15 August; some Protestant ones keep 31 October.

## Sunday is closed, and that is the law

Shop opening hours are regulated, and the practical effect is that a Blumenladen
is shut on Sundays and on public holidays. Railway stations, airports and a few
tourist exemptions are the only reliable way to buy a bunch on a Sunday morning.
If the day you care about is a Sunday, the Saturday before it is the day that
actually matters.
