## Not one country, for these purposes

Spain is several flower cultures under one flag, and a British sender who treats it
as a single market will get the customs of Catalonia confused with those of
Andalusia. What follows is the common ground, with the regional exceptions marked
where they matter, written for somebody ordering from Britain.

## The date is not the British date

Mothers' day here is the first Sunday in May. Mothering Sunday at home moves with
Lent and falls in March. They are roughly two months apart and are never the same
occasion. Fathers' day is the 19th of March, San Jose, which is a public holiday in
some regions and an ordinary one in others.

Then there is the santo, the feast day of the saint whose name you carry, which in a
great many families is celebrated as fully as a birthday. There is no British
equivalent, which is exactly why remembering it is such a good gesture. If you know
the first name, the date is easy to find.

## The twenty-third of April

In Catalonia, Sant Jordi is the day. People give each other a book and a rose,
stalls take over every square in Barcelona, and the rose trade does more in those
hours than on any other date in the year. Outside Catalonia it passes almost
unmarked, so aim it carefully: it is a wonderful thing to send to Girona or
Tarragona and a slightly baffling one to send to Bilbao.

## Holy Week reshapes the spring

Semana Santa is the week when the brotherhoods dress their processional floats in
carnations, lilies and, in Andalusia, azahar, the orange blossom whose scent is the
smell of the city for those days. Every shop in town is working to that deadline
rather than to yours. Hours shorten, streets close, and a good florist will be frank
with you about what is realistic.

## Heat, and what a shop will agree to make

From June to September the temperature decides. A conscientious Spanish florist will
not sell you something soft-headed for a flat without air conditioning, and if you
insist they will tell you exactly what will happen to it. Potted plants, succulents,
carnations and firm mixed bunches are what survive, and they are what an experienced
shop will offer. British senders, used to a mild summer, are the ones who argue with
this advice, and they are wrong to.

## The carnation, reconsidered

At home the carnation is filler, the thing that pads out a garage bunch. In Spain it
is a serious flower, grown in quantity, used in everything and given without a second
thought. If you want an arrangement that feels local rather than imported, it is the
honest answer.

## The days everything stops

The national holidays are the 1st and 6th of January, Good Friday, the 1st of May,
the 15th of August, the 12th of October, the 1st of November, the 6th and 8th of
December and the 25th of December. On top of those, every region and nearly every
town keeps a fiesta of its own, and on that day the shutters come down whatever the
national calendar says. Todos los Santos, the 1st of November, is about graves.
