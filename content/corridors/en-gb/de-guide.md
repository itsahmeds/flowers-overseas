## Three British assumptions that do not travel

This is the version of our German guide written for somebody sending from Britain.
The customs are the same customs; what changes is which of them will trip you up,
and all three of the usual ones come from habits we have at home.

**The date.** Mothering Sunday moves with Lent and lands in March. Muttertag is the
second Sunday in May. They are unrelated occasions with different histories, and the
gap between them is usually about two months. Nothing else in the German calendar
causes half as much trouble.

**The red rose.** At home a bunch of red roses can be sent with a wink. There it is
an unambiguous declaration, and a colleague or a friend who receives one will not
read it as a joke. Send colour instead, and keep the roses for the person you mean.

**The Sunday.** Shop opening is regulated, and a Blumenladen will be shut on a
Sunday and on every public holiday. Railway stations and airports are the practical
exceptions. If the day you care about is a Sunday, the Saturday before it is the
day that actually matters.

## Two flowers to keep away from the house

Chrysanthemums are cemetery flowers, and so, for most people, are white lilies and
callas. The association is renewed every year around the first of November, when
the cemeteries fill. A British supermarket treats the chrysanthemum as an ordinary
cheerful bunch, which is exactly why this one catches British senders out.

Counting, by contrast, is a relief. An even number is fine; the British dozen
travels perfectly well. Thirteen is the only number worth avoiding, and even that is
a courtesy to older recipients rather than a rule.

## The rest of the year

Vatertag is Christi Himmelfahrt, always a Thursday, the 14th of May in 2026 and the
6th in 2027, and it is a walking-and-drinking occasion rather than a flower one.
Allerheiligen on the first of November is a public holiday in the Catholic
Laender and a day of grave visits everywhere. Volkstrauertag in mid-November and
Totensonntag at its end are quieter versions of the same.

Advent begins on the fourth Sunday before Christmas, the 29th of November in 2026
and the 28th in 2027. The wreath comes first, then amaryllis, mistletoe and the
white Christrose. The fixed holidays to plan around are the 1st of January, the 1st
of May, the 3rd of October and the 25th and 26th of December; the moving ones are
Good Friday, Easter Monday and Whit Monday.

## What a German shop will make you

Something looser and more natural than a British florist would produce for the same
money: paper rather than cellophane, grasses and seed heads rather than gypsophila,
seasonal material handled with obvious care. Ask for what is in season and you will
be looked after. Ask for roses in January and you will get them, and they will be
the least interesting thing in the shop.

## Money, and where you stand

You pay in pounds sterling. One figure, VAT and delivery already inside it, shown
before you enter a card, and that is the figure that leaves your account. There is
no conversion applied afterwards and no fee at the end. No price travels with the
flowers, and there is no paperwork in the box.

You are buying from us, and not from whichever shop ends up making the bouquet. A
complaint comes to us, in English, rather than to a shop abroad.
