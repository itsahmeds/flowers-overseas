## A country that gives flowers constantly

The habit here is closer to daily life than to special occasions. A visitor to a
home brings flowers. A pupil brings them to a teacher, and at the end of term the
staff room disappears behind them. A woman whose name day it is will collect several
bunches over a single Tuesday. For a sender in Britain the useful consequence is
that you do not need an occasion, and that the gesture will not seem excessive.

The taste, though, is not a British taste. A bouquet is expected to be substantial,
densely built and elaborately wrapped. The restraint that signals good taste at home
can read as thin there, and the arrangement that would look showy in a British
sitting room looks entirely normal in a flat in Cluj.

## The calendar does not match ours at any point

Easter is the big one. The Orthodox reckoning puts it on the 12th of April in 2026
and the 2nd of May in 2027, so it rarely coincides with the British Easter and in
some years misses it by more than a month. The week around it reorders everything,
and the shops keep holiday hours.

The Sunday before is Florii, Palm Sunday, when willow branches are blessed and
everybody whose name comes from a flower celebrates together. Fifty days after Easter
comes Rusalii, Pentecost, another public holiday.

The first of March is Martisor, the charm on a red and white thread, pinned on and
worn through the opening days of the month, given with the first snowdrops of the
year. A week later, on the eighth, comes Ziua Femeii, kept with real enthusiasm and
still treated by many families as the day for mothers, even though the official Ziua
Mamei is now the first Sunday in May.

Name days are the part a British sender misses entirely. Sfantul Ion on the 7th of
January accounts for an enormous number of people. Sfantul Gheorghe on the 23rd of
April and Sfintii Constantin si Elena on the 21st of May do the same, and Sfanta
Maria on the 15th of August is so widely shared that shops order for it as they would
for a national holiday. If you know a first name you can find the day, and marking it
will mean more than a card on a birthday.

## The days the country stops

The public holidays are the 1st and 2nd of January, Boboteaza on the 6th and Sfantul
Ion on the 7th, the 24th of January, Orthodox Good Friday, Easter Sunday and Monday,
the 1st of May, the 1st of June for children, Rusalii and the Monday after it, the
15th of August, the 30th of November, the 1st of December for the national day, and
the 25th and 26th of December. The opening days of January and the whole week of
Easter are the two stretches when almost nothing is open.

Luminatia, on the 1st of November, is kept most strongly in Transylvania, where
cemeteries are lit with candles and dressed with chrysanthemums. That is the reason
the flower reads as it does, and the reason to keep it out of a living room.

## What a Romanian shop is good at

Volume and finish. A florist in Bucharest or Cluj will build something large,
symmetrical and heavily wrapped, with ribbon, paper and often a sleeve. Roses are
the staple, available all year and used for everything from a graduation to an
apology. Spring is the strongest season, when tulips, hyacinths and freesias arrive
in quantity and are at their best.
