# Spec 003 §14 A15: agent-written `de`/`pl` drafts (TASK-185)

**In one sentence:** agents replace about 340 echoed English strings per locale, plus 84 product descriptions, with German and Polish drafts while `/de` and `/pl` stay `noindex` and "Beta". The approach is sound and nothing changes for Google, but four gaps in the text should be closed before approval.

## Verdict: GO WITH FIXES
1. **Wrong path for legal documents (clause 4, AC-35, T-35).** Spec 041 §2 (L55) puts the legal documents in `content/legal/{locale}/`, not `content/pages/`. Change both places to "touches no file under `content/pages/**` or `content/legal/**`", and add `infoPages.legal.*` to the excluded keys by name. As written, "any `legal.*` key" does not clearly cover spec 041's `infoPages.legal.*`.
2. **Promises need the same gate as legal text (clause 4, open item (c)).** Some drafted keys make promises a buyer can hold us to: "We redeliver or refund, your choice", the 72-hour fresh-flower promise, the "Order by 14:00 … for delivery today" cutoff lines, FAQ answers about refunds, cancellation or withdrawal, and the 84 product descriptions. List them in `draft-policy.json` under a `reviewBeforeOrders` key. Then reword (c) so a person writes every excluded key, and a native reviewer attests every `reviewBeforeOrders` key, before the **first** of three events: the locale becomes indexable, `/de` or `/pl` can take an order, or any consent-gated tag is switched on for that locale.
3. **Bans in German and Polish are promised but not tested (clause 5, AC-37, T-37).** `bannedVoiceWordsIn` (`src/config/voice.ts`) checks English words only, so "Netzwerk", "Drittanbieter", "Korridor", "sieć" and "pośrednik" would all pass. Do one of two things. Either add the German and Polish forms to the list and add a T-37 seed ("Netzwerk" in a `de` value) that turns red, or delete the clause "stays banned in its target-language form" and leave those words to the native reviewer.
4. **The glossaries contradict A15 (clause 4).** `glossary.en.md` §2 still says "partner florist", and §4 bans "our florists", which the approved "Our florist…" sentence uses. `glossary.de.md` §2 and `glossary.pl.md` §3 say funeral copy and All Saints' Day copy are "never machine-drafted", but clause 4 does not exclude them. Do three things: (i) add a sentence saying the decisions log and spec 004 A5/A22 win over the glossary; (ii) either exclude the sympathy, funeral and All Saints' keys and seed rows, or say that A15 overrides those glossary lines; (iii) have TASK-185 bring those glossary lines into line in the same PR.

## The four hats
**Building.** (1) TASK-185 is one large PR: about 680 strings, 168 product rows × 3 fields, the tooling and the tests. Drafts written late in that batch may be worse than early ones; let the work order use one sub-agent per locale. (2) Check 10 as an error turns any open PR that adds an `en` key red when it rebases after TASK-185. Spec 041's TASK-182–184 add the whole `infoPages.*` namespace. Decide whether they merge before TASK-185 (it drafts them) or after (each PR carries its own drafts), and write that into their work orders. (3) Ordering otherwise holds. TASK-177 has merged. TASK-172 must merge first because it brings the freshness scan, which already covers German and Polish day words (spec 009 A11). TASK-179 must merge first because it changes `catalog.floristSentence`, the sentence that ends all 168 product rows. Drafting before either merges would mean doing the work twice.

**Google.** No concern. Drafted titles stay `noindex` and out of hreflang (AC-36), and product pages stay non-indexable (clause 7).

**Law and compliance** (I am not a lawyer; this is not legal advice).
(1) *Promises and product descriptions.* Under EU 2019/771 Art. 17, guarantee terms in advertising bind the seller when they are more favourable than the guarantee statement. A mistranslated remedy or product description is therefore a real exposure once orders open, and fix 2 gates it. Today nothing can be ordered, so the risk is low. [inferred]
(2) *Polish.* Poland's Language Act Art. 7 requires Polish for consumer offers and guarantee terms when the consumer lives in Poland and the contract is performed there. English legal, consent or price text on `/pl` is a launch blocker for those orders, not just a WCAG issue. A Polish lawyer should confirm this before `pl` checkout opens.
(3) *Price display and consent.* Three price lines are already German and Polish. `product.vat` ("VAT {rate}") and the corridor `fromValue` stay in English beside a € or zł price. That looks unfinished but does not mislead. An English consent sheet for German speakers is weak "informed" consent, but GA4 is off, so nothing depends on it yet (fix 2 covers this). The WCAG 3.1.2 gap is real. The EAA exempts microenterprises providing services (Art. 4(5)), which probably covers us [inferred]. A cheap partial fix is `lang="en"` on the consent sheet whenever its text falls back to English.

**Customer.** (1) German or Polish text is a clear improvement for buyers. (2) Mixed-language pages on a phone (the consent sheet, two price lines) still look like a beta, which the Beta tag explains. (3) The home sentence is the most visible risk. In German and Polish the possessive must agree with the chosen person. "meinen Schatz" and "drugiej połówki" carry a grammatical gender, so "seinen/ihren" and "jej/jego" need a native eye first.

## Questions the founder should ask
1. Can German or Polish buyers place an order on `/de` or `/pl` before a native reviewer has read the promise keys and product descriptions? (Fix 2 says no.)
2. Who wrote the German strings marked `source: "human"`, such as `catalog.price.allIn` and `company.description`? If an agent wrote them, the label is wrong. (A15 (a) also says "the founder rewrites" them, but the founder writes no German.)
3. Can the first native review be a small batch: the five price lines, the consent sheet and the home sentence?
4. Do spec 041's TASK-182–184 merge before or after TASK-185?
5. Should check 10 stay an error? Yes, in my view. Under clause 2, `i18n:draft` quietly turns a stale draft back into English, and check 10 is the only thing that catches it. A warning is how the 340 echoed strings built up.

## What looks right
Indexing stays untouched, only people can mark a string reviewed (AC-34), and legal and price text is excluded by data, not by memory. Every new rule has a test that must turn red when its subject is removed.
