# Spec 018: tracking, invoices and the customer account (draft, @ec08c54f)

## 1. In one sentence
This spec covers everything after payment: tracking, invoices, the account and privacy requests. It is sound and fits A6. Six fixes are small, but a cancel gap and a missing session field would show up in production. It also bundles four separate products into one two-week budget, so the founder should approve it as a launch slice plus a later slice.

## 2. Verdict: GO WITH FIXES
1. **§12 Tasks.** Mark which tasks block launch. Tasks 1, 2 and 3 are needed for the first paid order: B1's tracking link and the invoice code, ready for the day the accountant signs off. Tasks 4 and 5 (account, saved recipients, self-service export and erase) can ship after launch. Until then, `docs/runbooks/dsar.md` covers DSARs by hand, which GDPR permits: it requires an answer, not a self-service button.
2. **AC-34 and §5.3 `checkout/done`.** Only the account nudge should depend on the `account` flag. As written, turning `account` off also hides "Cancel for free" on the confirmation page. The buyer would then have no self-service cancel at launch, even though the page promises free cancellation (A6 D4).
3. **§5.1 and §5.2 `requestErasure`.** Say where "session created less than 15 minutes ago" is read from. Auth.js's `sessions` table holds only the token, user and `expires`, and a rolling `expires` moves. [inferred from the standard Auth.js adapter schema; the repo defines no `created_at`] Add `sessions.created_at` (or a `signed_in_at`) to the migration, and give AC-30 a case for it.
4. **§5.1 `privacy_request.due_at` and AC-31.** Compute the deadline as one calendar month, not 30 days. GDPR Art. 12(3) says "within one month". A request received on 31 January is due on 28 February, so `+30 days` lands late. Add a February case to the fixture.
5. **§6 and AC-13.** Say explicitly that `/{locale}/track` and `/{locale}/account`, with no trailing slash, are *not* covered by `Disallow: /*/track/`. That is the right result: the footer links to them from every page, and Google only obeys a `noindex` it is allowed to crawl ([Google](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/block-indexing)). Add a T-13 assertion that `robots.txt` does not block those two paths. Today §13 Q2 says "one rule covers all of /*/track/", which is not what the rule does.
6. **§12 "Requirements on other specs".** Q13's unique reference index, and the retry inside `place`, change approved spec 010's behaviour. Raise them as a spec 010 §14 amendment for the founder, not as a one-line note in a brief. In the same amendment, fix spec 010 §5.3's row "test and live: spec 013" so that one spec owns the live confirmation block. Spec 013's draft gives the confirmation layout back to 010.

## 3. The four hats
**Building**
- Size. The spec has 36 ACs and 6 tasks, and it depends on five other specs. Two of them, 013 (refund events with `refundId`, `captured_at`) and 016 (B1 to B6), are still drafts. Tasks 2 and 3 can only be finished against fixtures until 013 lands (§12 task 3).
- The cross-spec changes (§12) are where defects will come from: the SQL mirror of the reference derivation and the payload contract of the refund events. AC-8 and AC-10 test both, which is good.
- Over-built for launch: saved recipients, the guest privacy flow and the inactive `agent/PL` fixture. The data-driven invoice profiles are justified, because `plan/09` asks for them.

**Google:** no concern beyond fix 5. Every route is noindex and kept out of the sitemap, the header is unchanged, and AC-36 keeps public pages free of the cookie.

**Law and compliance** (not legal advice; 🧾 the accountant and ⚖️ a lawyer must sign off)
- 🧾 Invoices are kept 10 years (§8). Estonia requires 7 years from the end of the financial year (Accounting Act §12, per [EMTA](https://emta.ee/en/node/125/chapter/27199/pdf)), and Poland's limitation period is shorter [inferred]. Keeping data longer than needed works against data minimisation, so get the actual period from the accountant.
- 🧾 Poland's mandatory e-invoicing system (KSeF) appears not to apply here. It excludes B2C invoices and sellers with no seat or fixed establishment in Poland ([BDO alert](https://www.bdo.pl/getattachment/c9528a16-3799-4f71-8ee7-b923cd812ece/ALERT-BDO-1_03_26-EN.pdf), [EY Q&A](https://www.ey.com/en_pl/insights/tax/structured-e-invoices-qa)). The PDFKit design holds only while that stays true, so ask the accountant to confirm it.
- ⚖️ Q6: the saved address book rests on legitimate interest and an Art. 14 notice to the recipient. That is a new legal question, and launch does not need it.

**Customer**
- On a phone, a buyer opening B1 cannot cancel from the tracking page. Their only routes are the confirmation cookie (24 hours) or the account. A cancel link in B1, signed for that purpose (only the buyer receives B1), would be the one-tap path, and the shared tracking link would still be safe (Q10).
- No other concern. The lookup, the refund lines and the destination-zone times all build trust.

**§13 answers I disagree with:** Q6 (prefer "no address book in Phase 1"). Q2 (agree, but the reasoning is wrong, see fix 5). Q13 (agree, but through a 010 amendment, see fix 6). I agree with all the others; Q4, Q5, Q8 and Q12 stay with the accountant or lawyer.

## 5. What looks right
The tracking token carries no personal data and has a strict allowlist with a marker test (AC-4). Invoice numbering is gap-free and refuses demo and test orders. Refunded orders are shown honestly as "closed" with the amount and date. These are exactly the trust and compliance details this business sells on.
