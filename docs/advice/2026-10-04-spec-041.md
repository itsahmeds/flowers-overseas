# Spec 041: trust, help and legal pages (draft, PR 164 at bd3f2ace)

**In one sentence:** ten text pages that make the footer real and are honest about a business that is not open yet. The design is sound, but the stop-gap privacy notice and the guarantee page each miss one sentence the law expects, and one task has a dependency the plan does not list.

This memo is not legal advice. The lawyer review in `plan/13` B4 still has to happen before the first order.

## Verdict: GO WITH FIXES
1. **§2 Cookies, §8 "Controller identity now", Appendix B cookie notice, AC-29.** The stop-gap section "What else this website records" must also give the purpose and the legal basis of each record (RoPA rows 1–3: Art. 6(1)(f) and 6(1)(c)), name the companies that handle it (Railway, Cloudflare, Sentry, and Vercel while it is the fallback), and say that row 2 data may go to the US and on what safeguard. GDPR Art. 13(1)(c), (e) and (f) require all three, and §2 currently lists only how long data is kept, the visitor's rights and where to complain. Extend AC-25's test, which checks that every company in the RoPA is named, to cover this section.
2. **Same section, the right to complain.** Write "the data protection authority where you live or work", giving the Estonian authority (AKI) and the ICO as examples. AKI is the right body only once the company is established in Estonia, and until then the person responsible for the data is a private individual [inferred].
3. **Appendix A.3 and AC-16.** Add to the guarantee page: "This promise comes on top of your legal rights if goods are faulty; it does not replace or limit them." The EU rule on guarantees (Directive 2019/771, Art. 17(2)) and the UK one (Consumer Rights Act 2015, s.30) both require this statement. Without it, the 72-hour window reads like a limit on those rights.
4. **A.3 "If nobody is home".** Change "before anything is charged" to "before any extra charge". Appendix B takes the payment when the florist accepts the order, which is before delivery, so the current sentence is untrue.
5. **A.2, A.3 and A.4 question 5.** Use one substitution rule everywhere. A.3 says "same or higher value"; A.2 and A.4 say "same value".
6. **§12, task 1.** Add TASK-172 to task 1's dependencies. AC-16 checks the three "7-day" strings that TASK-172 changes, so task 1 fails CI if it merges first.
7. **§13 Q2.** Settle the pending support line before you approve. "We reply within a few hours", promised 24/7, has to be true at 03:00 on a Sunday for one person (`plan/13` E3 budgets 15–20 hours a week). A reply-time promise you cannot keep is a misleading claim under EU and UK consumer law. If it is not certain, use "Message us any time; we reply the same day."

## The four hats
**Building.** (a) The TASK-172 ordering in fix 6. (b) Task 1 is large: about 20 acceptance criteria, a new module, a gate moved between files and SEO registrations. Expect two review rounds. (c) Before `/plan-tasks`, the design round has to redraw four sets of drawings (§12, `CLAUDE.md`). That is the slowest step this week, not the code.

**Google.** No concern. Nothing is indexed until the indexing switch is turned on. Merging the thin pages (§2) and keeping legal drafts out of search but crawlable until a lawyer signs them (§6) are both right. The delivery page is the thinnest: seven rows that all say "not delivering yet". It can stay as it is until go-live.

**Law and compliance.** (a) **Right of withdrawal.** The exemption for perishable goods is correctly cited: EU consumer rights directive Art. 16(d), and UK Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013 reg. 28(1)(c) ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2013/3134/regulation/28)). Add-ons that do not perish stay open for the lawyer (§3). Free cancellation until the florist accepts is a voluntary promise, and it fits the contract only forming at acceptance. (b) **Imprint and trader identity.** Hiding the imprint for a few days while nothing can be bought is defensible. The 2019 Omnibus amendments mean the CRD Art. 6(1)(c) identity (address, phone, email) must be shown before any order, so checkout must stay shut until `registered` flips ([summary](https://www.mamotcv.com/insights/consumer-legislation-revamp-of-consumer-law-directives/)). (c) **You, a private person, as controller.** Naming you with a phone number satisfies Art. 13(1)(a) without an address. If you live outside the EU, Art. 27 may require an EU representative. The exemption for occasional, low-risk processing probably covers consent logs and server logs, but that is for the lawyer to decide [inferred] ([Art. 27](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-27-gdpr/)). A professional must sign off (a), (c) and the interim cookie-page notice.

**Customer.** (a) **The delivery photo.** A.3 promises it with no remedy, and the workflow does not exist yet (specs 016/018). On a page called "guarantee" that reads like a contract term. Either move it to How it works as a plan, or say it shows the bouquet, not the recipient (`plan/07` §1.2). (b) **Free cancellation.** If florists accept within minutes, the free window is close to nothing. The terms should say how the buyer learns that the order was accepted. Card holds also run out after about 7 days, and iDEAL and SEPA cannot be held at all ([Stripe](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/place-a-hold-on-a-payment-method)), so the "we refund it in full" branch will be common. (c) **Nobody home.** A free second attempt is a cost you pay the florist. Confirm you accept it.
