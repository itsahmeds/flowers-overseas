Why it costs more here
On a phone the DOM order is the visual order — there is no second column for the description to sit in. Four to six lines of authored prose between the name and the first price is four to six lines the thumb must pass, on top of the 245 px of chrome measured on every other page of the site.

The changes, argued
#Change, kind and argument
PM1Chrome 245 → 176, by the same three moves as every other page. Rearrangement + Removal See M1–M3. This is one change to one component and it is worth 69 px on every page of the site, which is why it is the first thing to do.
PM2The description moves below the delivery facts; the sizes and the price take its place. Rearrangement Same argument as P1, and stronger here because there is no second column: on a phone the buyer scrolls past four to six lines of prose to find out what the bouquet costs. The words are not cut and are not collapsed — they move, and they stay in the server-rendered HTML.
PM3The destination line moves below the photograph, next to the name. Rearrangement Same words. Above the photograph it is the first thing on the page and the least useful; beside the name it is read at the moment it matters.
PM4The photograph goes from 250 px to 280 px. Rearrangement Consequential. Bloom & Wild gives a product 327 px of width on a phone in a list; a product page should not give it less.
PM5The honesty label stays directly under the photograph. Kept It belongs to the image, and spec 006 §2.5 places it wherever an ai asset is shown. On the product page it is one line at this width, so the card-level wrapping problem in LM5 does not arise.
PM6The date block's unavailable wording, the demo sentence in place of a button, the vase exclusion and the sticky summary: unchanged. Kept Spec 009 §13 settled all four and this study found nothing against them.
