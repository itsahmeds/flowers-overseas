## 2. What we take

1. **The relay explained in one sentence, in the local language, high on the page.** Interflora PL: "bukiet trafia do lokalnej kwiaciarni w kraju odbiorcy, gdzie jest przygotowywany i doręczany" (interflora.pl/zagranica, 2026-09-09). This is our whole product; nobody says it as plainly except Interflora PL and Euroflorist PL.
2. **Kill the customs fear explicitly.** Euroflorist PL: "no documents or customs forms needed … the bouquet is not physically transported from Poland — a partner florist in the recipient's country prepares it" (euroflorist.pl/kwiaty-za-granice, 2026-09-09). Every corridor page needs this line, because "will it get through customs / will it arrive dead" is the buyer's real first fear.
3. **State the settlement currency plainly.** Euroflorist PL: prices "displayed and settled in Polish zloty (PLN), regardless of delivery address". Interflora PL offers a PLN/EUR/GBP switcher. Our version (`plan/04` §9): one total in the buyer's currency plus "You pay in GBP; the florist is paid in PLN".
4. **"No hidden fees" as a stated promise, not an implication.** Interflora PL: "nie pobieramy ukrytych opłat". We can say it and mean it, which none of the delivery-fee-adding competitors can.
5. **Named, specific cutoffs on the corridor page.** Interflora UK is the only one that puts real numbers in the country page copy: "Place your order before 2pm on weekdays or before 10am on Saturdays", plus "same-day cannot be guaranteed on Sundays or public holidays". Ours becomes a live countdown from destination data (`plan/04` §5).
6. **A country-specific address-format paragraph.** InternetFlorist's "What a complete Polish address needs" (2026-09-05) is the single most useful piece of country content in the whole field, and it maps straight onto our per-country address formats (`plan/03` §8).
7. **Country-specific practical rules: funerals, cemeteries, hospitals.** Interflora UK's Germany page names ward and room number for hospitals and cemetery/crematorium handling for funerals. High-intent, high-anxiety, and unique per corridor — exactly the content that makes our pages not thin.
8. **Local-holiday and local-occasion content per destination.** GiftBasketsOverseas' local-holiday guide and Interflora FR's "florists do not deliver on Sundays or certain public holidays … 3 October" are the pattern for our "Coming up in Poland" strip with order-by cutoffs.
9. **A real coverage number per corridor.** Fleurop states counts ("rund 5.000 … etwa 50.000 … 150 Ländern"); Interflora UK says "a large network" with no number and reads weaker for it. We show a count only where we have one (`plan/04` §3: "12 florists in Warsaw" is a count, not a claim).
10. **A full A–Z destination list plus a "popular from here" group.** Interflora PL and FloraQueen both do featured-then-A–Z; our homepage picker already groups "Popular from the UK" then A–Z. The corridor hub should mirror it as crawlable links.
11. **`FAQPage` + `BreadcrumbList` on every corridor page.** Interflora UK, Euroflorist and InternetFlorist ship it; FloraQueen (the biggest corridor-page operator) ships none, which is free ground for us. Add `ItemList` for the product block (InternetFlorist does).
12. **Delivery-photo proof as a corridor trust asset.** GBO's "Pictures of Gift Recipients" gallery is the only proof-of-delivery artefact in the field. Ours is per-country and real (`plan/04` §12), gated until ≥6 photos exist.

## 3. What we drop and why

1. **Corridor pages generated for countries we cannot serve.** InternetFlorist ships `florist-antarctica`; FloraQueen ships 12.6k city collections; GBO ~240k product×country and origin×destination pages. Our rule: a country page exists for live *or* honest guide status, and a city page only where coverage exists (`plan/05` rows 4–5, `plan/04` §6 waitlist empty state).
2. **The destination page showing domestic inventory in the sender's currency.** Euroflorist NO's Germany page lists Norwegian bouquets in NOK; Interflora FR lists English-named German products. Our corridor grid is destination inventory, priced in the buyer's currency.
3. **No indexable corridor URLs at all** (Fleurop's country search box; Euroflorist PL's dropdown hub with no per-country pages in the sitemap). This is the single largest ranking opportunity in the field and both leave it on the table.
4. **Template variables left pointing at the wrong country.** Fleurop's Poland corridor page reads "Empfänger in Frankreich"; FloraQueen's Berlin page carries "Inspired by Barcelona, made for you". Our corridor content is data-driven with a per-country review gate before publish.
5. **Handing the buyer to a sister site.** Euroflorist NO's Ireland page: "klikk her for å gå direkte til Euroflorists irske nettside". We own the corridor end to end.
6. **Display-only multi-currency with foreign settlement.** GBO shows ~120 currencies and settles in USD with a "rate may differ" disclaimer. We charge in the currency we display.
7. **Server-rendering nothing.** 1-800-Flowers' `/international/poland-12111` serves an empty `<title>` and nav-only HTML. Breaks priority #1.
8. **Unverifiable network claims and inconsistent ones.** "195 counties [sic] worldwide" on one 1-800-Flowers page vs "over 100 countries" on another; "over 30,000 florists" (Interflora UK) with no basis shown. We publish counts from our own data or nothing.
9. **A delivery-pass upsell competing with the corridor CTA.** Interflora UK's £22 platinum pass and Interflora FR's €24,95/an sit on the country page. One decision per screen.
10. **Origin×destination "corridor" pages as an SEO tactic.** GBO's `gifts-from-bermuda-to-guyana.htm` (≥30k URLs) is a pure doorway pattern. Our locale already encodes the origin; we do not multiply it into URLs.
11. **A vague "how it works" that never says who makes the bouquet.** 1-800-Flowers says "international affiliates"; FloraQueen says "bouquet creation centres". We name the model: a vetted local florist in the recipient's city.

## 4. Open questions for the founder

1. **Guide-status country pages.** `plan/05` row 4 allows indexing a country page as a "guide" before we are live there. What exactly does that page promise, and does it carry a waitlist form instead of a grid (`plan/04` §6)?
2. **How many destinations at launch?** Interflora PL publishes 135, Euroflorist PL 18, Interflora UK 143. Do we publish only live corridors (few, strong) or live + guide (many, thinner but crawlable)?
3. **City pages.** InternetFlorist ships ~70 Polish city pages; the field's evidence is that they rank and that they are the main doorway risk. Do city pages wait for real per-city florist coverage (current `plan/05` position), even if that costs Phase-1 traffic?
4. **Currency switcher vs locale-fixed currency.** Interflora PL offers PLN/EUR/GBP on the same pages; our plan fixes currency in a cookie per locale. Does the corridor page expose a switcher, and if so how do we keep "price shown = price charged" and the schema price consistent?
5. **Do we publish the address-format guidance per country as page content, or only as inline checkout help?** As content it is a ranking asset; inline it is a conversion asset. Both costs translation per locale × destination.
6. **Funeral and hospital delivery.** These are the highest-intent corridor queries and the highest operational risk (timed delivery, ward access). Are they in scope for Phase 1 Poland, and can partners commit to timed funeral delivery?
