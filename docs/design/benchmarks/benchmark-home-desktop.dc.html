The changes, argued
#ChangeKindThe argument
H1The header's ten category items become anchors instead of <span>, for the seven that have a live destination; the three with nowhere to go keep the muted treatment and say so.RearrangementMeasured: our home page carries 19 anchors against FloraQueen's 319, Interflora's 249 and Bloom & Wild's 159, and not one anchor on it reaches /en/poland/flowers, a live page holding 84 priced products. Fourteen of the fifteen visible header affordances are inert, which costs us every internal link plan/02 §11 expects the home page to pass and is the most plausible cause of "it looks unfinished": a thing that looks clickable and is not reads as broken.
H2The three dead account affordances and the dead search field keep their position but take the muted ink the system already has, and search gains the words "Opens with the shop".RearrangementThey cannot become links — there is no account and no search — so the only honest options are to remove them or to say what they are. Saying it costs nothing, keeps the layout stable for the day they do work, and turns four apparent faults into four statements of fact. The screen-reader-only sentence already there ("Search opens when the shop does") becomes visible to everyone.
H3The hero photograph goes from 820 px to 500 px, and the finder's delivery-dates line moves onto the button's row.RearrangementNo word and no control is lost; 320 px of photograph is. It buys the priced row in H4. Against the field our hero is the largest at 820, where Euroflorist runs 600, Interflora 452 and FloraQueen 400, so 500 is still generous and is still the biggest single element on the page.
H4A six-card row of priced Poland products, scoped to the one live destination, replaces the unpriced row currently at 1112.RearrangementThe row already exists and already names these products. What it lacks is the price, and the price exists: /en/poland/flowers publishes 229.00 PLN for Amber Hour one click away. CLAUDE.md's density rule already requires priced rows under the hero and forbids a card without a price; the shipped page keeps the row and drops the price, which is the worst of both. Scoping it to Poland is what makes the price sayable: a destination-less card genuinely may not carry one, and that rule is preserved by attaching the row to the live destination rather than by leaving it blank. This one needs a spec decision, not just a drawing — 008 owns what a home row may claim.
H5The heading "Trending now / Most sent this week" is deleted and replaced with "What we can make for Poland today", plus the ordering sentence the listing page already uses.RemovalA stronger argument, as the brief requires. We have taken no orders, so nothing has been "most sent this week": the claim is unevidenced. It is also self-contradicting on our own site — the disclaimer directly beneath it says the ranking "switches on once we have" real orders, and /en/poland/flowers, one click away, says in as many words "It is not a ranking by sales, by popularity or by payment." Spec 008 §8 / AC-9 already deleted exactly this language from the navigation ("Best sellers" → "Our selection") on the ground that Omnibus Art. 6a makes an unevidenced popularity claim an unfair commercial practice when an honest alternative is free. The row, its five products and the disclaimer all stay; only the claim goes.
H6Chrome height, the utility strip, the four language links and the currency chip: unchanged on desktop.Kept183 px is mid-field (128 / 143 / 174 / 183 / 200). There is nothing here to win, and the earlier report of 377 px / 42% that prompted this exercise was simply wrong.
