Competitor benchmark · the measurements · [internal]
Purpose
The founder said the site looks worse than the competition and called it cluttered. This artboard is the measurement that tests that sentence, page type by page type, so the redesign argues from numbers rather than from taste. It is a decision artefact, not a build target: when a change on the three page-type artboards beside this one is approved, the owning page wireframe under wireframes/ is redrawn and that is what an implementer builds from.
Method
Every figure was read from the live DOM with getBoundingClientRect() at an emulated 1280×900 and 375×812 on 2026-09-21. Nothing here was estimated from a screenshot. "Chrome" is the height of the stacked bars above the first page content; "preamble" is the distance from the bottom of that chrome to the top of the first product photograph.
Read against
the 2026-09-09 study in this folder, which measured content patterns. This pass measures space, which that study did not, and does not restate its findings.
Who we measured
FloraQueen (the one competitor with our exact model: one brand, many languages, delivery by a florist in the recipient's country), Interflora UK (the country-page and same-name-many-countries incumbent), Euroflorist NL (the European site-per-country model), Bloom & Wild (not cross-border, and included on purpose: it is the visual standard a European buyer's eye is calibrated by, and it is the only one of the four a designer would call well made).
What we could not read
Nothing was blocked. FloraQueen, Interflora UK and Euroflorist NL show a consent sheet that offers no plain refusal, so none was accepted and none was dismissed; each sits at the bottom or the centre of the window and changes no figure above. Bloom & Wild offers "Reject all" and it was taken.
Owning spec
none. This is research for 004, 008 and 009, and for the founder's decision before any of them is amended.

The single largest gap in this study is the last column. Our locale home carries 19 anchors against 319, 249 and 159. Seven of ours are destination links, one is the brand, one a telephone number, three are language links. Not one of them points at /en/poland/flowers, which exists, is live and holds 84 priced products. The header's ten category items, its search field and its three account affordances are <span> elements: fourteen of the fifteen things that look clickable in our header do nothing, carry no link and pass no ranking weight. That is a plan/02 §11 finding before it is a design one, and it is the most likely single cause of "it looks worse" — a page reads as unfinished when the things on it do not respond.
The product page
Ours is not shipped, and is not measured here. src/app/[locale]/ routes [segment]/[child] and no deeper, so no product URL resolves; the twelve cards on the Poland listing are deliberately not links, because a card pointing at a 404 would be worse than a card that waits. The page type is therefore benchmarked against wireframes/product-desktop.dc.html, which is the only artefact that exists, and the comparison is of block order, not of pixels. FloraQueen, measured. Chrome ends at 118. Gallery and <h1> at 138. The size selector, with an all-in price printed on each option, at 226–322. Add-ons with their prices at 440. Add to cart at 620. Every decision the buyer makes is inside the first 900 px, and the 60–90 words of description sit behind two accordion buttons at 701 and 826. Ours, as drawn. The DOM order on the artboard is breadcrumb → destination line → gallery → <h1> → description and care → tier selector → date picker → delivery facts → add-on prices → price summary. The 60–90 authored words sit between the name and the first price. That is the one ordering difference worth arguing about, and it is argued on benchmark-product-desktop.dc.html.
What this study does not propose to change
Said plainly, because the brief asked for it. Desktop chrome height. 183 px is mid-field. Nothing to win. The desktop product card. 70.1% photograph is inside the field's range and above Bloom & Wild's. The breadcrumb. It costs 20 px plus its gap and none of the four comparators prints one on a listing page. It stays: it is the BreadcrumbList source and ranking is priority 1. The 820 px home hero. Every one of the four leads with one large photograph and none of them puts a priced product above the fold on a home page. Ours is the largest at 820 against 600, 452 and 400, and it is the one page where the photography is real. It is trimmed on the home artboard to buy the finder's button, not because it is wrong. The 72 captioned placeholders. A known state, not a defect, and no proposal here designs around them.
