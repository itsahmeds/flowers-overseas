Product page · current and proposed · desktop · [internal]
Purpose
Compare the block order of our product page against the only competitor that puts the whole buying decision inside one screen. A decision artefact: approval sends the change to wireframes/product-desktop.dc.html, which is what an implementer builds from.
The honest caveat
Our product page is not shipped and could not be measured. src/app/[locale]/ routes [segment]/[child] and no deeper, so no product URL resolves, and the twelve cards on the Poland listing are deliberately not links — a card pointing at a 404 would be worse than a card that waits. "Current" below is therefore wireframes/product-desktop.dc.html redrawn at 1280 in its unavailable state, which is the state every destination is in today, and the comparison is of order, not of pixels we have measured.
What was measured
FloraQueen's product page at 1280×900 on 2026-09-21: chrome 118; gallery and <h1> at 138; size selector with an all-in price on each option 226–322; add-ons with their prices at 440; Add to cart at 620; the description behind two accordion buttons at 701 and 826.
Honesty
Nothing below removes a line. The demo sentence that stands where a button would be, the all-in price wording, the "nothing is added at a later step" line, the substitution promise, the delivery-facts block in its unavailable state and the "Example arrangement" label all stay exactly as spec 009 draws them.
Owning spec
009. Nothing here reopens 009 §13 Q4 (no countdown) or Q6 (no button, no add-on checkbox).

The changes, argued
#ChangeKindThe argument
P1The 60–90-word description and care block moves from between the <h1> and the tier selector to below the delivery facts.RearrangementMeasured: FloraQueen reaches its priced size options at 226 and its buy control at 620, with the description behind two accordions at 701 and 826. We put four to six lines of authored prose between the buyer reading the name and the buyer seeing what it costs. The block keeps every word and stays in the server-rendered HTML in full, so nothing is hidden from a crawler or a screen reader — it is a position change, not a disclosure. It is also moved rather than collapsed on purpose: a <details> would be the field's answer and it would shift the page when it opened, and the brief holds CLS at zero.
P2The price summary moves up to sit directly under the tier selector, ahead of the date picker and the add-on prices.RearrangementThe tiers already print an all-in price each; the summary is the number the buyer is actually asked to accept, and today it is the tenth block. Putting it next to the sizes is what makes the all-in promise legible as one number rather than three — and "price shown = price charged" is a project rule, so the page should show the price where the buyer decides.
P3The destination line moves from above the gallery into the right column, above the <h1>.RearrangementSame words, same link. It is the first thing a cross-border buyer needs to confirm, and beside the name it is read; above a 400 px photograph on the far side of the page it is furniture. Costs nothing and buys 26 px for the photograph.
P4The gallery goes from 400 px to 440 px tall.RearrangementConsequential, not independent: P1 and P3 free the space, and the one thing every competitor spends its product page on is the photograph. It stays a fixed ratio so the placeholder-to-photograph swap still costs zero layout shift.
P5The date block, its unavailable wording, the demo sentence in place of a button, and the absence of any countdown: unchanged.KeptSpec 009 §13 Q4 and Q6 settled these and this study found no evidence to reopen either. FloraQueen's date-and-fee chips remain the best single element in the field and remain unreachable for us until a destination has a cutoff.
P6The page is drawn, not built.NotedRecorded so the founder is not asked to approve a comparison that was not made: our numbers on this page type are from a drawing and FloraQueen's are from a live DOM. When the page ships it should be measured again against the same four sites.
