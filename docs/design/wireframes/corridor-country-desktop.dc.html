Sending flowers to Poland

Guide · not delivering yet

Poland is where we are opening first, and this is what we have learned while we choose the florists. Flowers are ordinary there in a way they are not in Britain: a single tulip on 8 March, a bunch for a name day, chrysanthemums that belong only to graves and nowhere else. The rules are specific, they are easy to get wrong from outside, and getting them wrong is what makes a bouquet land badly. We have written them down here — what is sent, what is not, how an address is put together, what the calendar looks like across a Polish year — so that you can send something right whether or not you send it through us. When we do open, the delivery details will appear at the top of this page and nothing else on it will change.

The authored intro, 120–200 words, human-written and never machine-drafted. The <h1> reads “Sending flowers to Poland” rather than “Send flowers to Poland”: the imperative is a call to an action the page cannot yet take.

Flowers in Poland

What is sent, and what is not

Sent mostTulips, which Poland grows and sells by the armful from February; roses; gerbera; carnations, which carry none of the cheapness they are read with in Britain; peonies in June; potted orchids as a lasting present.
Sent rarely, or neverChrysanthemums, which belong to graves. Even numbers of stems, which belong to funerals. Lilies into a house with a cat. Yellow, which some older recipients still read as jealousy.
The one to knowA single stem is a complete gift in Poland on 8 March. It is not a small gesture there; it is the gesture.

Questions about Poland

Twelve, and they are Poland's own

Do you deliver in Poland yet?

Not yet. We are choosing the florists now, and we would rather say that than take an order we cannot keep. This page is the guide we wrote while we do it; the day a florist in Poland starts making our bouquets, the delivery details appear at the top of it.

What do Poles send flowers for?

Name days — imieniny — as much as birthdays. Every date in the Polish calendar carries names, and for an older recipient the name day is the one that is celebrated. Women's Day on 8 March is the single biggest flower day of the Polish year after All Saints, and Grandmother's and Grandfather's Day fall on 21 and 22 January.

How many flowers should I send?

An odd number. Even-numbered bunches are for funerals and for graves in Poland, so a bouquet of ten says something nobody means to say. The exception is a single flower, which is the ordinary gift on Women's Day.

Which flowers should I avoid?

Chrysanthemums. In Poland they belong on graves, and they are what the whole country carries to the cemetery on 1 November; sending them to someone's home reads as a funeral tribute. Red roses are read as a declaration, which is fine if you mean it. Lilies are fine except for a household with a cat.

Why is 1 November such a large day?

Wszystkich Świętych — All Saints' Day. Families travel across the country to clean and light graves, and florists in every Polish town sell more in that week than in any other. It is a public holiday, and flowers ordered for it are ordered days ahead.

How is a Polish address written?

Street and building number, then the flat number after a slash: Marszałkowska 12/34. The postcode has five digits and a dash, 00-590, and it goes before the city. We ask for the address in that order, in that format, when we take an order for Poland.

Will Polish characters survive on the card?

Yes. ą, ć, ę, ł, ń, ó, ś, ź and ż reach the florist's screen and the handwritten card exactly as you typed them. You can write the whole message in Polish; you do not have to.

What currency will I pay in?

Yours. Poland uses the złoty and we settle with our florists in złoty, but the price you are shown and charged is in the currency of the page you are reading, with nothing added afterwards.

Will the recipient have to pay anything when it arrives?

No. Nothing is posted and nothing crosses a border: the flowers are bought in Poland and delivered in Poland, so there is no customs form, no import duty and nothing to pay at the door.

Can flowers be sent to a Polish hospital?

Usually to reception rather than to the ward, and never to intensive care. Give the ward and the room and the patient's full name as the hospital has it; Polish hospitals keep flowers at the desk and the ward collects them.

What about a funeral?

A wreath or a spray, in even numbers, with the family name on the ribbon, and the chapel or cemetery and the hour of the service. This is the one occasion where the even-number rule runs the other way.

Can I read this page in Polish?

Not yet. We publish a country guide only in a language a person has actually written and another person has checked, so the Polish guide appears when our Polish reviewer has finished it rather than when a machine has finished it. Until then this page exists in English only, and it says so rather than serving you a translation.
