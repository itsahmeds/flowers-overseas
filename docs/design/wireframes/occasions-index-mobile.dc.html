Occasions

Some of these fall on a date and the date is different in every country. Some of them are any day of the year. Each one has a page with what we make for it, and the dated ones carry the date for every destination we cover.

And one more with no date of its own: Name day — imieniny. Every day of the Polish calendar carries names, and for many people the name day matters more than the birthday. Its rule is none, so it is named here and never printed with a guessed date.

Every date is nextOccasions() over the destination's own rules, from 16 September 2026, formatted by formatDate. Both groups are in collator(locale) order rather than date order, which is what the spec fixes — a Polish reader gets a different sequence from an English one, and neither is sorted by how soon the day falls. The caption says in Poland out loud, because an index with no destination would otherwise be quoting one country's calendar as if it were everyone's — each hub carries the full per-country table, and this page carries the soonest date in the destination the dates are computed in.

States
Observed, on another country's dateSant Jordi is observed in Spain, Grandmothers' Day and the May Day lily of the valley in France. Spain and France are published, so all three have hubs, but none of their days is on Poland's calendar. They are listed, linked, under “Kept on a date we cannot compute here” with Name day, and the note says why: “the day belongs to another country, or the rule names no day at all”. They never get a row with a blank date. (Withdrawn: “observed nowhere we have published”, which was true only while Poland was the one published destination.)
Observed, no dateThe name-day sentence above. An occasion whose rule is none never appears in the dated table.
